1. "Saisons der Oase" (kicker above the title): Stumbled slightly — "Saisons" as a bare plural of a French/English loanword feels imported rather than something a German copywriter would reach for here, and it's not clear whether it means "the seasons of the year" or "the recurring festival periods." A more natural kicker would be something like "Feste der Oase" or "Feste und Feiern der Oase" (if festivals are meant) or "Jahreszeiten der Oase" (if the calendar year is meant).

2. "Vom Qaisariyah-Souk bis zu den Dattelpalmenfarmen pulsiert Al-Ahsa mit Saisons, die sein Erbe das ganze Jahr über beleben.": Had to read this twice. "pulsiert mit Saisons" is not a collocation a German writer would produce — "pulsieren" normally takes life, energy, colour, or activity as its object, not "Saisons" (seasons/editions of a festival). I couldn't tell if "Saisons" here means "festivals" or "times of year," which made the sentence's claim fuzzy on first pass. Something like "...ist Al-Ahsa das ganze Jahr über von Festen erfüllt, die sein Erbe lebendig halten" would read more naturally.

3. "Feste und Festivals kehren jedes Jahr in ihrer Saison wieder.": This stopped me — "Feste" and "Festivals" are two words for essentially the same thing placed side by side, and I paused wondering whether a distinction was intended (e.g. traditional folk festivities vs. organised festivals) that the text never explains. Picking one term, e.g. "Die Festivals kehren jedes Jahr zur gleichen Zeit wieder," would remove the doubt.

4. "Die Karten werden automatisch nach ihrer zeitlichen Nähe zum Datum Ihres Geräts sortiert – beginnend mit dem, was gerade läuft.": The first half is stiff and over-literal — "das Datum Ihres Geräts" reads like a direct translation of "your device's date"; no German speaker talks about a device "having" a date. It also creates a register clash within one sentence: bureaucratic ("zeitliche Nähe zum Datum Ihres Geräts") followed by casual ("was gerade läuft"). Suggest: "Die Karten werden automatisch nach ihrer Nähe zum heutigen Datum sortiert – beginnend mit dem, was gerade läuft."

5. "Erwartete Saison" (badge shown on a card's photo): Unclear standing alone — expected by whom, and expected to happen at all, or just expected around that time of year? Without the surrounding explanatory paragraph next to it, the badge alone doesn't tell me what "expected" is promising versus the "Dieses Jahr bestätigt" badge it's presumably contrasted with. A clearer standalone label might be "Voraussichtlicher Zeitraum."

6. Table heading "Saisonaler Kalender der Veranstaltungen – ungefähre Angaben, jährlich wiederkehrend": Reads like a string of catalogue keywords tacked on after a dash rather than a phrase a person would say — noticeably stiffer/more bureaucratic in register than the surrounding page copy.

7. Column headers "Termine | Ort | Zeitraum" against the first row "Processed Dates Festival | Al-Ahsa Municipality Fort, Hofuf | Winter · January – February": On first pass I expected a date under "Termine" ("dates/appointments") and instead found the event's name there, while the actual season/months sit under "Zeitraum" instead. The German header doesn't match what's actually listed in that column — "Termine" should probably be "Veranstaltung" or "Name," with the date-like information staying under "Zeitraum."

8. "Die Veranstaltungen richten sich sowohl nach dem islamischen Mondkalender (Hidschra-Kalender) als auch nach dem gregorianischen Kalender und kehren jährlich in ihren ungefähren Saisons wieder.": This needed a second read and still left me unsure. If events follow the Hijri lunar calendar, their Gregorian dates shift by roughly 11 days every year, so such events could not also "return annually in their approximate seasons" in the Gregorian sense implied earlier on the page. The sentence doesn't say which events follow which calendar, so as a reader I can't tell what to actually expect for any given festival's timing. That distinction is missing, not just unclearly worded.

9. "Die Programme sind auf die ganze Familie ausgerichtet und bieten Unterhaltung, Kreativität, Einkaufsmöglichkeiten und Kostproben für alle Altersgruppen.": "Kreativität" sitting between "Unterhaltung" and "Einkaufsmöglichkeiten" stood out as abstract next to three very concrete offerings (entertainment, shopping, tastings) — I paused trying to picture what "offering creativity" actually looks like on site. Something like "kreative Mitmachaktionen" would fit the list better.

10. "Für weitere Informationen und Aktualisierungen folgen Sie der Kommunalverwaltung von Al-Ahsa, der Al-Ahsa-Entwicklungsbehörde und den offiziellen Kanälen der Veranstaltungen.": "folgen Sie der Kommunalverwaltung" reads oddly — "folgen" (as in following an account) applied directly to a municipal administration as an institution, rather than to its channels, made me reread to figure out whether I'm meant to follow the two named authorities themselves or, as the last list item implies, only their "channels." The list mixes institutions and "channels" as if they were the same kind of object. Suggest something like "folgen Sie den Kanälen der Kommunalverwaltung von Al-Ahsa, der Al-Ahsa-Entwicklungsbehörde und der jeweiligen Veranstaltung."

11. Banner heading "Machen Sie aus Ihrer Saison eine ganze Reise": "Ihrer Saison" ("your season") stopped me — I don't have a personal "season," so I reread trying to work out whether this means "your visit," "your festival trip," or literally a season of the calendar year. "Machen Sie aus Ihrem Festivalbesuch eine ganze Reise" would remove the ambiguity.
