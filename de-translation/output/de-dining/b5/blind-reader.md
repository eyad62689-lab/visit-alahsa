1. "Der Freisitz des Aknan Café vor den Felsen des Qarah-Bergs bei Sonnenuntergang" — "Freisitz" is correct German, but it is the word city ordinances use for a licensed outdoor-seating permit ("Freisitzgenehmigung"), not the word a travel writer reaches for. Next to the poetic "vor den Felsen ... bei Sonnenuntergang" it reads oddly bureaucratic — a register clash. "Terrasse" or "Sitzbereich im Freien" would sit better with the rest of the sentence.

2. "Al-Qarah – am Fuß des Qarah-Bergs (Jabal al-Qarah)" — I stopped to check whether "Jabal al-Qarah" was a second, different place from "Qarah-Berg" just named. It is not; the parenthesis just repeats "Berg" (Jabal = mountain) right after "Bergs" was already used. First pass reads as redundant, not as a gloss.

3. "Ein hoher, weißer Raum mit raumhohen Fenstern rund um eine Barinsel aus Marmor und eine freitragende Holztreppe." — This genuinely confused me: "Fenster rund um eine Barinsel und eine Treppe" reads as floor-to-ceiling windows encircling the bar island and the staircase, which makes no spatial sense (windows sit in walls, not around freestanding furniture in the middle of a room). I think the intent was three separate features of the room, not windows wrapped around the other two. Something like "Ein hoher, weißer Raum mit raumhohen Fenstern, einer Barinsel aus Marmor und einer freitragenden Holztreppe" would remove the false attachment.

4. "ihrer Barinsel aus Marmor" — "Barinsel" (bar island) reads as a literal transplant of an English/kitchen-design term; German doesn't have an established use of "Insel" for a bar counter the way it does for a "Kücheninsel". First pass parses it, but it sounds translated rather than written. "freistehende Bartheke aus Marmor" would sound like something a German writer chose.

5. "Eine cremefarbene Fassade mit dem Namen in dunklen Buchstaben darüber, dazu Fenster in Holzrahmen und Tische auf dem Pflaster." — "darüber" left me unsure what it's above. The façade was just named, but letters can't hang above a façade — they'd be on it, or above the entrance. Since no door or entrance was mentioned, "darüber" has no clear anchor. Dropping it ("mit dem Namen in dunklen Buchstaben") or anchoring it ("über dem Eingang") would fix it.

6. "Direkt aus dem Tanur" (section kicker) — this is the very first appearance of "Tanur" in the section, and it's used with no gloss at all; the explanation "(Lehmofen)" only comes three lines later, in a different sentence. Until then the word is just an unexplained foreign term sitting in a heading.

7. "## Bäckereien für rotes Brot" — "Bäckereien für rotes Brot" is grammatical but not how a German would title this; "für" here suggests a bakery built for the purpose of red bread, an odd frame for a heading. "Bäckereien mit rotem Brot" or "Rotbrot-Bäckereien" would read as native phrasing.

8. "Dattelbrot und rotes Brot sind ein und dasselbe – zwei Namen für ein Gericht." — calling bread ein "Gericht" (a dish/course) struck me as off; in German "Gericht" implies a prepared meal, not a baked good. I had to reread to confirm they still meant bread. "zwei Namen für ein und dasselbe Backwerk" or simply "... – zwei Namen für dasselbe Brot" would avoid the mismatch.

9. "Das sind die Bäckereien, in denen dafür der Tanur (Lehmofen) angefeuert wird." — "dafür" sitting right before the subject "der Tanur" made me pause; I expected it to land next to the verb at the end of the clause. "in denen der Tanur (Lehmofen) dafür angefeuert wird" reads more smoothly.

10. "Ein Bäcker der Abu Fahd Al-Rabee Bakery hebt einen Laib an, mit einem im Boden eingelassenen Tanur und ringsum ausgebreiteten Broten." — on first pass "hebt einen Laib an, mit einem ... Tanur" reads as if he lifts the loaf using the oven (instrumental "mit"). Only on a second pass did I realize "mit" is just tacking on two more things visible in the photo. It reads like an English-style caption list stitched onto a German sentence. Splitting it would help: "Ein Bäcker ... hebt einen Laib an; im Boden ist ein Tanur eingelassen, ringsum liegen die Brote ausgebreitet."

11. "Al-Khudud-Viertel – nahe Ain Al-Khudud (der Khudud-Quelle)" — "Khudud" appears three times in one short line (district, spring name, and then glossed again as "Khudud-Quelle"). It's not wrong, just dense enough that I re-read the line to make sure the spring wasn't a separate thing from the district.

12. "Ein Bäcker sitzt an der Öffnung eines im Boden eingelassenen Tanurs, die Brote um ihn herum auf den Fliesen ausgebreitet." — the second half has no verb ("die Brote ... ausgebreitet" without "liegen" or "sind"). I kept expecting the sentence to continue. This absolute-participle construction is normal in English captions but not idiomatic German; "..., während die Brote um ihn herum auf den Fliesen ausgebreitet liegen" would read as something a German writer produced.

13. "Eine Lehmputzwand mit Zinnen und vorstehenden Balken sowie einer zweiflügeligen Holztür unter den Palmen." — "Zinnen" means battlements/crenellations, the word for a castle wall. For a bakery façade it stopped me for a moment — I pictured a fortress before realizing it describes the stepped mud-brick parapet. Not wrong (it's the standard term for this Najdi architectural feature), but it is the one word in the passage that first reads as the wrong building type.
