1. "Für Kaffeeliebhaber" directly followed by the heading "## Ausgewählte Cafés" — two heading-like lines in a row with no connecting text. On first read I couldn't tell whether "Für Kaffeeliebhaber" was itself the page title or an incomplete intro sentence (it has no verb). It only clicked once I saw the second line was the real heading. Not wrong, just a stumble on entry into the section.

2. "Al-Rifaa-Nord-Viertel – Hofuf" (repeated identically for Baithana, 7st. Speciality Coffee, and Al-Sayed Café). The four-part hyphen chain "Al-Rifaa-Nord-Viertel" reads at first glance as one proper name ("Al-Rifaa-Nord-Viertel" as a fixed place name) rather than "Viertel" (district) being the generic noun tacked onto "Al-Rifaa Nord". I had to stop and mentally split it into "Al-Rifaa-Nord" + "Viertel". A form like "Stadtteil Al-Rifaa-Nord" or "Al-Rifaa Nord (Stadtteil)" would parse on the first pass.

3. "Ein lehmverputztes Haus mit Holztür in einer der alten Gassen Hofufs." — the bare genitive "Hofufs" (Hofuf + genitive -s) looks foreign and slightly archaic; German place names ending in a consonant more commonly avoid the genitive-s in running text. I first read "Gassen Hofufs" as a possible typo before parsing it as a genitive. "...in einer der alten Gassen von Hofuf" would have been easier to read.

4. "Ein mit Lichtern behangenes Haus im Baustil Al-Ahsas, im historischen Herzen von Hofuf." — two things stumble here. First, "Al-Ahsas" (another bare genitive-s on a foreign name) momentarily reads as a different, unfamiliar place name rather than "of Al-Ahsa". Second, the comma before "im historischen Herzen von Hofuf" left me unsure for a moment whether that phrase still describes the "Baustil" (as if the building style itself were "in the historic heart of Hofuf") or restarts to describe where the house stands. Something like "...im Baustil Al-Ahsas, das im historischen Herzen von Hofuf liegt" would remove the ambiguity.

5. Dar Huwaija: the photo alt text says "Sitzplätze von Dar Huwaija zwischen verputzten Bögen und Holzstühlen" (seating between plastered arches and wooden chairs), but the description right below says "Sitzplätze zwischen weiß verputzten Bögen und Regalen mit Kaffeegeräten" (seating between white-plastered arches and shelves with coffee equipment). These describe two different things next to the arches — wooden chairs vs. shelves of coffee equipment — so I can't tell what the photo actually shows. This is a real contradiction, not just a paraphrase.

6. Al-Sayed Café: "Ein traditionell eingerichteter Innenraum mit Holzdecke, der den Charakter der traditionellen Kaffeehäuser bewahrt." — "traditionell" and "traditionellen" appear twice in one short sentence, which reads as repetitive rather than deliberate. Rephrasing the second occurrence (e.g., "...der den Charakter alter arabischer Kaffeehäuser bewahrt") would avoid the echo.

7. Ratio Al-Koot: the photo alt text introduces the word "Madschlis" ("Ein traditioneller Madschlis mit Kissen und Teppichen...") with no explanation, and only the description underneath glosses it: "Ein arabischer Madschlis (Empfangsraum) mit Kissen und Teppichen...". A reader (or screen-reader user) hits the unexplained term first and only learns what it means one field later. The alt text itself is unclear in isolation.

8. Soulaf Coffee: the alt text says "...mit Olivenbaum und Lamellendecke aus Holz" (a wooden slatted ceiling), but the description says "...in dessen Mitte ein Olivenbaum unter einem Oberlicht steht" (a skylight). A slatted wooden ceiling and a skylight (which implies an opening to the sky) are two different features — I can't tell from the text which one is actually over the olive tree in the photo.

9. Baking Up: "Gebäck und knusprige Knabbereien – laut eigener Beschreibung in Google Maps – hinter einer fröhlichen Fassade mit Sitzplätzen im Freien." The inserted citation "laut eigener Beschreibung in Google Maps" sounds like a footnote or disclaimer dropped into the middle of a marketing sentence — it breaks the tone and made me stop mid-sentence to figure out what it was referring to. It reads as bureaucratic/citation-register wedged into brochure-register text.

10. Same sentence, ignoring the inserted citation: "Gebäck und knusprige Knabbereien [...] hinter einer fröhlichen Fassade mit Sitzplätzen im Freien" literally states that the pastries and snacks are "behind a cheerful façade" — as written, the façade seems to be hiding the pastries, not the café itself. On first read I pictured the food behind a wall, which is presumably not the intended image. Something like "Hinter einer fröhlichen Fassade mit Sitzplätzen im Freien warten Gebäck und knusprige Knabbereien" would put the café, not the food, behind the façade.

11. Raslan: the location line reads only "Al-Khaleej-Straße (Katar-Straße)" — no district or city is given, unlike every other card in this list (all others name "Hofuf" or "Al-Mubarraz"). I finished the card not knowing which town Raslan is even in.

12. Same location line: "(Katar-Straße)" is given as an alternate name with no explanation of why the street has two names or which one is the "real" one — the parenthesis raises a question the text never answers. It also switches conventions mid-line: "Al-Khaleej" is left transliterated, while "Katar" is the fully German exonym for Qatar — the two halves of the same location line are handled two different ways, which is noticeable once you see it.

13. Raslan: the alt text says "Karak-Tassen und Frühstücksgerichte" (breakfast dishes), but the description says "...dazu einfache Frühstückshäppchen" (simple breakfast bites/snacks). "Gerichte" implies proper dishes, "Häppchen" implies small snacks — these suggest a different scale of what's being served, and I can't tell which is accurate.

14. Raslan: as with "Madschlis" above, "Karak" appears unglossed in the alt text ("Karak-Tassen...") before its explanation turns up in the description ("Karak (gewürzter Milchtee)..."). Anyone encountering only the alt text (e.g. a screen-reader user) meets the term before its meaning.

15. Across the set, the location line alternates between "Straße – Viertel" order (Dar Huwaija: "König-Fahd-Straße – Historisches Zentrum von Hofuf"; Ratio Al-Koot: "König-Abdulaziz-Straße – Historisches Zentrum von Hofuf") and "Viertel – Straße" order (Soulaf Coffee: "Al-Rawdah – König-Abdullah-Straße"). Since the dash gives no label for either side, I had to re-parse which part was the street and which was the district each time the order flipped.
