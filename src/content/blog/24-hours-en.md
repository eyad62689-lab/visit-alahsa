In Al-Ahsa for a single day? This itinerary distils the heart of the oasis — the largest palm oasis on earth, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2018 — into one day: a fortress and a heritage district, a cave-riddled mountain, and a souq more than two centuries old.

The stops are arranged by the **rhythm of the day**, not by fixed hours, because opening times and fees change with the seasons — the verified details live on each landmark's page, and the final word belongs to official channels.

## Morning: Qasr Ibrahim and the Al-Koot District

Start at [Qasr Ibrahim](/en/attractions/qasr-ibrahim/) in central Hofuf — the fortress that gathers mosque, garrison and tower beneath one wall; morning light is the best time for its architecture and inner courtyard. Close by lies the historic Al-Koot district, home to [Bayt Al-Bay'ah](/en/attractions/baiah/) — the Al-Mulla house built by Al-Ahsa's judge in 1203 AH, a symbol of the state's founding.

## Midday: The Caves of Jabal Al-Qarah and Dougha Al-Gharash

Head east to [Jabal Al-Qarah](/en/attractions/jabal-al-qarah/), the geological wonder at the heart of the oasis. Midday is no obstacle here — its caves **stay cool in summer and warm in winter**, a refuge when the heat peaks. At the mountain's foot in Al-Qarah village sits [Dougha Al-Gharash](/en/attractions/duqat-algharash/), the pottery house handed down through the Al-Gharash family, one of the Kingdom's best-known homes of the craft.

For lunch, the city's options are gathered in the [Restaurants & Cafés](/en/restaurants-cafes/) guide.

## Evening: Qaisariyah Souq

Close at [Qaisariyah Souq](/en/attractions/qaisariyah/) — a heritage market over two centuries old, its passages fragrant with perfume and copper, its cloaks embroidered by local hands. Evening is its best hour, when the shops open their doors and the souq finds its pulse — a fitting end to a concentrated day.
