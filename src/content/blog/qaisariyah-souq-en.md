You enter through one of its gates, the passage narrows and the shops press close together: [bishts](/en/blog/al-ahsa-traditional-crafts/) on their hangers, open sacks of spices, a shopkeeper looking up at you from behind his goods. This is Qaisariyah Souq in the city of Hofuf in Al-Ahsa, and the Saudi Press Agency (SPA) has quoted the Governor of Al-Ahsa as saying that [its history spans more than 200 years](https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2504544).

But the souq you walk through today was built anew after a fire that consumed most of its shops in 2001. Here we tell the history of Qaisariyah Souq as the sources give it, each by name: when it was built, when it was renewed, when it burned and how it returned, where it stands on the World Heritage List, and what is sold in it. Then we ask about its name, a name carried by souqs from Cairo to Granada.

## Where Is Qaisariyah Souq?

[Qaisariyah Souq](/en/attractions/qaisariyah/) lies in [the Al-Rifaa district of Hofuf](https://www.spa.gov.sa/1880556), and SPA places it in the centre of the city, on the western side of the district. The agency states that the souq has [14 entrance gates and holds more than 422 shops](https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2238945).

## When Was Qaisariyah Souq Built?

The official answer comes with an explicit qualification. SPA says that the building of Qaisariyah Souq goes back "to 1238 AH/1822 CE, according to what some sources relate". The qualification is part of the information: the agency relates and does not assert, and we give the date with its qualification.

SPA also reports historians as saying that the souq was standing in the nineteenth century; their argument is that travellers mentioned it in their memoirs, and that documents point to the same.

## What Did Travellers Write About the Qaisariyah?

The oldest description of Qaisariyah Souq that we have found was written by the traveller W. G. Palgrave in his book about his journey of 1862–1863. In Hofuf he describes a vaulted market that he calls "Keysareeyah": [a long barrel-vaulted arcade with a portal at each end](https://archive.org/download/narrativeofyears02palg/narrativeofyears02palg_djvu.txt). He says its shops were given over to costly goods: weapons, cloth, embroidery, and ornaments of gold and silver. Around it, he wrote, were alleys roofed with palm leaves against the heat.

After Palgrave, S. M. Zwemer, in his book published in 1900, described [the "Kaisariyeh or bazaar of Hofhoof"](https://archive.org/download/arabiacradleofis00zwem/arabiacradleofis00zwem_djvu.txt) as well supplied with goods, and listed among them weapons, cloth, gold embroidery, dates, vegetables and dried fish.

## When Was Qaisariyah Souq Renewed?

The Qaisariyah was renewed [between 1917 and 1923](https://whc.unesco.org/document/168727), according to the evaluation by ICOMOS, the advisory body that assessed the nomination of Al-Ahsa Oasis for UNESCO. So the Qaisariyah that Palgrave described did not remain as it was.

In a book published in 1926, R. E. Cheesman described in Hofuf [a long new colonnade of white masonry arches](https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.498934/2015.498934.in-unknown_djvu.txt), with its shops in covered corridors; for him it is part of the "new bazaar". Cheesman does not call this colonnade "the Qaisariyah", so we report his description as it stands and add nothing to it.

## What Is the "Kabank" of the Qaisariyah Souq Shops?

Historians, as reported by SPA, mention a small detail worth looking for with your own eyes in Qaisariyah Souq. The shop doors are called "kabank" (كبنك), and each door is made of three pieces: two pieces on which the shopkeeper sits, and in which he places the "quffa", a basket made of palm fronds, to keep rice and coffee, and a third piece that is lifted upwards.

## The Qaisariyah Souq Fire of 2001 and Its Return

Al-Jazirah newspaper reported in its issue of Friday 19 October 2001 that [a fire broke out in the Qaisariyah on Thursday morning](https://www.al-jazirah.com/2001/20011019/ln31.htm) and consumed more than 80% of its shops. According to the newspaper, the fire began at about 5:40 on the southern side of the souq and then spread as far as the northern side.

In the same report, Al-Jazirah described the Qaisariyah that burned as built of mud, with roofs of palm trunks.

Then the souq returned. Saudipedia states that Qaisariyah Souq was [built anew](https://saudipedia.com/ar/سوق-القيصرية-التاريخي) "in its traditional architectural style", and that its design was preserved.

On 16 May 2017, SPA reported that the souq, which had been rebuilt and rehabilitated and is managed by Al-Ahsa Municipality, won [the Architectural Heritage Award (second place) from the Arab Towns Organization](https://www.spa.gov.sa/w404462?lang=ar&newsid=1630351) in its 13th cycle, at a ceremony held in Doha.

In February 2026, the "Layali Al-Qaisariyah 2026" festival opened in Qaisariyah Souq, organised by Al-Ahsa Municipality in cooperation with Al-Ahsa Governorate and Al-Ahsa Development Authority, and in partnership with the Heritage Commission.

## Is Qaisariyah Souq on the World Heritage List?

What is inscribed on the list is Al-Ahsa Oasis as a whole, not the souq alone. On 29 June 2018, [the inscription of Al-Ahsa Oasis was announced in Manama](https://www.spa.gov.sa/w712214) on the World Heritage List, the fifth Saudi site to join it. [The World Heritage Committee's decision](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/7129) inscribes "Al-Ahsa Oasis, an Evolving Cultural Landscape" and states that it consists of twelve component parts.

The souq is present in the decision by name: when it lists what the integrity of the site rests on, it mentions the traditional historic souqs and gives the Qaisariyah in Hofuf as its example. SPA counts it among the landmarks that qualified the oasis for the list, along with [Amiriah School](/en/attractions/ameeriah/), [Jawatha Mosque](/en/attractions/jawatha-mosque/), [Qasr Ibrahim](/en/attractions/qasr-ibrahim/) and [Bayt Al-Bay'ah](/en/attractions/baiah/).

And it was in Qaisariyah Souq itself that [the people of Al-Ahsa celebrated their oasis joining the list](https://www.spa.gov.sa/1782954).

## What Is Sold in Qaisariyah Souq?

Qaisariyah Souq is still selling. Saudipedia lists sadu weaving and copperware among its handicrafts, and states that it has an area for traditional dishes and Al-Ahsa dates, as well as herbalists' shops.

According to SPA, the souq is distinguished by what is sold and displayed in it: [Hasawi bishts and abayas](https://www.spa.gov.sa/2404283), pottery, ironwork, leather goods and footwear, together with gold, watches, perfumes, fabrics and clothing. If the bisht is what you are after, our article on [the traditional crafts of Al-Ahsa](/en/blog/al-ahsa-traditional-crafts/) tells you about it.

For those shopping for the kitchen, SPA mentions among the souq's foodstuffs [spices and seasonings, and red Hasawi rice](https://spa.gov.sa/N2276587), along with [dried lomi](/en/blog/hasawi-lomi-lime/), pomegranate molasses, saffron, cardamom and rose water.

And out of a shop in the Qaisariyah came a bookshop. Saudipedia relates that Abdullah bin Abdulrahman Al-Mulla had [a small shop in Qaisariyah Souq](https://saudipedia.com/مكتبة-التعاون-الثقافي) where he sold perfumes, incense, some books of prayers and supplications, notebooks and pens, and that in 1368 AH/1948 CE he founded Al-Taawun Al-Thaqafi Bookshop, which the encyclopedia describes as "one of the oldest commercial bookshops in the Eastern Province".

## What Does the Name "Qaisariyah" Mean?

The word "qaisariyah" is of Greek origin according to The Encyclopaedia of Islam, an abbreviation of a phrase meaning "the imperial market". The encyclopaedia defines the qaisariyah as a large group of public buildings laid out as arcades, with shops, workshops and warehouses.

The traveller Palgrave paused over the name when he described the Hofuf souq. He said it was a Latin word, and wrote that he could not tell how it had reached lands that seem to have had little contact with the Roman and Byzantine empires. So the word is Latin for Palgrave, and Greek in origin according to the encyclopaedia.

Nor did the name belong to Hofuf alone. According to the encyclopaedia, al-Maqrizi mentioned a large number of qaisariyahs in his description of Cairo. In Fez, the qaisariyah is the name for the central market for cloth, carpets and jewellery, shut off by gates and walls from the rest of the city. The word also crossed into Spanish: alcaicería is a loanword from it.

As for why the Hofuf souq in particular carries this name, we found no source that answers, and we leave the question open.

## The Qaisariyah of Granada and the Qaisariyah of Hofuf

Among the souqs that carried the name is the Qaisariyah of Granada, the Alcaicería. The body that oversees the Alhambra (Patronato de la Alhambra y Generalife) states that it was [the place where silk and luxury goods were sold](https://www.alhambra-patronato.es/alcaiceria-granada), and that it had more than 200 shops, according to what the chronicles relate. It was walled, and had nine gates that were closed at sunset. On 20 July 1843 a fire destroyed it, and its rebuilding began a year later.

Both souqs burned and were rebuilt. They share the name and the type of market, and we have not found a source that documents a direct historical link between them.

## Before You Visit Qaisariyah Souq

- **Opening hours change.** You will find them up to date on the [Qaisariyah Souq page](/en/attractions/qaisariyah/). Check them before you go, especially on Fridays, when the hours differ from the rest of the week.
- **Look for the "kabank".** Stand in front of a shop door and study its three pieces, and remember that two of them are the shopkeeper's seat.
- **Make it the close of your day.** The [one day in Al-Ahsa](/en/blog/one-day-in-al-ahsa/) itinerary places Qaisariyah Souq in the evening, and the [48 hours in Al-Ahsa](/en/blog/48-hours-in-al-ahsa/) itinerary ends its first day there.
- **If you have come to shop**, read our article on [what to buy in Al-Ahsa](/en/blog/al-ahsa-souvenirs-guide/) before your visit, and open the [map of landmarks](/en/map/) to see what lies near the souq.

## In Brief

- Qaisariyah Souq is in the Al-Rifaa district in the centre of Hofuf, Al-Ahsa; it has 14 gates and holds more than 422 shops.
- Its building goes back to 1238 AH/1822 CE "according to what some sources relate", as SPA puts it, and the oldest description of it that we found was written by Palgrave about his journey of 1862–1863.
- It was renewed between 1917 and 1923 according to the ICOMOS evaluation, burned in 2001, and was then built anew in its traditional style.
- It is one of the landmarks of Al-Ahsa Oasis, inscribed on the World Heritage List since 2018; what is inscribed is the oasis, not the souq alone.
- Its name is of Greek origin according to The Encyclopaedia of Islam, and souqs in Cairo, Fez and Granada have carried it.

A souq that burned, then returned, and is still selling.
