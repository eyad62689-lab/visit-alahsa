In Al-Ahsa, craft is not an exhibit behind glass — it is a set of living trades handed down through families generation after generation, so much so that the city's name is bound to its accession to the **UNESCO Creative Cities Network**. This is a tour of the oasis's foremost crafts, and where to watch them being made.

## The Hasawi Bisht: The Cloak of Occasions

The Hasawi bisht is the cloak of ceremony and standing, and what sets it apart is the **Hasawi weave**: precise hand embroidery in *zari* threads coated with gold or silver water. Al-Ahsa families have passed the trade down generation after generation, and [Qaisariyah Souq](/en/attractions/qaisariyah/) — trading for more than two centuries — is the classic place to see the cloaks and buy them from the people who make them.

## Pottery: Dougha Al-Gharash

At the foot of [Jabal Al-Qarah](/en/attractions/jabal-al-qarah/) in Al-Qarah village sits [Dougha Al-Gharash](/en/attractions/duqat-algharash/), a pottery house where vessels are still shaped from clay in the old manual way and fired in its kilns. The Al-Gharash family has handed the craft down through the generations — press coverage records that pottery has endured on this spot for more than six hundred years — and its craftsmen have shown their work at international exhibitions in France, the United States and Canada. Today the Dougha is a visitor stop where you watch the full sequence — kneading the clay, the wheel, the kiln — and buy from its wares.

## Palm-Frond Weaving

From the palm's fronds and leaves, the people of Al-Ahsa wove their households' needs across the centuries, and frond-work is still present among the local crafts in the shops of [Qaisariyah](/en/attractions/qaisariyah/) and the Craftsmen Souq — proof that in the oasis, the palm gives all of itself, from fruit to frond.

## Where It All Comes Together: The Craftsmen Souq

In the heart of historic Hofuf, the [Craftsmen Souq](/en/attractions/craftsmen-souq/) opened in March 2020 to support handicrafts and folk arts: spread over more than 12,000 square metres, it houses 112 artisans' shops, an open courtyard, a traditional coffee house and a tannour bakery. It also holds a **handicrafts academy** run by experienced craftspeople who pass their trades to new generations — the heart of Al-Ahsa's bond with the Creative Cities Network.
