# Reader observations — جبل القارہ (reader-06b)

Read as an Urdu-speaking reader (Pakistani, Gulf resident) planning a possible trip. Only `page.md` was read. Items follow page order.

1. **Category label** — «فطرت»
   As a standalone label above a mountain page it reads a little abstract: «فطرت» is as much "innate disposition" as "the natural world", and for a moment I did not know what kind of section I was in. It resolves as soon as the title arrives.
   Grade: `minor`. A wording that would have worked: «قدرتی مقامات» or simply «قدرت».

2. **One-line summary (also repeated on the home card)** — «نخلستان کے قلب میں وقت کے تراشے ہوئے غار، جو گرمیوں میں ٹھنڈے رہتے ہیں اور سردیوں میں گرم۔»
   Reads well and is the best line on the page. One small mismatch: here the caves are «گرم» in winter, but the main paragraph and the FAQ both soften it to «ہلکی سی گرمائش», and the FAQ says the temperature stays «معتدل». Three different strengths of the same claim.
   Grade: `minor`.

3. **Main paragraph, sentences 2–3** — «چٹانوں میں غار ہیں اور راستے بل کھاتے چلے گئے ہیں۔ جب گرمی عروج پر ہو تو ان میں خوشگوار ٹھنڈک رہتی ہے، اور عین سردیوں میں ہلکی سی گرمائش۔»
   Two things. (a) After the fine opening sentence, «چٹانوں میں غار ہیں» is flat, almost a schoolbook statement, and it is not clear whether the «راستے» are inside the caves or on the hillside. (b) «ان میں» in the next sentence attaches first to the nearest plural, «راستے»; I had to step back to see that the caves are meant.
   Grade: `minor`. A wording that would have worked for (b): «ان غاروں میں».

4. **Same sentence, and again in the FAQ** — «عین سردیوں میں ہلکی سی گرمائش»
   «عین» sits naturally with a point («عین دوپہر», «عین وقت پر»); with a whole season it sounds slightly off, as if translated from "in the dead of winter". Understood at once, but no Urdu writer's first choice. It appears twice, word for word.
   Grade: `minor`. A wording that would have worked: «کڑاکے کی سردی میں» or «سخت سردی میں».

5. **Main paragraph, sentence 4** — «اسی قدرتی خوبی کی وجہ سے الاحساء کے لوگ قدیم زمانے سے یہاں پناہ لیتے آئے ہیں۔»
   «پناہ لینا» on its own suggests refuge from danger; the sentence does not say refuge from what. I inferred "from the weather" from the sentence before, but it is left unsaid.
   Grade: `minor`.

6. **Main paragraph, sentence 5, first clause** — «پہاڑ کی تنگ راہداریوں سے گزریں تو چٹانیں کندھوں کو چھوتے چھوتے رہ جاتی ہیں»
   I read this twice. The "almost did" idiom («…تے …تے رہ جانا») is correct, but in the habitual present and with rocks as the subject it is unusual, and on first pass I was not sure whether the rocks do touch the shoulders or do not. On the second pass it is a pleasing image.
   Grade: `minor`.

7. **Same sentence, second clause** — «پھر اچانک راستہ اونچی چھت والے ہال میں جا نکلتا ہے، جہاں روشنی کی ایک باریک کرن چھن کر آتی ہے۔»
   «ہال» makes me picture a built hall (شادی ہال, a function room), not a chamber in rock; it jars with the natural-cave picture the paragraph has been building. Also «چھن کر آتی ہے» normally names what the light filters through or from; here it comes from nowhere in particular.
   Grade: `minor`.

8. **Main paragraph, sentence 6** — «چوٹی سے نخلستان یوں پھیلا دکھائی دیتا ہے جیسے کھجور کے درختوں کا سمندر ہو۔»
   The sentence itself is good Urdu. But the page has been entirely about the inside of the mountain, and suddenly I am on the summit. Can a visitor go up to the top? Is it part of the same visit and the same ticket? The page never says, and nothing in the information card mentions it. It also sits a little awkwardly with the first sentence, which told me «اس کا راز بلندی میں نہیں».
   Grade: `moderate`. (Information is missing; I cannot supply it.)

9. **Main paragraph, last sentence** — «یہی ایک منظر بتا دیتا ہے کہ الاحساء دنیا کا سب سے بڑا نخلستان کیوں بنا۔»
   The logic stumbles. A view can show me *that* the oasis is vast, or why it is *called* the largest; it cannot tell me *why it became* the largest. «کیوں بنا» promises a cause the view cannot give, so the sentence sounds like a brochure flourish. The superlative itself is stated flatly with nothing on the page to stand behind it.
   Grade: `moderate`. A wording that would have worked: «…کیوں کہلاتا ہے۔»

10. **Card, location row** — «علاقہ | الاحساء میں، ہفوف کے مشرق میں»
    (a) Under the label «علاقہ» I expect the name of an area; instead I get a two-part description with «میں … میں», which reads clumsily. (b) «ہفوف» is never introduced: the page does not say what it is, and I am not sure how to pronounce it. (c) "East of ہفوف" with no distance tells me nothing I can plan with. (d) «الاحساء میں» is odd next to the paragraph, where «الاحساء» is first something that *has* an oasis («الاحساء کے نخلستان») and then *is* the oasis («الاحساء دنیا کا سب سے بڑا نخلستان»); here it is a region that contains a place called ہفوف.
    Grade: `moderate`. (What ہفوف is, and how far, are missing.)

11. **Card, best-time row, and the last FAQ answer** — «بہترین وقت | دن میں۔» / «جبل القارہ کی سیر کے لیے بہترین وقت کون سا ہے؟ — دن کا وقت۔»
    This tells me almost nothing. The place is open from morning until late evening, so "by day" only rules out the last few hours, and the page never says why daytime is better. Under «بہترین وقت» I was expecting a season or part of the day with a reason. As a FAQ answer, «دن کا وقت۔» reads like a blank that was filled in mechanically, and it lowered my trust in the rest of the card a little. The card value also carries a full stop («دن میں۔») that no other row has.
    Grade: `moderate`. (Which part of the day, which season, and why are missing.)

12. **Card, hours row** — «روزانہ 8:00–22:00»
    Two small parsing costs. In a right-to-left line a dash between two clock times does not tell me which end comes first; common sense settles it, but I did pause. And «22:00» is not how we say a time; I convert it in my head. The FAQ version («8:00 سے 22:00 تک») is clearer because of «سے … تک», though still 24-hour.
    Grade: `minor`. A wording that would have worked: «روزانہ صبح 8 بجے سے رات 10 بجے تک».

13. **Card, hours row, bracket; repeated in the FAQ** — «(چھٹیوں میں زیادہ دیر تک کھلا رہ سکتا ہے)» / «چھٹیوں میں زیادہ دیر تک کھلا رہ سکتا ہے۔»
    Which «چھٹیاں»? The weekly weekend, public holidays, school vacations? And how much later? «رہ سکتا ہے» on top of that makes it a "maybe" about an undefined period. If I were planning an evening visit I could not rely on this line at all. In the card the verb «کھلا رہ سکتا ہے» also has no subject in sight (the label is «اوقات»); in the FAQ the question supplies it.
    Grade: `moderate`. (Which days and until when are missing.)

14. **Card, fee row; repeated in the FAQ** — «بڑوں کے لیے 50 سعودی ریال»
    The moment a price is given «بڑوں کے لیے», I look for «بچوں کے لیے». It is not there, in either place, although the card two rows later recommends the place for «خاندان». A family reader is left with the obvious question unanswered.
    Grade: `moderate`. (The children's fee, or a statement that it is not known, is missing.)

15. **Card, fee row, second half; repeated in the FAQ without the gloss** — «ٹکٹ “ارض الحضارات” (تہذیبوں کی سرزمین) سے ملتے ہیں» / «ٹکٹ “ارض الحضارات” سے ملتے ہیں۔»
    I cannot tell what «ارض الحضارات» is. A website? An app? A company? A ticket window at the mountain? A different place I must visit first? The bracket translates the name but does not say what kind of thing it is, and «سے ملتے ہیں» works equally for all of these. My first reading was that tickets have to be collected from some other place called "Land of Civilizations" before going to the mountain. I also cannot tell whether I can simply pay at the entrance. In the FAQ the bracket is dropped, so someone who reads only the FAQ gets a bare Arabic name; «الحضارات» is not an Urdu word and I hesitated over how to read it. Finally, the notice line sends me to «وہاں کی انتظامیہ» without saying whether that is the same body. The two facts are also joined by a comma in the card as if they were one statement.
    Grade: `serious`. (What «ارض الحضارات» is, and how one actually buys, are missing; I cannot supply them.)

16. **Card, suitability row** — «کس کے لیے موزوں | خاندان · تصویر کشی · گرم دن»
    A mixed bag: a kind of visitor, an activity, and a kind of weather under one label. «گرم دن» as an answer to «کس کے لیے» made me re-read the label. «تصویر کشی» is correct but bookish; in everyday Urdu people say «فوٹوگرافی».
    Grade: `minor`.

17. **Suitability row read against the main paragraph** — «خاندان» vs «تنگ راہداریوں … چٹانیں کندھوں کو چھوتے چھوتے رہ جاتی ہیں»
    The paragraph describes passages so narrow the rock nearly brushes your shoulders; the card then says the place suits families. For someone travelling with elderly parents or small children that raises an immediate question (how much walking, how tight, how long does a visit take) which the page never touches.
    Grade: `moderate`. (Missing information, not a wording problem.)

18. **Notice line** — «اوقات اور فیس میں تبدیلی ہو سکتی ہے، اس لیے جانے سے پہلے وہاں کی انتظامیہ سے تصدیق کر لیں۔»
    Natural, polite Urdu; the best-sounding sentence in the practical section. Only «وہاں کی انتظامیہ» is vague as the text of a link: it does not tell me whether I will land on a phone number, a website, or something else, nor (see 15) whether it is the ticket seller.
    Grade: `minor`.

19. **FAQ, third answer** — «ان غاروں کا درجہ حرارت سال بھر معتدل رہتا ہے: جب گرمی عروج پر ہو تو خوشگوار ٹھنڈک، اور عین سردیوں میں ہلکی سی گرمائش۔»
    After the colon come two verbless fragments that do not grammatically continue «درجہ حرارت … رہتا ہے» (a temperature does not "stay a pleasant coolness"). I understood it, but it reads like the paragraph's sentence cut and pasted behind a new opening.
    Grade: `minor`.

20. **FAQ as a whole**
    All four answers repeat the card or the paragraph word for word; none adds anything. As a reader I reached the section hoping for the things the card left open (children, where to buy, which holidays, the summit) and found the same sentences again. That, together with «دن کا وقت۔», gives the section a machine-filled feel.
    Grade: `minor`.

21. **Home-page card, caption** — «ارضیاتی عجوبہ»
    «ارضیاتی» is a textbook or news-bulletin word (ارضیات = geology as a subject); on a small inviting card above a poetic summary it sounds stiff and technical. The summary below it is in a much warmer register.
    Grade: `minor`. A wording that would have worked: «قدرت کا عجوبہ».

---

Parts that read well and need no comment: the title; the opening sentence of the main paragraph («…کسی قدیم نگہبان کی طرح سر اٹھائے کھڑا ہے، مگر اس کا راز بلندی میں نہیں، اس کے اندر چھپا ہے۔»); the card heading «سیاحوں کے لیے معلومات»; the two button lines («گوگل میپس پر دیکھیں», «کوئی غلطی نظر آئی؟ ہمیں بتائیں»); the FAQ heading and all four questions. I found no gender, number or postposition errors anywhere on the page.
