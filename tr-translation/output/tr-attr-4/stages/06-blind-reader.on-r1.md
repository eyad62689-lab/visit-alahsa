# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), round 1

## Page 1 - Et-Tahimiye Doğu Camii

1. Tab/lead: "Et-Tahimiye'de çamurdan yapılmış eski bir cami". At first glance "Et-" reads as the Turkish word "et" (meat) and I had to re-parse it as an Arabic article. Same trap on every page with "El-", but "Et-" is the worst because it is also a real word. No fix, just flagging it as a first-pass misread.
2. "Hüfuf'a yaklaşık on iki kilometre uzaklıktadır." Hüfuf has not been introduced. I could not tell whether it is a city, a district or a landmark. Page 2 later implies a city ("Hüfuf'un merkezi"), but this page never says so. Something is missing.
3. "cami Et-Tahimiye Birinci Camii adıyla da bilinir" - "Birinci Camii" ("First Mosque") sounds odd. It begs the question of where the second one is. The page never answers it, even though it says the village had forty mosques. Left hanging.
4. "eskiden ise Ebu en-Nuuş Camii denirdi" - "Nuuş" with a double u: I read it as "nuş" (as in "nuş olsun") and was unsure how to pronounce it. The "ise" is also odd here. "eskiden ise" makes it sound like a contrast, but nothing contrasts. Also "eskiden / bir zamanlar / eskiden" appears three times across the lead and the first two sentences, which feels repetitive.
5. "Cenazeler yakındaki mezarlığa defnedilmeden önce cenaze namazı burada kılınırdı." I read it twice. "Cenazeler ... defnedilmeden" followed by "cenaze namazı" repeats the same word, and the subject is hard to spot. "Yakındaki mezarlık" does not say which one. Possible: "Yakındaki mezarlığa defnedilecek kişilerin cenaze namazı burada kılınırdı."
6. "Caminin tarihi yüzyıllar öncesine uzanır." Empty. "Yüzyıllar" tells me nothing, and no date appears anywhere on the page. I wanted to know how old it is. The date is missing.
7. "bugün bu camilerden ancak on bir kadarı ayakta" - fine. But "ayakta" for 11 mosques left leaves me wondering whether they are still in use. This is left hanging, not an error.
8. "Cami, iç avlunun çevresinde üç revaktan oluşan mütevazı bir çamur yapıdır." Stumbles. "Revak" is not a word most Turks know (I guess "colonnade/portico"). "Üç revaktan oluşan ... yapı" does not let me picture it (three porticoes around a courtyard?). "Çamur yapı" also sounds foreign; "çamurdan yapılmış" (as in the lead) is natural, and "kerpiç" may be what a Turkish writer would say. "Mütevazı" is also a slightly bookish choice.
9. "Dış duvarla çevrilidir, çatısı hurma ağacı gövdelerinden yapılmıştır ve yaklaşık elli kişi namaz kılabilir." The subject slips twice: the mosque, then the roof, then "elli kişi" ("fifty people"). The last clause has no locative ("camide"), and a Turkish writer would say "Camide yaklaşık elli kişi aynı anda namaz kılabilir" or "elli kişilik". The same pattern appears on the other pages.
10. "Eski Ez-Züheyri deresinin kıyısında inşa edilmiştir" - I do not know "Ez-Züheyri". "Eski ... deresi" makes me ask whether the stream still exists. Unclear. Also "Ez-" mid-sentence in capitals while other names such as "Cebel el-Kare" have lowercase "el" (see 11).
11. "Cebel el-Kare'nin yamaçları hemen yakındadır." "Kare" reads as the Turkish word "kare" (square). I misread "el-Kare" for a second. Also "Cebel" is never explained (a mountain?) and I was left guessing. The page names this place twice more but never says what it is.
12. "2017'de cami, El-Ahsa'nın resmen tescil edilmiş tarihî camileri arasına alındı." Bureaucratic. "Resmen tescil edilmiş" and "arasına alındı" say the same thing twice. It is also unclear who registered it. Possible: "2017'de El-Ahsa'nın tescilli tarihî camileri arasına girdi."
13. "Cebel el-Kare'ye yakın konumu, camiyi bölgeyi gezenler için uğraması kolay bir durak yapar." The brochure register arrives suddenly after neutral sentences. "Uğraması kolay" is fine Turkish but it is the only thing the page says about visiting. I still do not know whether I can enter, when, or what there is to see inside.
14. Whole page vs. Page 5: the page's title is "Doğu Camii" and Page 5 says its mosque is also "Doğu Camii" ("Cami Doğu Camii adıyla da bilinir"). Two different mosques share a name. This is confusing when reading both. East of what? Page 5 never explains either.

## Page 2 - El-Hubeyş Camii

1. Lead: "Güney er-Rifa mahallesinde" - I could not tell whether "Güney" is part of the name or means "south of er-Rifa". It is also lowercase "er-" here but "El-Kut", "El-Uveymiriye" are capitalised on other pages: an inconsistent look for place names (also "Cebel el-Kare" on Page 1).
2. Lead: "Şeyh Abdülaziz el-Alci'nin" - "Alci" reads like the Turkish "alçı" (plaster) or a Turkish surname "Alcı". I was unsure about the "c" (pronounced as in "cami"). Mild.
3. Lead: "Hicri on üçüncü yüzyıldan kalma" - I had to pause to convert. The body gives "(yaklaşık on dokuzuncu yüzyıl)", which helps, but the lead has no help. Not a contradiction, just effortful.
4. "Güney er-Rifa mahallesi, Hüfuf'un merkezindedir. Mahallede El-Hubeyş Camii bulunur;" Two sentences with the same "X mahallesi... Mahallede" frame, repeated on Pages 3 and 5. It sounds formulaic.
5. "El-Ahsa'nın en eski tarihî camilerinden biridir" - "en eski tarihî" is redundant, because "eski" and "tarihî" overlap. "El-Ahsa'nın en eski camilerinden biridir" would do.
6. "Şeyh Abdülaziz el-Alci Camii adıyla da bilinir." The name is given with no explanation, and I got the explanation only in the next sentence ("Bu ad ... birine aittir"). Fine, but the next sentence is weak: "Bu ad ... âlimlerinden birine aittir; âlimin dersleri bu camide verilirdi" repeats what the lead said.
7. "fıkıh ve dil bilgisi alanındaki önde gelen âlimlerinden" - "dil bilgisi" is ambiguous. I read it as "grammar", but of which language? (Probably Arabic, but the page does not say.) "Âlim" is a bit bookish, while Page 5 says "ilim ehli": the register wanders between pages.
8. "Cami, kireç taşı ve çamurdan yapılmıştır." Spelling: "kireçtaşı" is written as one word. Small.
9. "Çatısı şandal ağacı, bambu ve dokuma hasırlarla örtülüdür." "Şandal" reads as "sandal" (a shoe or a boat). I had to read the next sentence to learn it is timber. "Örtülüdür" with three materials, one of them a tree, sounds off. "Dokuma hasır" is fine.
10. "Şandal, eskiden Arap Körfezi bölgesine dışarıdan getirilen bir kerestedir." "Dışarıdan" - from where? Unanswered, and it is the question this sentence raises. The reader leaves with "imported from somewhere". Missing information, not a rewrite issue.
11. "Cami, yuvarlak kemerli revağı ve kuzeydoğu köşesindeki kare biçimli minaresiyle öne çıkar." "Revak" again unknown. "Öne çıkar" is a brochure phrase that Page 3 also uses.
12. "Toplam alanı yaklaşık 318 metrekaredir; yaklaşık doksan kişi namaz kılabilir." "Yaklaşık 318" - an approximation with an exact-looking number is odd. Numbers in words ("doksan") and digits ("318") sit in one sentence; on Page 3 the same mix recurs ("otuz" / "214"). "Cami ... namaz kılabilir" lacks "camide" (as noted on Page 1).
13. "'Prens Muhammed bin Selman Tarihî Camileri Geliştirme Projesi' kapsamında, projenin ilk aşamasında rehabilite edildi." "Rehabilite edildi" is bureaucratic and I read it as medical. Page 4 uses "restore edildi" and Page 3's lead also "restore edildi", so I cannot tell whether they mean different things. The project name is very long and heavy for a Turkish sentence.
14. "Özgün özelliklerini koruyarak yeniden ibadete açık, yaşayan bir cami oldu." The participle "koruyarak" attaches to "cami oldu", as if the mosque did the preserving. "Yeniden" implies it had been closed, but the page never said so. "Yaşayan bir cami" is a calque ("living mosque").

## Page 3 - Ümm Zureynik Camii

1. Tab/lead: "Ümm Zureynik" - "Ümm" is hard to read in Turkish (the double m after ü). I would read "Ümmü" or "Üm". Also the vowels are inconsistent across the pages: "Züheyri" (ü) on Page 1, "Zureynik" (u) here, "Ukayr" on Page 4. I could not predict how each name is pronounced.
2. Lead: "adını bitişiğindeki mezarlıktan alır ve tarihî camiler projesi kapsamında restore edildi." Two things at once, in a single long line. "Tarihî ... tarihî" repeated and "restore edildi" in the lead vs. "rehabilite edildi" in the body. Also, the "mezarlık" is never named, so I could not tell what the link is between a cemetery and "Ümm Zureynik". Hanging: the claim "adını mezarlıktan alır" cannot be checked, because the cemetery's name is missing.
3. "El-Uveymiriye mahallesi, Hüfuf merkezinin en eski semtlerinden biridir." Fine, but "mahalle" and "semt" used back-to-back are slightly confusing.
4. "Yüz yıldan eski, küçük, tarihî bir camidir ve adını yanındaki eski mezarlıktan alır." Three adjectives in a row, comma-separated, reads like a list. "Yüz yıldan eski" is vague: how much older? Tab said "bitişiğindeki", here "yanındaki", a variant of the same word.
5. "Cami, geleneksel yöntemle çamur, alçı ve hurma ağacı gövdelerinden yapılmıştır." "Geleneksel yöntemle" followed by the materials list is clumsy.
6. "Silindir biçimli, ahşap balkonlu minaresiyle öne çıkar; bu minare, Suudi Arabistan'ın Doğu Bölgesi'ndeki cami mimarisinin ayırt edici bir özelliğidir." Stumbles. "Bu minare ... özelliğidir" says one minaret is a feature of the architecture of a whole region. I think the intended meaning is that this type of minaret is characteristic. "Doğu Bölgesi" also reads as a generic "eastern region" until I realised it is a name.
7. "Ayrıca iç mekânda duvarlar, pencereler ve kemerler alçı süslemelerle bezelidir." "Ayrıca" feels tacked on. "Süslemelerle bezelidir" is redundant ("decorated with decorations") and a little Ottoman-bookish.
8. "Bir süre namaz kılınmayan cami, ... projenin ikinci aşamasında rehabilite edildi." "Bir süre" - how long? And no year is given for this restoration (Pages 2 and 5 give 2020). The reader is left asking when.
9. "Cami, yalnızca otuz metrekarelik bir alandan yaklaşık 214 metrekareye genişletildi" "Yalnızca" sounds judgmental or defensive. "Otuz" in words, "214" in digits, in the same sentence. "Yaklaşık 214" again an approximation with a precise figure. Also the lead and Page 2 stress that the original character is preserved, whereas here a 30 m² building becomes seven times larger, and the page does not say how that was done or what "restored" means here. Left hanging.
10. "yaklaşık 94 kişi namaz kılabilir" - "94" in digits here, "doksan" in words on Page 2. Also no locative ("camide").
11. "Böylece mahalle, ibadete açık bir camiye yeniden kavuştu." "Kavuştu" is a warm, almost sentimental verb; the rest of the page is neutral. "Böylece" turns the sentence into a conclusion that follows from the previous one (the capacity), which it does not really. "İbadete açık bir cami" and "yeniden" are fine.
12. Whole page: it is not stated whether the mosque can be visited, nor where in the neighbourhood it stands. That is not a defect to fix by adding information, but a reader planning a trip gets nothing practical.

## Page 4 - Ukayr Limanı Camii

1. Tab/lead: "Ukayr" - I would pronounce it "u-kayr", and the final "yr" cluster does not exist in Turkish. Other names on the pages use "ey" (Züheyri, Hubeyş, Zureynik, Uveymiriye, Cübeyl), but this one uses "ay". The inconsistency makes it look like a typo for "Ukeyr". Misread.
2. Lead: "Ukayr Limanı'nın yapıları arasında yer alan tarihî cami; Kral Abdülaziz döneminde geliştirilen liman tesislerinden biri." The mosque is described as one of the port facilities. "Geliştirilen" here, but "inşa edilen ve geliştirilen" in the body: a small mismatch. "Kral Abdülaziz" is not an issue (the "Kral" disambiguates from the Ottoman sultan), but no date or years are given in the whole page.
3. "Tarihî Ukayr Limanı, El-Ahsa'nın doğusundaki Arap Körfezi kıyısındadır; Suudi Arabistan'ın doğu kıyısındaki ilk deniz limanıdır." Two "doğu" within one line plus a chain of genitives ("El-Ahsa'nın doğusundaki Arap Körfezi kıyısındadır"). I had to read it twice. "İlk deniz limanı" - first in what sense (oldest? first built?). Unclear.
4. "Limanın yapıları arasında tarihî bir cami bulunur." This repeats the lead and does not name the mosque. It reads as if the page does not know which mosque it is describing.
5. "Necid'in denize açılan eski kapısında denizciler, tüccarlar ve yolcular bir zamanlar bu camide namaz kılardı." Stumbles. "Necid" reads as a male first name (Necid), and I did not know it was a region. The sentence starts with a place-phrase, "Necid'in ... eski kapısında", and I could not tell what the "gate" is. I worked out later that it is the port. Also "bir zamanlar" is used once more. Possible: first say that Ukayr was the gate, then the sailors.
6. "Cami, Kral Abdülaziz döneminde inşa edilen ve geliştirilen liman tesisleri arasında sayılır" - "sayılır" is the bureaucratic "is counted among". It is not clear whether the mosque was built in that period or only developed.
7. "kale, han, emirlik binası ve gümrük binası da bu tesislerdendir." "Emirlik binası" is a calque; "emirlik" is an emirate, not an office, and I could not tell what building it is. "Han" is fine. "Da bu tesislerdendir" is bookish.
8. "Ukayr, o günlerde El-Ahsa'nın ticaret damarıydı." "O günlerde" - which days? There is no earlier date. "Ticaret damarı" is a journalistic metaphor and sits next to the "kapı" metaphor of an earlier sentence: two figures for the same thing.
9. "Cami, tarihî camilerin restorasyon programı kapsamında restore edildi." "Restorasyon ... restore" repeats. Also, "tarihî camilerin restorasyon programı" is lowercase and generic. On Pages 2, 3 and 5 the program has a full, capitalised name ("Prens Muhammed bin Selman ... Projesi"). I could not tell whether this is the same program. Hanging.
10. "Bu çalışmayı Cübeyl ve Yenbu Kraliyet Komisyonu destekledi." Unintroduced body; "Cübeyl" reads like a first name, and the reader cannot tell what the commission is or why it is supporting a mosque in El-Ahsa. "Destekledi" - how? Unclear. Missing.
11. "Cami, programın kapsadığı El-Ahsa camilerinden biriydi." Redundant: we were just told it was restored under the program. The past tense "biriydi" also jolts after the present tense elsewhere. How many mosques the program covered is not said.
12. "Bugün cami, tarihî liman alanının içindedir." "Bugün" suggests it used to be elsewhere; it was already said to be among the port's buildings. Redundant with sentence 4.
13. "Eski liman ziyaretçilerine bütün hikâyesini anlatır; cami bu hikâyenin bir parçasıdır." Garden path: "Eski liman ziyaretçilerine" first reads as "to the old port's visitors" (visitors of the past), and only at "anlatır" do I see that the old port is the subject. Also personification and "bütün hikâyesini" is brochure language.
14. Whole page: nothing is said about the mosque's building (size, appearance, capacity, whether prayer is held). This page never tells me what I would do there, unlike the others. Missing, not to be filled in.

## Page 5 - Şeyh Muhammed bin Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Camii

1. Tab/lead: "Şeyh Muhammed bin Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Camii" - very long. "Ebu Bekir" brings the caliph to mind; "Mulla" reads as "molla" (a title, a scholar), so I read "El-Mulla ailesi" as "family of mullahs" rather than as a surname, until "ilim ehli" confirmed it.
2. Lead: "El-Kut mahallesinin kalbinde" - "Kut" is a real Turkish word ("kut", sacred good fortune), so "El-Kut" read oddly for a second. "Kalbinde" is a cliché, and the body repeats it in the first sentence.
3. Lead: "ilim ehli El-Mulla ailesiyle bağlantılı" - "ilim ehli" is Ottoman-bookish, and "bağlantılı" is vague and bureaucratic: connected how? The body answers only later (imams from the family). The relation of the person in the title to the family is not stated.
4. "Hüfuf'un tarihî El-Kut mahallesinin kalbinde Şeyh Muhammed bin Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Camii bulunur." A heavy chain of three genitives followed by a very long name before the verb: I lost the thread and had to re-read.
5. "Caminin yakınında El-Mulla ailesinin evi ve dinî okulları vardır." "Dinî okulları" is unclear: is it a "medrese" or a modern school? "Evi" is singular but "okulları" plural. It is not clear whether these still exist or can be seen.
6. "Cami Doğu Camii adıyla da bilinir." Same name as the mosque on Page 1 ("Et-Tahimiye Doğu Camii"). It confused me. Why "Doğu" for a mosque "in the heart" of the neighbourhood? Nothing explains it.
7. "El-Ahsa'nın en eski tarihî camilerinden biridir" - same "en eski tarihî" redundancy as Page 2. "Geçmişi yaklaşık üç yüzyıl öncesine uzanır" is fine, but "yaklaşık üç yüzyıl" and Page 2's "Hicri 13. yüzyıl (yaklaşık 19. yüzyıl)" are given in different units; I had to compare them by hand.
8. "Çamur, çakıl ve hurma ağacı gövdelerinden yapılan cami, uzun süre bir ilim merkezi olarak görev yaptı" - "görev yaptı" is a bureaucratic phrase for a building. "Uzun süre" is vague (when?). A long participle before the subject delays the verb.
9. "burada fıkıh, Kur'an tefsiri ve dil bilgisi dersleri verilirdi." "Dil bilgisi" again ambiguous (see Page 2). Otherwise fine. I could not tell whether lessons are still given.
10. "Caminin imamları kuşaktan kuşağa ilim ehli El-Mulla ailesinden geldi." "Geldi" reads a little odd ("came from the family"); "ilim ehli" repeats the lead. The page does not say whether the family still provides the imams.
11. "bu aşama Hicri 1441 (2020) yılında tamamlandı." "Hicri 1441 (2020) yılında" is awkward to read. Page 2 gives only "2020" for the same project stage and "Hicri on üçüncü yüzyıl" in words, whereas here "1441" is in digits. The calendar and number styles are mixed across the pages.
12. "Cami, ... projenin ilk aşamasında rehabilite edildi" - "rehabilite" (see Page 2 item 13) reads as a medical term. The semicolon then adds a second sentence that has "bu aşama", so it is not obvious whether "tamamlandı" refers to the stage or to the mosque.
13. "Geleneksel karakteri korundu; cami yaklaşık 125 kişi namaz kılabilir." "Cami ... 125 kişi namaz kılabilir" - the same missing locative as on the other pages ("camide"). The semicolon joins two unrelated statements, and "yaklaşık 125" is a precise-looking number with "yaklaşık".
14. Whole page vs. Page 2: the brand in the tab is "Visit Al-Ahsa" while the text writes "El-Ahsa" (all pages). A Turkish reader sees two spellings of the same name.
