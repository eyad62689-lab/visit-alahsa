Did the reader stumble on any of the three round-2 edits themselves? No.

- **tahimiyah-e, «… çatısı hurma ağacı gövdelerinden yapılmıştır ve camide yaklaşık elli kişi namaz kılabilir».**
  The reader made no remark on the sentence. The round-1 slip (the subject lost in the third coordinated clause)
  is not repeated. Two remarks sit next to it and neither is about «camide»: p1#5 (the bare «Dış duvar», a round-1
  edit that dropped «Bir» at the previous reader's request) and p1#6 (he went back to find the subject of «inşa
  edilmiştir»; he found it, «cami», and asks for no fix; the sentence before now names the mosque as the place).
- **uqair-mosque, «Eski liman, ziyaretçilerine bütün hikâyesini anlatır».** One remark (p2#11). The reader parsed
  the clause correctly: he calls it personification, so he read «Eski liman» as the subject that tells. The
  round-1/round-2 garden path («Eski liman ziyaretçilerine» as a compound) is gone. What he objects to («bütün
  hikâyesini» overclaims, the story is never told, «ziyaretçilerine» implies the port is open to visitors) is the
  English's own sentence («part of the full story the old port tells its visitors»). Not a misread caused by the
  comma.
- **abubakr-mosque, «Geleneksel karakteri korundu; camide yaklaşık 125 kişi namaz kılabilir».** One remark (p3#8),
  and it is not about «camide» (the old nominative-«cami» reading is not repeated). He says «karakteri» has no stated
  owner for a moment (it could be the project's or the district's «before I settled on the mosque»), and that
  «yaklaşık 125» looks precise and is in digits. The first is a hesitation on a clause that round 2 did not touch,
  resolved by the reader himself to the English's referent («preserving its traditional character», the subject «It»,
  the mosque); the clause after the semicolon now also names the mosque («camide»). The second is the English's
  figure and the digits/words point (r1 p1#14, P11). No edit; recorded as a note below.
