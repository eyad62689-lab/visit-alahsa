## The deciding question

Did the reader stumble on either round-1 edit itself? No.

- **kanzan-park.body, last clause (r2 p1#10).** The reader quotes «Projenin kalan inşaat aşamaları sürüyor; parkın tamamen hizmete girmesi henüz teyit edilmedi»
  and reads it correctly as «not confirmed that it has fully opened». Its alternative («or no confirmed date?») is a doubt about what the English leaves open
  («full operation is yet to be confirmed»), not a misreading, and the subject «parkın» is named and carries the verb. The complaints are «kalan … sürüyor»
  clumsy (English «the remaining construction phases are still under way») and that the page does not say which listed features can be used now (a missing
  fact: the body already says the upper section's features are in partial operation and the cable car and lakes only announced; nothing may be added). The reader
  also states the status correctly elsewhere (p1#6, p1#9: «is the section open now», «do the lakes exist or are they plans»), which is exactly the open
  question the page is built to leave open.
- **musa-park.summary (the edit; r2 p2#2–#4).** The reader quotes the lead only for the place name («Müberrez'in El-Faysal mahallesinde …»), for «kapsayıcı»
  and for «engelli çocukları akranlarıyla buluşturuyor», and understands the last as «children with disabilities and their peers». It does not stumble on the
  appositive «bir aile parkı, … girişiminin parçası» or on who does the verb (the equipment, «parkta»). The old stumble (r1 p3#2, the participle chain before the
  noun) is gone. Not a misread; the edit stands.
- **Anything else bad enough to hold a page below 90?** No. Every other note repeats an r1 ruling on unchanged text, is the English's own wording, is a missing fact,
  is the logged source defect, or is frame.
