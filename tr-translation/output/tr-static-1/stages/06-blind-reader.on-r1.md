# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r1

## Page 1 - Al-Ahsa Oasis and UNESCO (/tr/unesco/)

1. Search description: "El-Ahsa Vahası alanının on iki bileşeninden 6 tanesinin kendi sayfası var". Stumbles: "alanının ... bileşeninden ... tanesinin" is a stack of genitives, and the sentence mixes the spelled-out "on iki" with the digit "6". "6 tanesi" also sounds spoken, not written. The list of six names then arrives as a bare colon list.
2. "Listedeki numarası 1563'tür." I took a moment to see that "Listedeki" points back to the Dünya Mirası Listesi and "numarası" belongs to the oasis. Dry and bureaucratic, but understandable. The sentence does not say what the number is for (a reference number?).
3. "Bunlardan 6 tanesinin bu rehberde kendi sayfası vardır." Same mixed numeral problem ("on iki" in the previous sentence, "6" here). "tanesi" is colloquial next to the formal "vardır".
4. "Bileşenler burada resmî listedeki numara sırasına göre yer alır." Fine in itself, but the cards then start at 1563-004 and skip 008, 010 and 011. The page never says that the missing numbers are components without pages here. A reader may wonder whether something was lost. (Information is missing; I am not filling it in.)
5. Kasr İbrahim: "Duvarları çamurdan, çatıları hurma ağacı gövdelerinden bir kale". The two-part modifier before "bir kale" makes you wait for the noun. "Kasr" first read as the Turkish word "kasır" with a letter missing, so the name looked odd. The title says "Kasr" but the summary calls it a "kale"; the reader is left unsure whether it is a palace or a fortress.
6. El-Kaysariye Çarşısı: "El-Ahsa'nın en eski halk ve geleneksel çarşılarından biri". "halk ve geleneksel çarşı" sounds foreign. "Halk çarşısı" is not a Turkish category, and "halk" and "geleneksel" next to each other read as a literal carry-over. I could not tell what "halk" adds.
7. Same card: "zanaat ve yerel ürünler için bir gezi noktası". "gezi noktası" sounds foreign (a tourist-office phrase). Also "İki yüzyıldan eski tarihî bir çarşı" followed by "en eski ... çarşılarından biri" says the same thing twice.
8. Huzam Sarayı: the title says "Saray", the summary says "kale". Same title/summary mismatch as Kasr İbrahim. "Hüfuf'un batısında" is understandable, but Hüfuf is not introduced, so I do not know whether it is a town or a district.
9. Huzam and Sahud both end with nearly the same clause: "UNESCO Dünya Mirası Listesi'ndeki El-Ahsa Vahası alanının parçası" and "...alanının bileşenlerinden biri". Repeated boilerplate, and the page's own intro already says every card is a component. Also "parçası" vs "bileşenlerinden biri" is an unexplained switch of terms. The clause is a long genitive chain, and it was read twice.
10. Sahud Sarayı: "Müberrez'in kalbinde yedi kuleli, devasa bir savunma kalesi". "Müberrez" first read as the Ottoman-sounding adjective "müberrez" (distinguished), not a place name. "devasa" is brochure-loud next to the dry cards around it. Again "Saray" in the title and "kale" in the text.
11. Cüvasa Camii: "Medine'den sonra cuma namazının kılındığı ilk yer". I read it twice: it could mean "the first place after Medina where Friday prayer was held" or "the first place after Medina". The wording leaves the reading open. Also "El-Ahsa'nın ve İslam'ın en eski camilerinden biri" is a superlative stacked on another superlative claim.
12. El-Asfar Gölü: "Arap Körfezi bölgesindeki en büyük su kütlelerinden biri". "su kütlesi" is a hydrology textbook phrase; no one would call a lake that on a travel page. The superlative also has no unit or number, so I cannot judge it.
13. Same card: "göçmen kuşları ve doğa tutkunlarını kendine çekiyor". "kendine çekiyor" sounds a little foreign (calque). The subject is a lake, and I did not know what I would actually do there.
14. "UNESCO Dünya Miras Merkezi'nin bu alana ait sayfasında". The page elsewhere writes "Dünya Mirası Listesi/alanı", so "Miras Merkezi" reads like a slip. The usual Turkish form is "Dünya Mirası Merkezi". "bu alana ait sayfa" is bureaucratic.
15. Link text "UNESCO'da 1563 numaralı alan ↗". Fine, but "numaralı alan" repeats the earlier number sentence and is curt. The arrow is clear. Browser tab "UNESCO'da El-Ahsa Vahası" is fine.
16. Register shifts: the intro uses the formal aorist ("yer alır", "vardır"), while the cards switch to a lively present ("barındırıyor", "çekiyor"). The page reads as if two writers wrote it.

## Page 2 - report an error (/tr/report/)

1. "Doğru bilgi vermemize yardımcı olun" (the small heading). Stumbles: "vermemize yardımcı olun" is a double-clause that sounds like a translation. "Doğru bilgi vermemize" can be read as "so that we give correct information", but it took a second pass.
2. "Bizim için en önemli şey bilgilerin doğruluğudur." The word "şey" is casual, while the "-dır" ending is formal. Slightly mixed register. "bilgilerin" plural is also heavy.
3. "yanlış yere konmuş bir fotoğraf". Grammatical but unusual; a Turkish writer would more likely say "yanlış yerde kullanılmış" or "yanlış sayfada duran". Sounds foreign.
4. "bize bildirin, biz de inceleriz" is a warm, spoken ending after the formal first sentence. Slightly casual for the rest of the page.
5. Field label "E-posta adresiniz (isteğe bağlı, yanıt istiyorsanız)". The parenthesis has two ideas that overlap; it is not wrong, but "yanıt istiyorsanız" explains why you might fill it, so the first half is partly redundant.
6. Button "Bildirimi gönderin". Understandable. Turkish buttons are usually bare ("Gönder"), and "gönderin" with the polite imperative feels stiff. It matches the title "Hata bildirin", so it is consistent.
7. "Bildiriminiz doğrudan Visit Al-Ahsa ekibine ulaşır ve hiçbir kısmı yayımlanmaz." "hiçbir kısmı" is bureaucratic. It also does not say whether the e-mail address is stored. Left hanging: what happens to the data.
8. "Visit Al-Ahsa" is used with the Latin name here, but the page and the other pages say "El-Ahsa". Two spellings of the same place next to each other (the brand and the Turkish name). Understandable, but I paused.
9. Placeholder 1: the field label says "Hatanın bulunduğu sayfa" and the placeholder says "Sayfa bağlantısı veya gezilecek yerin adı". The label asks for a page, the placeholder offers two options; fine, but "gezilecek yerin adı" is long. Placeholder 3 "name@example.com" is English, whereas the rest is Turkish; harmless.
10. The hidden-field label "Bu alanı doldurmayın:" is clear on its own.

## Page 3 - shown after the report is sent (/tr/report/thanks/)

1. "Visit Al-Ahsa ekibi bildiriminizi inceleyecek ve hatalı olduğu doğrulanan her bilgiyi düzeltecek." The participial chain "hatalı olduğu doğrulanan her bilgiyi" is heavy. I read twice to see who verifies and what "hatalı olduğu" refers to. Bureaucratic.
2. Same sentence: no time frame and no promise of a reply. The reader may wonder whether they will hear back; the e-mail field was optional and this page does not mention it. Left hanging.
3. "Katkınız sayesinde El-Ahsa rehberi herkes için doğru bilgi vermeye devam eder." "sayesinde ... devam eder" is a generic thanks line; slightly preachy and formal. "rehberi" here is a guide, but the page earlier used "rehber" for a site; fine.
4. The browser tab "Bildiriminiz bize ulaştı, teşekkür ederiz — Visit Al-Ahsa" is very long for a tab; it will be cut off on a phone. The button "Ana sayfaya dönün" is fine.

## Page 4 - the 3D terrain map (/tr/terrain-map/)

1. Title "Arazi haritası". "arazi" means land or plot (property) in everyday Turkish; I first thought of a land-registry or off-road map. "Kabartma harita" or "rölyef haritası" would be what I expect. The description clarifies, but the title alone misleads.
2. "Cebel el-Kare" (description, list). Read "Kare" as the Turkish word "kare" (square). The same trap as `kara`. Needs the reader to know it is a name.
3. "Vahanın kabartma görünümü: ... vahanın doğusundaki kumullar." "kabartma görünümü" is a stretch; "kumul" is a less common word than "kum tepeleri". The sentence starts with a noun phrase and a colon, then lists; fine, but "Vahanın ... vahanın" repeats.
4. "Gezilecek her yer haritada kendi konumunda gösterilir." Passive and bureaucratic; "her yer" is a strong claim, and the list below is long, so I did not know if it is complete.
5. "Bir yeri haritada görmek için listeden o yerin adını seçin." Unclear: the name is not the control; only a small icon button (◎) next to it is, according to the page. A reader would click the name and nothing may happen. The icon is not explained on the visible page.
6. "Klasik harita →" and the no-JS "Klasik harita". Left hanging: I do not know what the "classic map" is (2D? a different map?). Never explained. The arrow suggests a link.
7. "Ukayr" appears as its own entry and then "Ukayr Kalesi", "Ukayr Limanı Camii", "Ukayr Plajı". What "Ukayr" alone is (a town? a site?) is not said. Left hanging.
8. "El-Kubbe Camii": "Kubbe" is an ordinary Turkish word (dome), so I read it as "the dome mosque" first. A name that reads as a common noun.
9. "Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Ailesi Ribatı": "Ribat" is a word I could not place; "Mulla" reads like "molla". Same family appears as "Şeyh Muhammed bin Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Camii", very long; the two spellings of the family name look inconsistent.
10. "Ümm Zureynik Camii": the doubled "mm" and "Ümm" looks odd to Turkish eyes; I could not tell how to pronounce it.
11. "El-Kut Parkı", "El-Musa Parkı", "Et-Taraf Hayvanat Bahçesi": "Kut", "Musa" and "Taraf" are ordinary Turkish words/names (kut, Musa/Moses, taraf = side), so I read them as such. "Taraf" in particular reads as "the side zoo".
12. "Zeki es-Salim Çiftliği": "Zeki" is a common Turkish given name and an adjective ("clever"), so the name looked half Turkish, half Arabic.
13. "Es-Sevsen Kuş Bahçeleri": "kuş bahçesi" sounds foreign; I would expect "kuş parkı" or "kuşhane". Plural "Bahçeleri" also made me unsure if it is one place or several.
14. "Eş-Şeybani / Muşakkar Parkı": the slash is unexplained. One park with two names, or two parks? Left hanging.
15. "Kanzan Macera Parkı (Eş-Şube)" is under "Doğa", while a separate "Parklar ve bahçeler" category exists, so the category choice puzzled me. The parenthesis "(Eş-Şube)" is not explained.
16. "Duğa el-Garaş Çömlek Atölyesi": "Duğa" and "Garaş" are hard to pronounce; "Garaş" looks like "garaj" or "karaş". Unclear whether "Atölye" is a workshop to visit.
17. "Spor Stadyumu Parkı" and "Kral Abdullah Çevre Parkı": the first is odd (a park of a stadium?); "Çevre Parkı" could read as "environment park" or "ring park". I could not tell what I would do there.
18. Naming inconsistency: some names are transliterated (El-Kaysariye Çarşısı), others translated (Kadınlar Çarşısı, Zanaatkârlar Çarşısı, Medeniyetler Diyarı, Çilek Çiftliği). Also "Cebel el-Erba", "Cebel Kanzan" and "Cebel Ebu Huseys" use "el-" or not. Reads uneven.
19. "Deneyimler ve el sanatları" has a single entry, "Hurma Köyü". A category name this big for one item looks like something is missing, and "Hurma Köyü" gives no hint of what it is.
20. Button label and tooltip: "Haritada gösterin: Beyt el-Biat" / "Haritada gösterin". The imperative "gösterin" is stiff; "Haritada göster" is what a Turkish interface would say. The tooltip has no object, so it is vague on its own.
21. Toggle "3D görünüm / 2D görünüm": it is unclear whether the label shows the current state or what the click will do. Left hanging; not answered by the page.
22. "Haritayı yakınlaştırmak için Ctrl tuşunu basılı tutarak kaydırın": "kaydırın" can mean scroll or swipe; understandable from context. Mac version with the "⌘" symbol alone is fine. "Haritayı hareket ettirmek için iki parmağınızla sürükleyin": "hareket ettirmek" sounds foreign; "kaydırmak" would be what I expect. Also "iki parmağınızla" is a surprise: I thought one finger would move a map.
23. "Sayfasına gidin →": "sayfasına" has no visible owner. In the popup the name probably sits above, but out of context it is a dangling pronoun. Also the imperative is stiff.
24. "Harita yükleniyor…" is fine.
