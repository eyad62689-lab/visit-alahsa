# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r2

## Page 1 - Al-Ahsa Oasis and UNESCO

1. "Listedeki alan numarası 1563'tür. Bu alan on iki bileşenden oluşur." Bureaucratic ("alan numarası", "alan" used for the whole site). Read fine but dry; a traveller would say "site"/"yer". Not a blocker.
2. "Bu rehberde 6 bileşenin kendi sayfası vardır." Understood, but the page never names the other six components, so the sentence raises "which are the other six?" and leaves it hanging (only the external link at the bottom hints at it). Missing, not to be filled in here.
3. "Bileşenler burada resmî listedeki numara sırasına göre yer alır." The numbers shown run 004, 005, 006, 007, 009, 012. The reader wonders where 001-003, 008, 010, 011 went; the gap is only explained indirectly by sentence 2. Left hanging.
4. "Bileşen 1563-004" etc. The hyphenated number looks like a date or phone number on first pass; it is not obvious that "1563" repeats the site number and "004" is the component.
5. Kasr İbrahim: "Duvarları çamurdan, çatıları hurma ağacı gövdelerinden bir kale; cami, kışla ve kuleleri bir arada barındırıyor." Clear and natural. Small stumble: the name says "Kasr" (palace/mansion) but the text says "kale" (fortress); I was briefly unsure what I would see.
6. Huzam Sarayı: "Hüfuf'un batısında..." Hüfuf has not been introduced; "Hüfuf" with ü reads strangely (I pronounce it with a front vowel), and the reader does not know this is a town in the oasis.
7. Huzam Sarayı: "...bir kale; UNESCO Dünya Mirası Listesi'ndeki El-Ahsa Vahası alanının parçası." Name says "Saray", text says "kale": same palace/fortress mismatch as in item 5. The second half just repeats what the page already says for every card and tells me nothing about what I would do there.
8. Sahud Sarayı: "Müberrez'in kalbinde yedi kuleli, devasa bir savunma kalesi; ... bileşenlerinden biri." "Devasa" is slightly brochure-loud; "savunma kalesi" is fine. The closing clause is again the filler repeated from Huzam. "Müberrez" is not introduced either (town vs district).
9. El-Kaysariye Çarşısı: "El-Ahsa'nın en eski halk ve geleneksel çarşılarından biri". "Halk ve geleneksel çarşı" sounds foreign (a calque); no Turkish writer would pair "halk" and "geleneksel" like that. Also "İki yüzyıldan eski tarihî bir çarşı" and "en eski" say the same thing twice.
10. El-Kaysariye Çarşısı: "zanaat ve yerel ürünler için bir gezi noktası". "Gezi noktası" sounds foreign/translated; "zanaat ve yerel ürünler için gezilecek bir yer" would be more natural.
11. Cüvasa Camii: "Medine'den sonra cuma namazının kılındığı ilk yer." On first pass I read "the first place after Medina" and was unsure whether this means the first outside Medina; it parses but needs a second look. Also "en eski camilerinden biri" and "ilk yer" sit slightly against each other (oldest-of-several versus first).
12. El-Asfar Gölü: "Arap Körfezi bölgesindeki en büyük su kütlelerinden biri". "Su kütlesi" is textbook/technical for a lake; "en büyük göllerinden biri" would be warmer, though the card already says "Gölü". "...doğa tutkunlarını kendine çekiyor" is acceptable but a little brochure-ish.
13. "On iki bileşenin tam listesini ve haritalarını UNESCO Dünya Miras Merkezi'nin bu alana ait sayfasında bulabilirsiniz." Fine; but "Dünya Mirası Listesi" above versus "Dünya Miras Merkezi" here reads as an inconsistency to a lay reader. Link text "UNESCO'da 1563 numaralı alan" again "alan" and is somewhat cryptic.
14. Search-engine description: "Kendi sayfası olan 6 bileşen:" is telegraphic and the colon list of names with no verbs reads like a keyword list.

## Page 2 - report an error

1. "Doğru bilgi vermemize yardımcı olun" (H1 lead-in). Stumbled once: "help us give correct information" is grammatical but clumsy; the page title right below ("Hata bildirin") already says it.
2. "Doğru bilgi bizim için her şeyden önemlidir." Register: a little grand and boilerplate ("her şeyden önemli" is overstated for a tourism site).
3. "...fark ederseniz bize bildirin, biz de inceleriz." Register shifts: the heading is formal-polite ("yardımcı olun") while "biz de inceleriz" is casual. Minor.
4. Label "E-posta adresiniz (isteğe bağlı, yanıt istiyorsanız)". Clunky: "isteğe bağlı" and "yanıt istiyorsanız" say the same thing; "yanıt almak istiyorsanız" is the natural verb.
5. Field label "Hatanın bulunduğu sayfa" vs placeholder "Sayfa bağlantısı veya gezilecek yerin adı". Label promises a page, placeholder also allows a place name; fine but not identical.
6. Button "Bildirimi gönderin". Turkish forms usually use the bare "Gönder" / "Bildirimi gönder"; the polite plural on a button sounds slightly foreign. Same for "Ana sayfaya dönün" on page 3.
7. Placeholder "Hatayı kısaca anlatın; doğru bilgiyi ve kaynağını biliyorsanız onları da ekleyin." Fine; "onları" should be "bunları" strictly, but few would notice. "name@example.com" is untranslated (a Turkish form would show something like ad@ornek.com), not a real problem.
8. "Bildiriminiz doğrudan Visit Al-Ahsa ekibine ulaşır ve yayımlanmaz." Clear. The brand "Visit Al-Ahsa" in Latin spelling next to Turkish "El-Ahsa" elsewhere looks like two different spellings of the same name.
9. Honeypot label "Bu alanı doldurmayın:" Understood. Only a note: if the label were ever visible it is perfectly clear.

## Page 3 - shown after the report is sent

1. "Katkınızla El-Ahsa rehberi herkes için doğru kalır." "Doğru kalır" sounds foreign (calque of "stays accurate"); "doğru bilgiyle kalır" or "güvenilir kalır" would read naturally. Also "El-Ahsa rehberi" can be read as a tour guide (person) rather than the guidebook/site.
2. "Visit Al-Ahsa ekibi bildiriminizi inceleyecek ve doğrulanan hataları düzeltecek." Clear and fine; no timescale and no mention of a reply, which is fine because none is promised. "Visit Al-Ahsa" vs "El-Ahsa" in the next sentence: same spelling inconsistency as on page 2.
3. Title "Bildiriminiz bize ulaştı, teşekkür ederiz" is natural and good.
4. "[button] Ana sayfaya dönün" - see page 2 item 6 (polite plural imperative on a button).

## Page 4 - the 3D terrain map

1. Title and description naming: tab says "Arazi haritası", description says "Vahanın kabartma görünümü". Two terms for the same thing; "arazi haritası" sounds foreign (I expect "kabartma harita" or "topografik harita"), and "kabartma görünümü" is also a little off.
2. "Cebel el-Kare" (description, list). Misread on first pass: "Kare" reads as the Turkish word "kare" (square). I had to realise it is a mountain name. "Cebel" itself reads as old-fashioned/Ottoman.
3. "Gezilecek yerlerin hepsini haritada kendi konumlarında bulabilirsiniz." "Kendi konumlarında" is foreign; "hepsini haritada yerleriyle birlikte görebilirsiniz" would be natural.
4. "Bir yeri haritada görmek için listeden o yerin adını seçin." Unclear: the thing to press is the small icon (◎) beside the name, not the name; the page never says what the ◎ symbol is. Also nothing says whether the map must be opened first with "Arazi haritasını açın" (the button sits above the list), so I would not know what pressing ◎ does on a map that is still a picture. Left hanging.
5. "Klasik harita →" appears before the map button with no explanation of what "klasik" means (classic versus new?). The reader cannot tell it is a flat/2D alternative.
6. "Gezi rehberi" (eyebrow). Fine; again "rehber" could be a person, but here it is clearly the guidebook section.
7. "El-Hamidiye Çarşısı" misreads for a Turkish reader: "Hamidiye" immediately evokes Sultan Abdülhamid (Hamidiye Kruvazörü, Hamidiye çeşmesi), and I briefly assumed an Ottoman connection. It is also filed under "Tarihî yerler", while the other markets (El-Kaysariye, Kadınlar Çarşısı) sit under "Geleneksel çarşılar"; inconsistent grouping.
8. "Ukayr" and "Ukayr Kalesi" both under "Tarihî yerler". Looks like a duplicate; unclear if "Ukayr" is the town or a separate site. "Ukayr" is hard to pronounce on first pass (ay diphthong).
9. "Dar et-Turas" under Müzeler: left untranslated while neighbours say "Müzesi"; the reader cannot tell it is a museum from the name. "El-Ahsa Evi Müzesi" and "El-Ahsa Müzesi" look near-identical.
10. "Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Ailesi Ribatı", "Şeyh Muhammed bin Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Camii": "Mulla" reads oddly because Turkish spells it "Molla"; the long name is heavy but understandable. "Ribat" is a known-ish word but the reader may not know it is a religious building (it sits under "dinî mekânlar", which helps).
11. "El-Kubbe Camii": "Kubbe" is an ordinary Turkish word (dome), so it reads as "the dome mosque" rather than a proper name. Same for "El-Kut Parkı" (kut = a Turkish word) and "El-Musa Parkı" (reads as Moses).
12. "Ümm Zureynik Camii": "Ümm" with ü sounds strange; Turkish writers would use "Ümmü" or "Umm". "Zureynik" is hard to pronounce.
13. "Kanzan Macera Parkı (Eş-Şube)": the parenthetical "Şube" is an everyday Turkish word (branch/department), so it reads as "branch" and I did not understand it as a place name. Unclear what it adds.
14. "Cebel eş-Şaban": "Şaban" reads as the Turkish male name/month. "Cebel el-Erba" and "Cebel es-Suleym" fine but unfamiliar.
15. "Es-Sevsen Kuş Bahçeleri": "Sevsen" parses as "sevsen" (if you loved), and "kuş bahçeleri" sounds foreign (calque); "kuş parkı" is what I would expect.
16. "Eş-Şeybani / Muşakkar Parkı": the slash is unclear: one park with two names, or two places?
17. "Medeniyetler Diyarı": the only park name translated into Turkish instead of transliterated; reads like a slogan and I cannot tell what it is. Also "Spor Stadyumu Parkı" is bland but clear.
18. "Duğa el-Garaş Çömlek Atölyesi" filed under "Geleneksel çarşılar": a workshop in a markets category; "Duğa" is hard to read.
19. "Hurma Köyü" under "Deneyimler ve el sanatları": a category with one item; nothing says what you do there (a village? a shop?). Missing, not to be filled in.
20. Mixed naming strategy in one list: "Kadınlar Çarşısı", "Zanaatkârlar Çarşısı", "Çilek Çiftliği" are translated, while "El-Kaysariye Çarşısı" and "Zeki es-Salim Çiftliği" are transliterated. "Çilek Çiftliği" has no place name and could be anywhere.
21. Button text "Haritada gösterin: ..." / "Haritada gösterin". Polite plural on an icon button sounds like an instruction to me; Turkish UI would say "Haritada göster". The ◎ glyph is not explained anywhere.
22. "3D görünüm / 2D görünüm": ambiguous whether the label shows the current mode or the one you will switch to. "3D" in Latin digits is common but "3B" is the official Turkish form; not a problem for most readers.
23. "Haritayı yakınlaştırmak için Ctrl tuşunu basılı tutarak kaydırın": natural and clear. Mac version "⌘ tuşunu": the symbol alone may not be recognised; "Command (⌘)" would be clearer. Minor.
24. "Haritayı hareket ettirmek için iki parmağınızla sürükleyin": "haritayı hareket ettirmek" sounds foreign (it means set the map in motion); "haritada gezinmek" or "haritayı kaydırmak" is what a Turkish writer would say.
25. "Sayfasına gidin →" (popup link): stands alone with no noun; "whose page?" Only the popup context makes it understandable. Also polite-plural imperative again.
26. "Harita yükleniyor…" fine.
27. No-JavaScript fallback "Klasik harita" is a bare label: does not tell the reader that the 3D map needs JavaScript or why this is shown instead. Left hanging.
28. Link on /tr/map/: "3D arazi haritası →" is clear, but the destination page's main term is "Arazi haritası" and its toggle is "3D görünüm"; slight naming drift between link, title and toggle.
