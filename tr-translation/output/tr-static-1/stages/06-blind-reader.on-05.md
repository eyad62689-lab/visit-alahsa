# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-05

## Page 1 - Al-Ahsa Oasis and UNESCO

1. Search description: "on iki bileşeninden 6 tanesinin kendi sayfası var". Spelled-out "on iki" and the digit "6" sit side by side in one clause. The digit plus "tanesi" reads clumsy, and the two number styles clash. Both could be digits or both words.
2. Same description, "bileşen". As a Turkish reader I understand it, but it sounds like a calque of "component". It is bureaucratic for a tourist site; "bölüm" or "parça" would be more ordinary. It recurs in every card caption ("Bileşen 1563-004").
3. "1563 numaralı alan olarak alındı". "Alındı" alone is vague. I first read "was taken as area no. 1563". "Listeye alındı" is clear only from the preceding words, and "alan olarak" is stiff.
4. "Bileşenler burada resmî listedeki numara sırasına göre yer alır." The cards start at 1563-004 and skip 008, 010 and 011. The sentence makes me expect a complete run, and the page never says why 001-003 are missing. The sentence "Alan on iki bileşenden oluşur. Bunlardan 6 tanesinin..." does explain the skipped numbers, but the missing 001-003 is left hanging.
5. Kasr İbrahim: "Duvarları çamurdan, çatıları hurma ağacı gövdelerinden bir kale". The long pre-noun modifier chain delays "bir kale" to the end, so I read it twice. "Kasr" is also not the usual Turkish form ("kasır"). I took the name as a palace, and then the summary says "kale".
6. Same card: "cami, kışla ve kuleleri bir arada barındırıyor". Fine on its own, but it shows the mixed endings across cards: some summaries end in a finite verb (barındırıyor, çekiyor) and some are noun fragments (bir gezi noktası). The register wobbles between brochure present tense and encyclopedic fragment.
7. El-Kaysariye: "El-Ahsa'nın en eski halk ve geleneksel çarşılarından biri". "Halk ve geleneksel çarşı" sounds foreign (it reads as translated "popular and traditional markets"). I could not tell whether "halk" means a public or folk market. It also repeats "geleneksel", already in the caption "Geleneksel çarşılar". It also says "iki yüzyıldan eski" and "en eski ... biri" back to back, which is slightly redundant.
8. Same card: "zanaat ve yerel ürünler için bir gezi noktası". "Gezi noktası" is a calque; no Turkish writer would say it that way. It does not say what you actually do there, since it names crafts and local products but not whether you buy, watch or just stroll.
9. Huzam Sarayı: "Hüfuf'un batısında, ... bir kale; UNESCO Dünya Mirası Listesi'ndeki El-Ahsa Vahası alanının parçası." The title says "Sarayı" (palace) but the text says "kale" (fort), which causes a mismatch on first pass. The second half is boilerplate repeating what the whole page already says (the same clause appears on the Sahud card), so it carries no information about the place itself. "Hüfuf" is unfamiliar and never placed relative to the oasis.
10. Sahud Sarayı: "Müberrez'in kalbinde yedi kuleli, devasa bir savunma kalesi". "Kalbinde" is brochure-style and "devasa" is a shouty adjective next to the otherwise flat cards. The same palace/fort mismatch applies as in item 9. "Müberrez" is a new place name with no explanation.
11. Cüvasa Camii: "Medine'den sonra cuma namazının kılındığı ilk yer." I first read it as "the first place after Medina", which is not quite a mosque claim. Even in the right reading it is a big claim for a one-line card, and nothing says where it comes from. "El-Ahsa'nın ve İslam'ın en eski camilerinden biri" is two superlatives in a row.
12. El-Asfar Gölü: "Arap Körfezi bölgesindeki en büyük su kütlelerinden biri". "Su kütlesi" is textbook or bureaucratic wording; a travel text would say "göllerinden biri", but the title already says "Gölü", so the sentence feels redundant.
13. Same card: "göçmen kuşları ve doğa tutkunlarını kendine çekiyor". It sounds like a brochure ("kendine çekiyor"). The coordination of birds and nature enthusiasts as objects is unusual. It still does not say what you would do there.
14. "Resmî kaynak ... UNESCO Dünya Miras Merkezi'ndeki sayfasındadır." The body text says "Dünya Mirası" everywhere else, but here it is "Dünya Miras Merkezi". The Turkish name is normally "Dünya Mirası Merkezi", so this stumbles and is inconsistent. "...sayfasındadır" also reads stiff ("the full list is on its page").
15. Tab/H1 vs body: the tab says "UNESCO'da El-Ahsa Vahası" while the heading says "Dünya Mirası Listesi'nde El-Ahsa Vahası". Both fine, but the search description says "(2018)" in brackets inside a sentence, which reads oddly in Turkish.

## Page 2 - report an error

1. "Bizim için en önemli şey doğruluktur." A bit stiff and slogan-like; "doğruluk" could mean honesty or accuracy and I had to pause. It is the same sentence in the description and the body.
2. "...bize bildirin, biz de inceleyelim." "Biz de inceleyelim" is hortative in form and reads casual and a little odd after the formal "-in" imperatives; I took it as "let's look into it" said to the reader. "İnceleriz" would be calmer.
3. "yanlış yerde duran bir fotoğraf". "Duran" (standing) is a calque of "misplaced". A Turkish writer would say "yanlış yere konmuş" or "yanlış yerde kullanılmış".
4. Heading "Doğru bilgi vermemize yardım edin". Grammatical but heavy. "Doğru bilgi vermemize yardımcı olun" is more natural, and "yardım edin" can read as pleading.
5. Label "İlgili sayfa". "İlgili" is bureaucratic and ambiguous (related page? the page concerned?). The placeholder "Sayfa bağlantısı veya gezilecek yerin adı" clears it up only after I read it.
6. Label "E-posta adresiniz (isteğe bağlı, yanıt istiyorsanız)". The parenthesis stacks two conditions and "isteğe bağlı" followed by "yanıt istiyorsanız" is slightly redundant but understandable.
7. "[button] Bildirimi gönderin". A finite polite imperative on a button is uncommon; Turkish buttons normally use a bare noun or "Gönder". Also "bildirim" first reads as "notification" before "report".
8. "Bildiriminiz doğrudan Visit Al-Ahsa ekibine ulaşır; hiçbir bölümü yayımlanmaz." "Hiçbir bölümü" is bookish. I could not tell whether the e-mail address is included in "bölüm". Mixed spelling: "Visit Al-Ahsa" here but "El-Ahsa" elsewhere in the same text.
9. Placeholder "name@example.com". It is English and not localised (a Turkish page would usually show "ad@ornek.com" or similar). Not a problem for understanding.
10. Honeypot label "Bu alanı doldurmayın:". Fine, but it is an odd instruction if a visitor ever sees it, since it gives no reason.

## Page 3 - shown after the report is sent

1. Tab title "Bildiriminiz bize ulaştı — teşekkür ederiz — Visit Al-Ahsa". Two em dashes in one title; long and fussy.
2. "doğrulanan her bilgiyi düzeltecek". Stumble: "verified information" is by definition already right, so "correct every verified piece of information" reads as a contradiction on first pass. The meaning must be "every error that is confirmed", but the sentence says something else.
3. "notunuzu" versus the title word "Bildiriminiz". Two different words (bildirim / not) for the same thing within one short page.
4. "Katkınız El-Ahsa rehberinin herkes için doğru kalmasını sağlar." "Doğru kalması" is a bit grand and unnatural; "doğru kalması" does not quite say "accurate", and the sentence is a promise larger than the form supports.

## Page 4 - the 3D terrain map

1. Title "Arazi haritası". "Arazi" in Turkish usually means land or a plot (cadastral, off-road), so on first read I expected a land-registry or off-road map, not a relief map. The description itself says "kabartmalı".
2. "Vahanın kabartmalı hâli". Poetic and slightly odd ("the relief state of the oasis"); "hâli" is bookish here.
3. "Gezilecek her yer de yerinde duruyor." Stumbled: it sounds like an idiom ("everything is still in its place") and I could not tell what it promises. Probably "every place is shown at its real location", but that is a guess.
4. "doğudaki kumullar". East of what? The sentence does not say, so it is left hanging.
5. "Bir yeri haritada görmek için listeden o yerin adını seçin." The list shows the name next to a small icon button (◎), so it is unclear whether the name or the icon is the control.
6. "Cebel el-Kare" (description and list). "Kare" reads straight as the Turkish word for "square", and "Cebel" is rare. The mispronunciation or misreading is easy.
7. "Klasik harita →" and "Klasik harita" (no-JS block). Unclear what "classic" means compared with "arazi". Nothing says what it is or why you would choose it.
8. "(◎) El-Hamidiye Çarşısı". A Turkish reader links "Hamidiye" to Sultan Abdülhamid and the Ottoman Hamidiye name, so this reads as an Ottoman landmark. It is listed under "Tarihî yerler", while El-Kaysariye Çarşısı is under "Geleneksel çarşılar", so two markets are filed differently without explanation.
9. "Ukayr" and "Ukayr Kalesi" listed separately under Tarihî yerler. Two near-identical entries with no hint of the difference.
10. "(◎) Dar et-Turas" under Müzeler. "Dar" is untranslated; the name alone does not say it is a museum.
11. "(◎) El-Halife Miras Müzesi". In Turkish "miras" means inheritance, so "Miras Müzesi" reads as an inheritance museum; "Kültür Mirası Müzesi" would be natural. (It is a calque of "Heritage Museum".)
12. "(◎) Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Ailesi Ribatı". "Ribat" is a bookish word and "Ailesi" makes the name very long; I could not tell what it is (a mosque? a lodge?). The following "Şeyh Muhammed bin Ebu Bekir el-Mulla Camii" is long enough to be hard to scan, and "Mulla" reads as "molla".
13. "(◎) El-Hubeyş Camii" and "(◎) El-Hubeyl Gölü". The two names differ by one letter and sit on the same page. Easy to confuse.
14. "(◎) El-Kut Parkı". "Kut" immediately evokes "Kut'ül-Amâre" and the Turkish word "kut" (sacred luck). Misread on first pass.
15. "(◎) Kanzan Macera Parkı (Eş-Şube)". "Şube" is an ordinary Turkish word (branch, office), so the parenthesis reads as "branch" rather than a place name. It is also placed under "Doğa", while the other parks are under "Parklar ve bahçeler".
16. "(◎) Eş-Şeybani / Muşakkar Parkı". The slash is unclear: one park with two names, or two parks?
17. "(◎) Medeniyetler Diyarı" under "Parklar ve bahçeler". "Diyar" is bookish, and I could not tell what kind of place this is (a park? an exhibit?). Nothing explains it.
18. "Deneyimler ve el sanatları" has one entry only, "(◎) Hurma Köyü". It is not clear whether this is a village or a venue, and a category with a single entry looks unfinished.
19. Category "Doğa" mixes mountains ("Cebel ..."), lakes and beaches, with names such as "Cebel eş-Şaban" and "Cebel es-Suleym" where the article is written es-/eş-/ed-/ez-. A reader does not know the rule and the variation looks like inconsistency.
20. aria-label "Haritada gösterin: Beyt el-Biat" and title "Haritada gösterin". The polite imperative tells the user to show it on the map, which reads as if the user is being asked to do the work. A button says "Haritada göster" in natural Turkish. "Beyt" is also archaic.
21. "[toggle] 3D görünüm / 2D görünüm". Unclear whether the label shows the current mode or what the button will switch to.
22. "Haritayı hareket ettirmek için iki parmağınızla sürükleyin". Understandable, but "hareket ettirmek" is clumsy for panning ("kaydırmak" would be the usual word), and "kaydırın" is used for scrolling in the Ctrl line above, so one verb is doing two jobs.
23. "Sayfasına gidin →" at the foot of a popup. The possessive "sayfasına" has no visible antecedent in the text I was given, and the polite imperative plus arrow is slightly stiff.
