# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-05

## Page 1 - food (/tr/food/)

1. Browser tab: "El-Ahsa mutfağı — Visit Al-Ahsa". Unclear / spelling mismatch. The page says "El-Ahsa" everywhere, but the tab ends with "Al-Ahsa". It reads as two different places on first pass. I only noticed it was the brand name because of the "Visit".

2. Search description: "El-Ahsa mutfağında bir gezinti: ... mandi, haris, mufallak, hurma pekmezli balalit, hurma asidesi, sagu ve hurma ekmeği." Misreads on first pass. "haris" reads as the Turkish word "haris" (greedy, avid), so I took it for an adjective in a list of dishes. "mufallak" and "balalit" are words I cannot place at all. I only learn from the cards that they are dishes.

3. "hurma asidesi" (description, card title, body). Misreads on first pass. "asidesi" looks like "asit" (acid) with a suffix, so "date acid" is my first reading. Only "kaşıkla yenen yumuşak bir lapa" in the card makes it clear this is a dish. The two-syllable "aside" is not a word most Turkish readers know as a food.

4. "Bu malzemelerden, sofralarda kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılan sıcak yemekler doğar." Hangs / does not fit. "Sıcak yemekler" does not match the page, which has sweets (balalit, aside, sagu) and bread. "doğar" is a literary verb for cooking, and it comes back in the haris card ("haris doğar"). The intro ingredient list also leaves out hurma itself, although most of the eight dishes are built on it.

5. "hurma pekmezi (dibs)", "kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi)". Misreads on first pass. "dibs" reads as an English word. "lomi" looks like a typo or a name. Both are given in parentheses with no pronunciation cue. "lomi" is never used again as a dish word, only inside card bodies, so the reader has to remember it. "Misket limonu" is understandable, but "kurutulmuş misket limonu" is listed under "baharatlarla (...)" in the pilav card. A dried lime is not a spice, so the parenthesis reads slightly oddly.

6. "Lezzet turu". Unclear. "Tur" suggests a guided food tour I could book. Below it is just a list of eight dishes. It promises an activity and delivers a menu.

7. "Vahanın kalbinden sekiz yemek". Register. Brochure-ish, though it is acceptable. The "sekiz yemek" count matches the cards (8). But the cards include three sweets and a bread, which I would not call "yemek" in a strict sense. This is a minor point.

8. Pilav: "Yerel El-Ahsa pirinci ...". Foreign. "Yerel" and "El-Ahsa" are redundant together, and the same phrase opens the intro. It sounds like a translated label.

9. Pilav: "rengi kızılımsı kahverengiye döner, lezzeti zenginleşir". Fine to read, but "El-Ahsa pilavı" in Turkish suggests a plain rice side dish. The card describes meat, tomato sauce and a main course. The label "Ana yemek" rescues it, but I had to correct my first picture.

10. Mandi: "El-Ahsa'da mandi pişirilmekten çok, sırlar gibi toprağa emanet edilir." Stumbles. I read it twice. "pişirilmekten çok" is a calque (Turkish would say "pişirilmekten ziyade"), and "sırlar gibi toprağa emanet edilir" ("is entrusted to the earth like secrets") is overwrought. "emanet" is a burial/trust word, so it sounds almost funereal. The register is odd for a food page.

11. Mandi: "Sade söylemek gerekirse, mandi toprağa açılmış bir çukura gömülü bir tandırda pişer." Sounds foreign and stumbles. "Sade söylemek gerekirse" is a calque; "Kısacası" or "Basitçe söylemek gerekirse" would have worked. The participial chain "toprağa açılmış bir çukura gömülü bir tandırda" stacks three "bir" and I lost the thread before the verb arrived. The meaning is "in a tandır buried in a pit dug in the ground".

12. Mandi: "Et saatlerce kil tandırın soluğunu içine çeker, pirinç ise hurma ağacının ve dumanın kokusunu taşır." Stumbles. "Tandırın soluğunu içine çeker" is an unfamiliar image. Also, "pirinç" appears suddenly. The page never says what mandi is (rice plus meat), only what the meat and rice do. Which meat is not said. It is left missing, not wrong.

13. Mandi: "Bu yemek, kelimeleri yokken cömertliği bilen bir vahanın hikâyesini anlatır." Unclear and off in register. "Kelimeleri yokken" is ambiguous. It could be "when the dish has no words" or "when the oasis had no words". The sentence is preachy and tells me nothing about what I would eat or do.

14. Haris: "Buğday ve et saatlerce birbirine karışıp tek bir ruh olunca haris doğar." Off in register. "tek bir ruh" is overly poetic for a recipe-ish description. Combined with "doğar", this is the second birth image on the page.

15. Haris: "El-Ahsa'nın ziyafetlerinde ve düğünlerinde cömertliğin ilk yemeğidir." Unclear. "Cömertliğin ilk yemeği" is a slogan, not a statement. Does "ilk" mean first served, most important or oldest? Also "cömertlik" was already used in the mandi card, so the word becomes repetitive. The "Bu sayfa hakkında" list names "düğün ziyafetleri", while this card says "ziyafetlerinde ve düğünlerinde", which is a small mismatch.

16. Haris: "Ramazan sofralarının da çok eski bir dostudur." Off in register. Personification ("dost") is twee. "çok eski" is not backed by any detail. Nearly the same construction as sagu's "Ramazan sofralarının başında gelen".

17. Mufallak: "Geleneksel bir El-Ahsa yemeğidir." Unclear. The sentence says nothing. This is the weakest opening on the page, after a strong claim in the title card. The photo description says "hurmalı yarma buğday", which tells me more than this sentence. The card never says what the dish tastes like.

18. Mufallak: "Yarma buğday (ceriş) baharatlarla ve kurutulmuş misket limonuyla pişirilir, sonra hurma ve haşlanmış yumurtayla süslenir." Fine to read, but "ceriş" appears only here. Is it a second name or a variety? Not clear. "süslenir" (decorated) for a date and egg topping reads like a brochure.

19. Mufallak: "Vahanın lezzetlerini bir araya getiren doyurucu bir tabaktır." Register. Filler sentence; it adds nothing and could belong to any dish.

20. Balalit: "Altın sarısı tel şehriye safran ve kakuleyle kokulandırılır, El-Ahsa usulüne göre hurma pekmeziyle tatlandırılır." Stumbles. "Altın sarısı tel şehriye" followed by two passive verbs in one sentence; I read "altın sarısı" as describing the finished dish, not the vermicelli. "El-Ahsa usulüne göre" is bureaucratic.

21. Balalit: "Hurma ağacının başkenti olan bir topraktan geldiği için tatlılık hurmanın kendisinden gelir." Unclear and logical jump. The causal "için" does not hold: the dish comes from a place that is "the capital of the date palm", therefore the sweetness comes from dates? It also repeats "geldiği ... gelir". "Hurma ağacının başkenti" is brochure-shouty. In addition, saffron and cardamom, and "şeker" in other cards, make "tatlılık hurmanın kendisinden gelir" sound like a claim that needs checking.

22. Balalit: "Balalit omletle birlikte sunulur." Unclear. A sweet with an omelette surprised me. Is it on top, beside, mixed in? The photo caption says "Omlet tavasının yanında", which does not settle it. I do not know what I would actually be served. The card should say how, but I cannot supply it.

23. Aside: "El-Ahsa'nın kendi hurması hafif ateşte buğday unuyla birlikte erir." Stumbles. "Kendi hurması ... erir" is the subject doing something odd: do the dates melt, with the flour? Also the card never mentions pekmez, although the photo caption describes "ortasında hurma pekmezi çukuru". Photo and text do not match.

24. Aside: "Sonra sadeyağ ve bir tutam karabiberle taçlandırılır: sadeyağ parlaklık verir, karabiber sıcaklığını canlandırır." Foreign. "taçlandırılır" is brochure language. "karabiber sıcaklığını canlandırır": whose "sıcaklığını"? Does it mean heat as spice, or warmth as in a winter dish? Also "karabiber" read normally as black pepper; no problem here.

25. Aside: "Aside kaşıkla yenen yumuşak bir lapadır; soğuk gecelerde ailenin çevresinde toplandığı El-Ahsa kış tatlısıdır." Stumbles and off in register. "Lapa" in Turkish suggests a bland porridge or a poultice and is unappetizing. The final part is a long participial chain ("soğuk gecelerde ailenin çevresinde toplandığı El-Ahsa kış tatlısıdır") and I lost the thread before the verb arrived. "ailenin çevresinde toplandığı" has an odd possessive: the family gathers around what? I read it as "around the family". "ailelerin etrafında toplandığı" would have worked.

26. Sagu: "Küçük taneler suda pişirilir ve şekerle tatlandırılır." Unclear. "Küçük taneler" of what? The title says "Sagu" and the photo mentions nuts, but the sentence has no noun. It is left missing, not wrong.

27. Sagu: "Ateşte eriyerek saydam bir mücevhere dönüşür; safran ve kakule onu ışıldatır." Foreign and off in register. "mücevher" is brochure-shouty. Sago does not melt; "eriyerek" misleads. Spices do not "ışıldatır" (make something shine); I stopped on this.

28. Sagu: "Ramazan sofralarının başında gelen El-Ahsa tatlısıdır." Misreads on first pass. "başında gelen" can read as "served at the start of the Ramazan table" rather than "foremost among". Also "ramazan" is lowercase in the "Bu sayfa hakkında" list but "Ramazan" capitalised in the cards, so the page is inconsistent.

29. Sagu: "Dokusu narin, geçmişi köklüdür; hazırlanışı vaha halkı arasında kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılmıştır." Foreign / register. Stock-phrase filler, and "kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılan" has already appeared in the intro. The shift to "-mıştır" is bookish compared with the present tense of the rest of the page.

30. Hurma ekmeği: "Yerel bir ekmektir." Unclear. Bare statement. Same problem as mufallak: the card opens with nothing.

31. Hurma ekmeği: "Hamuruna hurma ezmesi yoğrulur ya da içine hurma ezmesi doldurulur; üzerine çörek otu serpilir." Fine to read, but "hurma ezmesi" repeated twice. "Hamuruna ... yoğrulur" is a slightly odd construction ("hamura ... yoğrulur" would be more natural, as "hamurun içine yoğrulur").

32. Hurma ekmeği: "Böylece iyi ekmeğin lezzeti hurmanın tatlılığıyla buluşur." Foreign. "iyi ekmeğin lezzeti" is empty, and "buluşur" is brochure language. "Böylece" suggests çörek otu is the reason, which does not follow.

33. Hurma ekmeği: "“Kırmızı ekmek” olarak da bilinir." Hanging. This raises the question of why it is red, which the page never answers. The text and the photo also disagree: the caption says "sacda" and "kil fırının önünde", but the text names neither. The text also does not mention "kahve ibriği" in the photo.

34. Hurma ekmeği: "Arap kahvesiyle sunulur." Mild. "Arap kahvesi" works for Turkish readers, but the coffee pot in the photo caption is not mentioned elsewhere. Not an error.

35. "Bu sayfa hakkında: Buradaki açıklamalar geleneksel yemekleri ve bu yemeklerin bilinen malzemelerini tanıtır; kesin tarif vermez." Foreign / bureaucratic. Disclaimer language ("tanıtır", "kesin tarif vermez") sounds official. "bilinen malzemelerini" is vague: known by whom?

36. "Ölçüler ve adlar evden eve, bölgeden bölgeye değişir." Fine, but "adlar" is ambiguous. Dish names? Ingredient names? Combined with the earlier "ceriş" and "Kırmızı ekmek", it is plausible, but not clear.

37. "Bu sayfadaki yemekler El-Ahsa'daki restoranlarda yıl boyu sunulur." Contradicts / overclaims. All eight dishes, including a "kış tatlısı" (aside) and Ramazan dishes, "yıl boyu" in restaurants? The next sentence tries to explain, but a reader planning a trip will take this as a promise. No restaurant is named here.

38. "Dört kartta bir vesile ya da mevsim anılır: ramazan, kış geceleri, düğün ziyafetleri, misafir sofraları." Stumbles and register. "vesile" is Ottoman-bookish; "bir vesile ya da mevsim anılır" is an impersonal passive that does not say which four cards. I counted: pilav, haris, aside, sagu, which does match four. The reader should not have to count. "kutlamalar" (pilav card) is not in this list, while "misafir sofraları" is.

39. "Bu dört kart yemeğin El-Ahsa sofrasındaki geleneksel yerini anlatır; yemeğin ziyaretçiye ne zaman sunulduğunu değil." Stumbles. Long and abstract; "ziyaretçiye ne zaman sunulduğunu" is stiff. The sentence contradicts the "yıl boyu" claim in the sentence before only on the surface, so the pair reads as hedging.

40. "[link] Tadına bakacak bir yer mi arıyorsunuz? Restoranlar ve kafeler listesine göz atın →". Mostly fine. "Tadına bakacak bir yer" is slightly foreign (more natural: "Tatmak için bir yer"). The register shifts to a direct "arıyorsunuz" after an impersonal page.

41. Place name "El-Ahsa" (throughout). Misreads on first pass. A Turkish reader may know it as "Ahsa" or "Hasa". The hyphenated "El-Ahsa'nın / El-Ahsa'da" with apostrophe suffixes is acceptable. The page never says where El-Ahsa is (country or region) and who might want to go. This is missing, not wrong.

42. Toggle: "Devamını okuyun / Devamını gizleyin". Fine to read. "Devamını gizleyin" is slightly stiff; shorter would work. Minor.
