# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r1

## Page 1 - food (/tr/food/)

1. Browser tab "El-Ahsa mutfağı — Visit Al-Ahsa": the first half is Turkish and the second half is English, with a different spelling of the place name ("Al-Ahsa" vs "El-Ahsa"). I took it for a brand tail, but the two spellings side by side make me wonder whether they are two different places. Nothing to add; just noting the mismatch.

2. Search description "El-Ahsa mutfağında bir gezinti: El-Ahsa pilavı, mandi, haris, mufallak, hurma pekmezli balalit...": it is a string of eight dish names I do not know, with no hint of what any of them is. "haris" on first pass reads as the old Turkish word "haris" (greedy, covetous), not a dish. "balalit" and "mufallak" could not be placed at all. As a snippet it promises nothing I could picture.

3. "El-Ahsa" everywhere (title, headings, text): the hyphen makes my eye read "El" as the Turkish word "el" (hand; also "stranger/people"), and "Ahsa" has no Turkish anchor, so I was unsure how to say it (stress and the "h"). It is consistent across the page, but the first reading is slightly off.

4. "Bu malzemelerden sofralarda kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılan sıcak yemekler ortaya çıkar." I stumbled: "sofralarda ... aktarılan" (handed down at tables) is an odd place for "passed from generation to generation", and the participle phrase sits before "sıcak yemekler", so I had to reread to find the subject. Also, "sıcak yemekler" promises hot dishes, yet the next section includes desserts and a bread; that is not contradicted later but it is not covered either.

5. "hurma pekmezi (dibs), kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi) ve yarma buğday": "dibs" looks like an English word to me. In Turkish "misket limonu" is the fresh green lime, so "kurutulmuş misket limonu" made me picture a dried green lime; fine, but it is a new item for me. Later "ceriş" appears in brackets for the wheat, but this intro does not give it, so "yarma buğday = ceriş" is only joined in the mufallak card.

6. "Lezzet turu" (section label): "tour" suggests a route with stops, but what follows is a list of eight cards. Sounds like a brochure heading rather than a content label. "Vahanın kalbinden sekiz yemek" right after it is fine, and the eight count matches the cards.

7. Pilav: "rengi kızılımsı kahverengiye döner, lezzeti zenginleşir." The subject is not stated; I assumed the rice, and "lezzeti" could be the whole dish. The two clauses are joined with a comma and no connective, which reads a bit like a list rather than a sentence. "Kutlamalarda ve misafir sofralarında sunulur." is fine and clear.

8. Pilav: "El-Ahsa pilavı" is named after the rice ("El-Ahsa pirinci"), but the card does not say whether the rice is special, so the name looks circular: El-Ahsa rice with ... = El-Ahsa pilaf. I was left wondering what makes it different from any meat-and-rice dish. Information missing, not to be filled.

9. Mandi: "El-Ahsa'da mandi pişirilmekten ziyade, sırlar gibi toprağa emanet edilir." Sounds foreign and literary: "sırlar gibi toprağa emanet edilir" (entrusted to the earth like secrets) is a calque-feeling poetic turn. "pişirilmekten ziyade" is bookish. I had to read twice to see that it is about cooking in a pit.

10. Mandi: "Daha açık söylemek gerekirse, mandi yerdeki bir çukura gömülmüş tandırda pişer." The "to put it plainly" opener is bureaucratic/explanatory; it makes the previous sentence feel like it needed apology. Suggest simply starting with the plain statement.

11. Mandi: "Et saatlerce kil tandırın soluğunu içine çeker, pirinç ise hurma ağacının ve dumanın kokusunu taşır." "Tandırın soluğunu içine çeker" (the meat draws in the breath of the tandır) is purple and personifying; no Turkish cook would say it. "tandır" itself is a known word, but a clay tandır in a pit is not what a Turkish reader pictures first (they picture the Anatolian tandır), so the image is fine but the phrasing is not.

12. Mandi: "Bu yemek, cömertliğin adını koymadan önce de onu bilen bir vahanın hikâyesini anlatır." Hard to parse: "adını koymadan önce de onu bilen" is a long relative chain and I lost the thread before "vahanın". It means something like "an oasis that knew generosity before it had a name for it", but it reads abstract and slightly pretentious.

13. Haris (heading and text): the word reads as the Turkish word "haris" (greedy/avid), and the card's first sentence does not rescue it because it starts with "Buğday ve et...". Only the page context says it is a dish. First-pass misread.

14. Haris: "Buğday ve et saatlerce birbirine karışıp tek bir ruh olunca haris doğar." "tek bir ruh olunca ... doğar" is poetic and register-shifts upward from the surrounding explanatory tone; also "doğar" (is born) for a stew. Understandable, but sounds like ad copy.

15. Haris: "cömertliğin en önde gelen yemeği" and "ramazan sofralarının da eski bir dostudur". "En önde gelen" is stiff and "eski bir dost" personifies a dish; the second is oddly casual next to the first. The sentence also says haris is the foremost dish of generosity at feasts and weddings, which I cannot picture as a claim; it is vague about what you would see or eat.

16. Repetition: "saatlerce" and "cömertlik" appear in both the mandi and haris cards, and "vahanın ..." begins many sentences. Reading the page in order, the cooking-for-hours and generosity themes feel recycled.

17. Mufallak: "Yarma buğdaydan (ceriş) yapılan geleneksel bir El-Ahsa yemeğidir." "(ceriş)" in brackets right after the wheat: I could not tell if ceriş is the name of the wheat or the dish, and I do not know how to read it ("ceriş" vs "cereş"). The photo caption calls the dish "hurmalı yarma buğday", but the text says dates are only a garnish.

18. Mufallak: "Vahanın lezzetlerini bir araya getiren doyurucu bir tabaktır." Generic and bureaucratic-brochure; it tells me nothing I can act on except "filling".

19. Balalit: "tel şehriye, El-Ahsa'daki gibi hurma pekmeziyle tatlandırılır." "El-Ahsa'daki gibi" on a page about El-Ahsa food sounds foreign, as if comparing with some other place's habit; "as it is done in El-Ahsa" while we already are in El-Ahsa.

20. Balalit: "Hurma ağacının başkenti olan bir toprakta tatlılık hurmanın kendisinden gelir." "Hurma ağacının başkenti" is a calque-sounding claim and "toprakta" is slightly bookish. Also a small contradiction: the sentence says sweetness comes from the date itself, while the previous sentence says the vermicelli is sweetened with date molasses (pekmez); close enough, but I paused.

21. Balalit: "Balalit omletle birlikte sunulur." The dish is classified as "Tatlı" and just described as sweet with saffron, cardamom and molasses; then it comes with an omelette. Turkish reader: sweet vermicelli with omelette is surprising, and I cannot tell whether the omelette is sweet, savoury, or on top. The photo caption ("balalit için tel şehriye" next to an omelette pan) is awkward too: "balalit için tel şehriye" reads as raw vermicelli, not the finished dish. Something is missing and I will not guess.

22. Aside: "El-Ahsa'nın kendi hurması hafif ateşte buğday unuyla birlikte erir." "Kendi hurması" (El-Ahsa's own date) is odd, and "erir" for dates plus flour made me picture melting rather than cooking; I read it twice. "Buğday unuyla birlikte erir" is not how the cooking of a flour mixture is usually said.

23. Aside: "Sonra sadeyağ ve bir tutam karabiberle taçlandırılır: sadeyağ asideye parlaklık verir, karabiber de sıcaklığını alevlendirir." "Taçlandırılır" (crowned) and "alevlendirir" (ignites) are brochure-loud. "sıcaklığını" is ambiguous: the pepper's heat, or the dish's warmth? "karabiber" parsed as black pepper without trouble.

24. Aside: "Kaşıkla yenen yumuşak bir bulamaç olan aside". "Bulamaç" is a real but unappetising word (porridge/mush, also used for mud or a mess); I would not choose it to sell a dessert. And "aside" itself reads as the English word "aside" on the page, so the first mention in running text read like a stray English word.

25. Aside / "Bu sayfa hakkında" tension: the aside card says it is "El-Ahsa'nın kış tatlısı", while the end note says the page's dishes are served in restaurants year-round. Not strictly contradictory, but I wondered whether I could eat it in summer. The page does not say.

26. Sagu: "Ateşte eriyerek safran ve kakuleyle ışıldayan saydam bir mücevhere dönüşür." The pearls "melt in the fire" yet were "cooked in water" in the previous sentence; "mücevhere dönüşür" is ornate. I also never learn what sagu is (my guess: tapioca-like pearls) because "sagu taneleri" is just named; the dish name is not placed for a reader who does not know it.

27. Sagu: "Dokusu narin, geçmişi köklüdür; hazırlanışı vaha halkı arasında elden ele aktarılır." "Geçmişi köklüdür" is stock praise with no content; "elden ele aktarılır" is unusual for a recipe (usually "kuşaktan kuşağa", which the intro already used). "Baş köşesindedir" is fine idiomatically, but a dessert occupying the head of a table is a stretch.

28. Hurma ekmeği: "Hamuru hurma ezmesiyle yoğrulur ya da içi bununla doldurulur; üzerine çörek otu serpilir." "Bununla" is clumsy; "içi" (its inside) made me think of a bread with a hollow interior and I had to reread. The photo caption mentions "sac" and a clay oven but the text does not, so I could not tell how it is baked.

29. Hurma ekmeği: "Bu ekmek, iyi bir ekmeğin lezzetini hurmanın tatlılığıyla birleştirir." Tautological and empty: "a good bread's flavour with the sweetness of dates". Sounds translated.

30. Hurma ekmeği: "“Kırmızı ekmek” olarak da bilinir." Left hanging: nothing in the card suggests anything red (dates and nigella seeds), so the name raises a question the page does not answer.

31. "Bu sayfa hakkında": "geleneksel yemekleri ve yaygın bilinen malzemelerini tanıtır." Bureaucratic; the object phrase "yemekleri ve ... malzemelerini" is a coordinate with a shared verb that I had to reread. "yaygın bilinen" is a bit off; "bilinen" or "yaygın" alone would be more natural.

32. "Dört kartta bir özel gün ya da mevsim anılır: ramazan, kış geceleri, düğün ziyafetleri, misafir sofraları." I counted: haris names two (weddings, Ramadan), sagu names Ramadan, aside names winter nights, pilav names celebrations and guest tables. So "four cards" and "four occasions" do not map one-to-one for me, and "misafir sofraları" is not a special day or a season. The sentence promises a precise count the cards do not clearly match.

33. "Bu dört kart yemeğin El-Ahsa sofrasındaki geleneksel yerini anlatır; ziyaretçiye ne zaman sunulduğunu değil." The closing clause is foreign: "ziyaretçiye ... sunulduğunu" (to the visitor) shifts to the visitor while the subject is the dish's place at the local table. It reads as a legal disclaimer, an odd register after the warm card texts.

34. Toggle "Devamını okuyun / Devamını gizleyin": the closed label is fine, but "Devamını gizleyin" (hide the rest) is mechanical; the open state I would expect something like "Daha az göster". Also the label "read the rest" on cards that are already only two to five sentences felt slightly grand.

35. Photo captions vs text: the sagu photo mentions walnuts and pistachios ("ceviz ve Antep fıstığı") that the text never lists, and the mufallak photo says "hurmalı yarma buğday" while the text says dates are added as a garnish. Small mismatches between picture description and card text; nothing to add, only noting they do not line up.
