# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r3

## Page 1 - food (/tr/food/)

1. Browser tab "El-Ahsa mutfağı — Visit Al-Ahsa": the page says "El-Ahsa" but the tail says "Al-Ahsa". Two spellings of the same place in one line. I did not know if they were the same place until I saw the rest of the page.

2. Search description "El-Ahsa mutfağında bir gezinti: El-Ahsa pilavı, mandi, haris, mufallak, ...": a bare list of names. I could place pilav, aside and ekmek. I could not place haris, mufallak, balalit or sagu. "Haris" first read as the Turkish word "haris" (greedy). Nothing in the snippet says these are dishes, though the "mutfağı" frame helps a little.

3. "El-Ahsa mutfağı vahanın bereketine dayanır": the page never says where El-Ahsa is (country or region). Everything is "vaha" and "vahanın". A reader who does not already know the place has no anchor. This is missing, not a wording fault.

4. "Bu malzemelerden sıcak yemekler ortaya çıkar; bu yemekler sofralarda kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılır.": reads as filler and as translated ("ortaya çıkar"). It also promises "sıcak yemekler", but half of the eight cards are desserts and bread.

5. "hurma pekmezi (dibs), kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi)": the bracketed names are never used again. The "lomi" in the brackets is dropped at once, and "kurutulmuş misket limonu" is repeated in full on the cards. I did not know why the names were given. "Dibs" reads like an English word.

6. "yarma buğday": the intro uses it, then "buğday" appears alone on the haris card and "(ceriş)" on the mufallak card. I could not tell whether these are the same wheat.

7. "Lezzet turu / Vahanın kalbinden sekiz yemek": "Lezzet turu" suggests a guided tour you book, which this is not. The brochure tone is stronger than the intro above it. "Vahanın sofrasından / ... / Vahanın kalbinden" repeats "vaha" three times in four lines.

8. "[photo] ... kil tencerede karıştırılan El-Ahsa pilavı": no problem.

9. El-Ahsa pilavı: "rengi kızılımsı kahverengiye döner, lezzeti zenginleşir." I had to look back to see what "rengi" and "lezzeti" refer to (the rice, after the long list of ingredients). The sentence before it is a long compound with a parenthesis, so the subject is far away.

10. El-Ahsa pilavı: "Kutlamalarda ve misafir sofralarında sunulur." Fine alone. But it is one of the occasion mentions that the closing note counts (see item 33).

11. Mandi: "El-Ahsa'da mandi pişirilmekten ziyade, sırlar gibi toprağa emanet edilir." Two problems. First, "toprağa emanet etmek" reads to me as burial or sowing, so it sounds solemn and a little ominous for food. Second, the next sentence says it is cooked in a pit, so "pişirilmekten ziyade" contradicts it.

12. Mandi: "Kısacası, mandi yerdeki bir çukura gömülmüş tandırda pişer." "Kısacası" signals a summary, but this is the first plain statement of what mandi is. "Yerdeki bir çukura gömülmüş tandır" is also heavy. "Çukura gömülmüş" is hard to picture because a Turkish tandır is already a pit.

13. Mandi: "Et saatlerce kil tandırın soluğunu içine çeker, pirinç ise hurma ağacının ve dumanın kokusunu taşır." "Tandırın soluğunu içine çeker" sounds foreign. "Hurma ağacının kokusu" is unclear. I do not know whether wood from the palm is burned or the palm is only a figure.

14. Mandi: "o vaha, cömertliğin adını koymadan önce de cömertliği bilirdi." I read it three times and could not decide what it promises. It is a riddle in the middle of a food description, and it does not match the rest of the page.

15. Mandi: the card never says what the meat is, or that rice is served with it. The title is just "Mandi". I was left with "a tandır dish".

16. Haris: "Buğday ve et saatlerce birbirine karışıp tek bir ruh olunca haris adlı yemek doğar." On first reading "haris" is the Turkish word for greedy or covetous, so "haris adlı yemek" sounded odd. The chain "karışıp ... olunca ... doğar" and "tek bir ruh" are literary and oversold. The sentence only says that wheat and meat cook together for hours.

17. Haris: "cömertliğin en önde gelen yemeğidir": "en önde gelen yemek" is foreign. A dish does not "lead" generosity in Turkish. "Ziyafetlerinde ve düğünlerinde" is fine.

18. Haris: "ramazan sofralarının da eski bir dostudur." Personification, and the tone shifts to warm and chatty after the solemn "ruh" line. It is not wrong, but it is brochure-ish and mixed in register.

19. Mufallak: "Yarma buğdaydan (ceriş) yapılan geleneksel bir El-Ahsa yemeğidir." "Ceriş" is not a word I know. It is unclear whether it is the wheat or the dish. It is also the only card where the tab-style gloss is bracketed in the middle of a participle phrase.

20. Mufallak: "Vahanın lezzetlerini bir araya getiren doyurucu bir tabaktır." A generic closing line that says nothing about what mufallak actually is. "Mufallak" itself I would pronounce as "müfellek" or "mufallak", with no guide.

21. Mufallak photo caption: "mufallak: hurmalı yarma buğday." The text says dates and boiled egg are only a garnish, so "hurmalı" here reads as a different dish name. A small mismatch.

22. Balalit: the name starts with "bal", so on first pass I expected honey, and the card has none. "Hurma pekmezli balalit" in the title and no honey in the text is a first-pass misread.

23. Balalit: "hurma pekmeziyle de El-Ahsa usulünce tatlandırılır." "El-Ahsa usulünce" is left hanging. What is the El-Ahsa way? The text never says. Also "de" here sounds off.

24. Balalit: "Hurma ağacının başkenti olan bir toprakta tatlılık hurmanın kendisinden gelir." "Hurma ağacının başkenti" is an odd capital-city image. It also sounds like a slogan, and "tatlılık hurmanın kendisinden gelir" does not fit with the earlier sentence that already adds pekmez.

25. Balalit: "Balalit omletle birlikte sunulur." This is a surprise that is not explained. A sweet vermicelli with an omelette, served how: on top, beside, mixed? Nothing says. The photo mentions an omelette pan. The sentence stands alone at the end like an afterthought.

26. Hurmalı aside: "kaşıkla yenen yumuşak bir bulamaçtır." "Bulamaç" suggests gruel or paste and is not appetising for a dessert. It is understandable but the register is off.

27. Hurmalı aside: "El-Ahsa'nın kendi hurması hafif ateşte buğday ununa karışıp erir." "Kendi hurması" is foreign. The dates "mix and melt" by themselves, which is a strange subject. Also "El-Ahsa'nın" repeats in the sentence before it.

28. Hurmalı aside: "sadeyağ tatlıya parlaklık verir, karabiber de onun sıcaklığını alevlendirir." "Onun" is ambiguous (the dessert's or the ghee's?). "Sıcaklığını alevlendirir" and "taçlandırılır" are over-literary. "Karabiber" in a dessert is a surprise but fine as stated. It does not trip as "black" because it is the ordinary word.

29. Hurmalı aside: "Soğuk gecelerde aile bu tatlının etrafında toplanır." A general claim, written as if in a family album. "Aile" without possessive sounds like the translator's. Consistent with "kış tatlısı", but see item 33.

30. Sagu: "Küçük sagu taneleri suda pişirilir ve şekerle tatlandırılır." The card never says what sagu is. I know "sago" in Turkish, and "sagu" looks like a typo of it. The text is missing a basic what-is-it line.

31. Sagu: "Taneler ateşte eriyerek safran ve kakuleyle ışıldayan saydam bir mücevhere dönüşür." Saffron and cardamom appear for the first time here, though the sentence before listed only water and sugar. "Eriyerek" plus "mücevhere dönüşür" is florid and does not match the plain first sentence.

32. Sagu: "Dokusu narin, geçmişi köklüdür; hazırlanışı vaha halkı arasında elden ele aktarılır." "Elden ele aktarılır" mixes the idioms "elden ele geçmek" and "aktarılmak". "Geçmişi köklüdür" is generic. The "ramazan sofrası" mention before it repeats the haris card.

33. Hurma ekmeği: "Yerel bir ekmektir." An abrupt start with no subject. "Yerel" to what? It also does not say the name of the bread. The next sentence "Bu ekmekte iyi ekmeğin lezzeti hurmanın tatlılığıyla birleşir." is circular ("iyi ekmeğin lezzeti") and sounds translated.

34. Hurma ekmeği: "“Kırmızı ekmek” olarak da bilinir." Nothing says why red, and in Turkish "kırmızı ekmek" does not suggest date bread. Left hanging.

35. Hurma ekmeği photo caption: "sacda" (on a sheet griddle), while the same caption speaks of a clay oven and the text mentions neither saç nor fırın. Small mismatch.

36. "Bu sayfa hakkında": "geleneksel yemekleri ve yaygın olarak bilinen malzemeleri tanıtır" is bureaucratic ("tanıtır", "yaygın olarak bilinen"). It is fine in meaning, but the register is the flattest on the page.

37. "Dört kartta bir özel gün ya da mevsim anılır: ramazan, kış geceleri, düğün ziyafetleri, misafir sofraları." The list names four occasions, but the cards do not map one to one. Haris alone names ziyafet, düğün and ramazan; the pilav card says "kutlamalar" and "misafir sofraları". So "dört kartta bir ... anılır" does not match what I read. I counted four cards (pilav, haris, aside, sagu) after going back.

38. "Bu dört kart yemeklerin ziyaretçilere ne zaman sunulduğunu değil, El-Ahsa sofrasındaki geleneksel yerini anlatır." A long, heavy sentence, bureaucratic. It also sits next to "yıl boyu sunulur" and the card "kış tatlısı". I understood the intent only on the second read.

39. "Bu sayfadaki yemekler El-Ahsa'daki restoranlarda yıl boyu sunulur." An availability promise for all eight dishes at the end of the page, after the cards. It is a claim I cannot check, and it sits oddly against the "ramazan" and "kış" cards. Whether this is meant for visitors or not is unclear.

40. Link: "Tadına bakabileceğiniz bir yer mi arıyorsunuz? Restoranlar ve kafeler listesine göz atın →": fine, natural. "Göz atın" is the most casual register on the page.

41. Toggle "Devamını okuyun / Devamını gizleyin": "Devamını gizleyin" is not natural Turkish for closing a card. I would expect "Daha az göster" or "Kapat". "Devamını okuyun" is fine.

42. Whole page: nearly every sentence is passive or impersonal ("pişirilir, tatlandırılır, sunulur, aktarılır"), plus constant "vaha" and "El-Ahsa'nın" openings. It reads like a translated brochure. The tone swings between plain information and poetic lines (mandi, haris, sagu), so the register is not even.
