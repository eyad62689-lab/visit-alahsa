# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r2

## Page 1 - food (/tr/food/)

1. Browser tab: "El-Ahsa mutfağı — Visit Al-Ahsa". Misreads/inconsistent: the same tab spells the place two ways ("El-Ahsa" and "Al-Ahsa"). I wondered for a second whether these were two different places. Also, "El" is an ordinary Turkish word (hand), and the hyphen makes it read oddly at first, especially next to "elden ele" further down. The English brand tail in a Turkish tab is a small foreign note, but probably intended.

2. Search description: "El-Ahsa mutfağında bir gezinti: El-Ahsa pilavı, mandi, haris, mufallak, hurma pekmezli balalit, hurmalı aside, sagu ve hurma ekmeği." Stumbles lightly: the name "El-Ahsa" occurs three times in one short line. Most of the dish names are unknown to me, so as a snippet it promises "a stroll" but I cannot tell what the dishes are. Nothing here says where El-Ahsa is (country or region); that is missing, and I never find it anywhere on the page.

3. "El-Ahsa mutfağı vahanın bereketine dayanır: El-Ahsa pirinci, hurma pekmezi (dibs), kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi) ve yarma buğday." Sounds slightly bookish ("bereket") but acceptable. Misread: "dibs" looks like an English word or a typo of "dips/dip" (bottom); I did not know it was an Arabic name until I saw the brackets. "lomi" is a bracketed name I cannot place.

4. Same sentence, follow-up: the bracketed names "dibs" and "lomi" are given once and never used again. Later cards say "hurma pekmezi" and "kurutulmuş misket limonu" in full (three times), so I was left wondering whether "lomi" is a different thing from the "kurutulmuş misket limonu" in the pilav and mufallak cards.

5. "Bu malzemelerden sıcak yemekler ortaya çıkar; bu yemekler kuşaktan kuşağa sofradan sofraya aktarılır." Unclear/contradiction: "sıcak yemekler" (hot dishes) does not fit a page whose cards include three desserts and a bread. The double "bu ... bu ..." and the double "-dan ... -a" pairs ("kuşaktan kuşağa sofradan sofraya") feel stacked and formulaic. "Aktarılır" will come back twice more (sagu card), so the idea is used up by the third time.

6. "Vahanın kalbinden sekiz yemek". I counted: pilav, mandi, haris, mufallak, balalit, aside, sagu, hurma ekmeği = eight, so the count holds. Mild unclear point: "yemek" for a section that includes bread and three sweets; acceptable but a touch loose.

7. Pilav card, "Kutlamalarda ve misafir sofralarında sunulur." Fine to read. Small note: "Kutlamalar" is a general occasion that is not on the later list of the "four" occasions (see item 31), so the count there is hard to reconcile.

8. Pilav card, "rengi kızılımsı kahverengiye döner, lezzeti zenginleşir." Slight stumble: "rengi" has no explicit subject after the long preceding sentence; I assumed the rice, not the sauce or the meat. Otherwise clear and natural.

9. Mandi card, "El-Ahsa'da mandi pişirilmekten ziyade, sırlar gibi toprağa emanet edilir." Register: suddenly poetic and brochure-like, after a plain pilav card. "Pişirilmekten ziyade ... emanet edilir" took me two reads; I understood it as "not cooked but entrusted to the earth like a secret", which is a flourish, not information.

10. "Yalın söylersek, mandi yerdeki bir çukura gömülmüş tandırda pişer." Sounds foreign: "Yalın söylersek" is a calque; a Turkish writer would say "Kısacası" or just state it. The sentence also contradicts the rhythm of the first one (poetry, then "to put it plainly"), which feels like the text correcting itself. Also unclear to me whether the "tandır" is a clay oven in the ground that the food is lowered into: the Turkish "tandır" I know is a clay oven, so it is understandable, but "yerdeki bir çukura gömülmüş tandır" is heavy.

11. "Et saatlerce kil tandırın soluğunu içine çeker, pirinç ise hurma ağacının ve dumanın kokusunu taşır." Sounds foreign: "tandırın soluğunu içine çekmek" is a literal-sounding image; no Turkish writer would say a meat "inhales the breath of the oven". "Hurma ağacının kokusunu taşır" is also odd: I do not know whether the wood of the date palm is the fuel, or whether the rice smells like the tree. No fuel is mentioned elsewhere in the text (only the photo says "odun ateşi" for other dishes), so I cannot resolve it.

12. "Bu yemek bir vahanın hikâyesini anlatır: o vaha, cömertliğin adını koymadan önce de cömertliği bilirdi." Left hanging / unclear: I read it three times. "Cömertliğin adını koymadan önce" has no clear subject action (who names generosity?). It reads like a translated aphorism; it tells me nothing I would do or taste. It also introduces "bir vahanın ... o vaha" instead of "El-Ahsa", so it could be any oasis. Register: brochure-poetic.

13. Mandi card in general: it never says what the meat or the rice are, only atmosphere. (I am not asking you to add; I am reporting that the card gives me no picture of the dish after three poetic sentences.)

14. Haris card, title "Haris". Misread on first pass: in Turkish "haris" is an existing, somewhat old-fashioned word meaning greedy/avid. I read the card title as the adjective until the body called it a dish. The first sentence ("... haris adlı yemek doğar") rescues it only at the very end of the sentence.

15. "Buğday ve et saatlerce birbirine karışıp tek bir ruh olunca haris adlı yemek doğar." Stumble: the subordinate clause is long and ends in "ruh olunca", which I took as spiritual language; the verb "doğar" arrives only at the end. Register: mystical/brochure ("tek bir ruh"). The sentence also names the dish only at the very end, after the card title already gave it.

16. "El-Ahsa'nın ziyafetlerinde ve düğünlerinde cömertliğin en önde gelen yemeğidir; ramazan sofralarının da eski bir dostudur." "Cömertliğin en önde gelen yemeği" is abstract and heavy; "eski bir dost" is personification again. Also "cömertlik" has now appeared in both the mandi and the haris cards, so it begins to feel like a recurring slogan. Orthography: "ramazan" is in lower case here and throughout; I would expect "Ramazan" as a month name.

17. Mufallak card, "Yarma buğdaydan (ceriş) yapılan geleneksel bir El-Ahsa yemeğidir." "ceriş" in brackets is an unfamiliar word; I cannot tell if it is the Arabic name of the cracked wheat. It is not used again. "mufallak" itself I would pronounce "mu-fal-lak" without knowing the stress or whether the double "l" is long; no help is given.

18. "Buğday baharatlarla ve kurutulmuş misket limonuyla pişirilir, sonra hurma ve haşlanmış yumurtayla süslenir." Clear. Small surprise: dates and boiled egg together; the text does not say that this is sweet-savory. The photo caption calls it "hurmalı yarma buğday", but the card never says it is sweet at all. I was left unsure whether this is a sweet or a savoury main (it is labelled "Ana yemek").

19. "Vahanın lezzetlerini bir araya getiren doyurucu bir tabaktır." Generic filler; "bir araya getiren" is vague. It tells me nothing I did not already know.

20. Balalit card, title "Hurma pekmezli balalit". Misread: "bal-" at the start reads as "honey" (bal) in Turkish, and "balalit" in my head split into "bal" + "alit". I expected a honey dish; the text says date molasses. Also the title is long and carries "hurma pekmezli" like a recipe name, while the description repeats "hurma pekmeziyle".

21. "Safran ve kakuleyle kokulandırılan altın sarısı tel şehriye hurma pekmeziyle El-Ahsa usulünce tatlandırılır." Stumbles: a long chain (participial "kokulandırılan altın sarısı tel şehriye" then an instrumental "hurma pekmeziyle" and "El-Ahsa usulünce") before the verb arrives at the very end; I lost the subject. "Tel şehriye" is a clear Turkish word and is fine. The "altın sarısı" comes right after "safran" so I could not tell what is golden: the vermicelli or the saffron.

22. "Hurma ağacının başkenti olan bir toprakta tatlılık hurmanın kendisinden gelir." Sounds foreign and brochure-like: "hurma ağacının başkenti" is an odd metaphor in Turkish. Also contradictory to the previous sentence: the sweetness is said to come from the date itself, but the previous sentence says it is sweetened with date molasses (pekmez), so "kendisinden" over-promises.

23. "Balalit omletle birlikte sunulur." Left hanging: a sweet dish served with an omelette is surprising and the text drops it in a final four-word sentence without any hint of how it tastes together. Also the category says "Tatlı" (dessert), so I did not know if I would eat this at the end of a meal or for breakfast.

24. Aside card, title "Hurmalı aside". Misread: "aside" looks like the English word (aside = "kenara"), and the suffix forms later ("asideye", "asidenin") look like "asi" (rebel) + "-de" or like "asit" (acid). I stumbled on "asidenin sıcaklığını". The dish's name is not glossed until the fifth sentence.

25. "El-Ahsa'nın kendi hurması hafif ateşte buğday ununa karışıp erir." Stumble: dates do not "melt" into flour in my head; "erir" and "karışıp" together are unclear (the dates dissolve? the flour melts?). Also "kendi hurması" (its own dates) is a slightly odd emphasis.

26. "Sonra sadeyağ ve bir tutam karabiberle taçlandırılır: sadeyağ asideye parlaklık verir, karabiber de asidenin sıcaklığını alevlendirir." Register: "taçlandırılır" and "alevlendirir" are shouty brochure verbs. "Karabiber" is fine, but the "kara-" is not black as in a name here, it is the pepper, so no problem. "Asidenin sıcaklığı" is ambiguous: temperature or spiciness? The same colon-and-semicolon structure explains each ingredient twice.

27. "Kaşıkla yenen yumuşak bir bulamaç olan aside El-Ahsa'nın kış tatlısıdır." The definition arrives after four sentences of preparation, so I was reading about a dish I could not picture. Also stumble: the subject is a long relative chain, "Kaşıkla yenen yumuşak bir bulamaç olan aside ..." which I read twice.

28. "Soğuk gecelerde aile bu tatlının etrafında toplanır." A bit sentimental and abstract; "aile" without "ailece" or "ailenin" feels bare. It also adds a second occasion (cold nights) beyond "kış" in the previous sentence.

29. Sagu card, "Küçük sagu taneleri suda pişirilir ve şekerle tatlandırılır. Ateşte eriyerek safran ve kakuleyle ışıldayan saydam bir mücevhere dönüşür." Stumble/unclear: the second sentence has no explicit subject and I could not tell if the "taneler" melt (eriyerek) or become transparent; "eriyerek ... dönüşür" is contradictory for pearls. "Safran ve kakuleyle ışıldayan" suggests the spices make it glow. "Saydam bir mücevher" is florid and off for a page that otherwise lists plain ingredients. I also do not know sagu, and the page does not say what the grains are.

30. "El-Ahsa'nın bu tatlısı ramazan sofralarının baş köşesindedir. Dokusu narin, geçmişi köklüdür; hazırlanışı vaha halkı arasında elden ele aktarılır." "Baş köşesindedir" is a natural idiom. "Dokusu narin, geçmişi köklüdür" is brochure-style. "Elden ele aktarılır" repeats the "aktarılır" of the intro; with "kuşaktan kuşağa" there, this is the second inheritance claim in the page. Also: the photo caption mentions walnut and pistachio ("Ceviz ve Antep fıstığıyla süslenmiş sagu") but the description never mentions nuts; I am only noting the mismatch.

31. Hurma ekmeği card, "Yerel bir ekmektir." Very bare after the long poetic cards; register jumps. "Yerel" is unspecified (local to where?).

32. "Hamuru ya hurma ezmesiyle yoğrulur ya da ezmeyle doldurulur; üzerine çörek otu serpilir." Misread: "ezme" in Turkish usually means a spicy vegetable paste/meze, so "hurma ezmesi" gave me a moment's pause; I understood it as date paste only after "yoğrulur". Otherwise clear.

33. "Bu ekmekte iyi ekmeğin lezzeti hurmanın tatlılığıyla birleşir." Sounds foreign/tautological: "iyi ekmeğin lezzeti" is empty (what is the taste of good bread?). Then "Arap kahvesiyle sunulur" is clear.

34. "“Kırmızı ekmek” olarak da bilinir." Left hanging: why red? Nothing in the card suggests red (the pilav card has "kızılımsı kahverengi" but that is a different dish). I wondered whether this is the same bread or another. Also it appears as the last line, so the reader is not told which of the names is the main one.

35. Section "Bu sayfa hakkında", "Buradaki açıklamalar, kesin tarif vermek yerine geleneksel yemekleri ve yaygın olarak bilinen malzemelerini tanıtır." Register: suddenly bureaucratic/disclaimer after poetic cards. "Malzemelerini" refers back to the dishes, which was a small stumble. Also I noticed no measures at all in the page, so "Ölçüler ... değişir" warns about something I never saw.

36. "Ölçüler ve adlar evden eve, bölgeden bölgeye değişir." Clear, but "adlar" (names) varying is a surprising claim and I would want to know which names; the page does not say. Small.

37. "Bu sayfadaki yemekler El-Ahsa'daki restoranlarda yıl boyu sunulur." A flat factual promise: all eight dishes all year in restaurants. It sits oddly against the cards that tie dishes to ramazan, winter nights and weddings, and the next sentence has to explain that away.

38. "Dört kartta bir özel gün ya da mevsim anılır: ramazan, kış geceleri, düğün ziyafetleri, misafir sofraları. Bu dört kart yemeğin ziyaretçilere ne zaman sunulduğunu değil, El-Ahsa sofrasındaki geleneksel yerini anlatır." Stumbles and contradicts: I counted four cards that name an occasion (pilav, haris, aside, sagu), but the haris card alone names three (ziyafet, düğün, ramazan), and "misafir sofraları" is not a "special day or season". "Ramazan" is in two cards. "Yemeğin" (singular) refers to all dishes. The second sentence needs two reads; the clause "yemeğin ziyaretçilere ne zaman sunulduğunu değil" is a negative explanation of something I had not asked.

39. Link: "Tadına bakabileceğiniz bir yer mi arıyorsunuz? Restoranlar ve kafeler listesine göz atın →". Natural and clear; slightly more casual than the cards but it is a call to action, so acceptable. "Tadına bakabileceğiniz" is a mouthful, but understandable.

40. Toggle: "Devamını okuyun / Devamını gizleyin". Fine and idiomatic. Small point: "Devamını okuyun" suggests I will go to another page; here it opens in place. Not a real problem.

41. Overall register: the first half of each card is often poetic or brochure-loud ("sırlar", "ruh", "mücevher", "taçlandırılır", "alevlendirir", "eski bir dost") while the closing note and the bread card are dry. Together with the recurring "cömertlik" and "aktarılır", the page sounds as if written by two hands.

42. Overall: I never learn where El-Ahsa is, who "vaha halkı" are, or what time of year I would go; for a planner this is missing. (Reported as missing, not filled in.)
