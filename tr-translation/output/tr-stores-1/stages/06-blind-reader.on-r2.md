# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r2

## Page 1 - Al-Ahsa online stores (/tr/online-stores/)

1. Heading: "Yerel ürün satan El-Ahsa çevrim içi mağazaları rehberi". Five nouns and a participle stacked in a row; I had to read it twice to see that "rehberi" is the head noun. "El-Ahsa'daki yerel ürün satan çevrim içi mağazalar için rehber" would have gone down easier.

2. Intro: "hurma, el sanatları ve yerel ürünler." The promise is local products, but the third group later holds flowers, cosmetics, menswear and home decor. I expected dates and crafts and did not expect a flower shop, so the intro does not prepare me for it.

3. Search-engine description: "özgün El-Ahsa ürünleri satan ... El-Ahsa pirincine". "El-Ahsa" three times in one sentence, plus "özgün", reads like brochure stuffing.

4. Platform labels "Salla" and "Zid". "Salla" reads as the Turkish imperative "salla" (wave / shake), so "Platform: Salla" made me pause. Neither word is explained anywhere on the page, so I do not know what kind of thing a platform called Zid is.

5. Platform label "Salla (Mahally rehberi üzerinden)". I cannot tell what "Mahally rehberi" is or what "üzerinden" means for me. Do I buy through Mahally, or was the store merely found in it? It is not explained.

6. Counts and the word "mağaza". The filters say 39 stores (15 + 7 + 17 adds up). But several cards are Instagram accounts with a "Hesabı ziyaret edin" button, not stores. The count and the word "mağaza" do not quite fit them.

7. Eş-Şira Hurma: "Halas ve Raziz hurması, hurma poleni suyu ve Şaabiyat satar." "Halas" reads first as the Turkish word "halas" (deliverance). "hurma poleni suyu" left me with no idea what it is or what it is for. "Şaabiyat" is a word I could not place at all and it is not glossed. The sentence also lacks a subject at the start; the name sits on the line above.

8. "Şişi hurması". The first read was "şişi" (a knitting-needle or skewer word) or "şişe", not a date variety. Nothing says it is a variety.

9. Hecer Bahçeleri: "Ayrıca hurma pekmezi ve hurma tahini vardır." "hurma tahini" is unclear to me. I know tahini as sesame, and I cannot picture a date version. "Fabrikanın Instagram ve Snapchat hesapları bulunur" is fine, but "bulunur" and "vardır" alternate between cards for no visible reason.

10. Eş-Şark Hurma: "Hurma ve hurma ürünleri satar." Says nothing (a date factory sells dates). "Fabrikanın en çok öne çıkan ürünü hurma şekeridir." "en çok öne çıkan" is clumsy. "hurma şekeri" is ambiguous: sugar made from dates, or a date sweet?

11. Sifsif Mağazası: "X, Instagram ve Snapchat hesapları vardır." A lone "X" parses as a placeholder or letter, not a network. Also "Aile markasının ana ürünleri ... tatlılarıdır" opens with a brand that is never named in the text (the card title is "Mağazası"). Mild.

12. Sifsif: "hurma dolgulu hamur işi (maamul)". Glossed here, good. On later cards "maamul" appears bare ("hurmalı maamul", "maamul satar"); fine only if I read the cards in order, but the filters let me jump around.

13. El-Emin Hurma Fabrikası: "aside karışımları". "aside" is not glossed on this page. I read it as the English word "aside" and had no idea it is a food.

14. El-Emin: "preslenmiş hurma". Stumbled: pressed dates, as in paste blocks? Unclear what the product is.

15. El-Emin: "Kontrol edildiği sırada bazı ürünler stokta yoktu." Passive with no agent and no date. Checked by whom, when? It reads like a leaked editor's note, and the reader is left with an open question about whether it is still true. "1995'te kurulan bir fabrika. Fabrika ..." repeats "fabrika" back to back.

16. El-Abdüllatif: "hurma paketleme ve hurma alım hizmetleri sunan". "hurma alım hizmetleri": I cannot tell whether they buy dates from farmers or help me buy. Unclear. "Seçkin hurma" is also bookish; "seçkin" shows up on four cards (also Ariş, Et-Tayyar) and tires.

17. İbn Zeyd: "İbn Zeyd'in “Kulla” ve “Şanna” adlı iki El-Ahsa serisi". "iki El-Ahsa serisi" is left hanging: a series of what? "Halibi hurması" is a variety name I could not place. "Suudi Arabistan içine ve dışına gönderim yapar" sounds like a calque; "Suudi Arabistan'ın içine ve dışına" is not how a Turkish writer would say it.

18. Vakla: "Hurma servis kapları da sunar." Fine, but the sentence before is a chain of three items plus "hurma ezmesi"; no problem on its own. No further note.

19. Ali Hattab: "Susam tahini". Tahini already means sesame paste, so this is redundant and made me wonder whether another kind exists (cf. "hurma tahini" on Hecer). Also "Halas ve Sugay hurması" again singular "hurması" after two names (fine).

20. Akil el-Habib: "Bal ve limon ürünleri de vardır." "limon" here: ordinary lemon, or the misket limonu used elsewhere? The page uses "misket limonu" elsewhere, so a bare "limon" is unclear.

21. El-Cevheriye: "1993'te kurulduğunu belirten bir fabrika." The hedge "belirten" (a factory that states it was founded in 1993) makes me distrust the claim and also reads oddly, unlike the other cards that just say "kurulan". "Üst kalite" is brochure-ish.

22. Ariş: "El-Ahsa hurmasını seçerek sunar: seçkin Halas, Şişi ve elle hazırlanan Raziz." "seçerek ... seçkin" repeats the root. After the colon, two varieties are listed plain and the third carries a process ("elle hazırlanan"), so the list is lopsided and I cannot tell whether only Raziz is hand-prepared.

23. Et-Tayyar: "geleneksel hediyeler" is unclear: which traditional gifts? Nothing says. "Acve" and "Sukkari" are listed without any hint that they are varieties; I guessed.

24. Er-Rafayi: "Paketleme ve fümigasyon hizmetleri de sunar." "fümigasyon" is a technical word that stopped me; I would not know it is about insect control. "Bir tarım şirketi" then a long chain; fine.

25. El-Yasemin: "Ağırlıklı olarak hurmaya odaklanır, ancak yalnızca hurmayla sınırlı değildir." This restates what the previous sentence already said and cancels itself ("mostly dates but not only dates"). It sits under the Hurma group while saying it is not only dates; filler.

26. Kaytan (Ahmed Abdullah el-Kattan Meşalih): "Kaytan" is an ordinary Turkish word (braid / cord) and I read it as that, not as a name. "Meşalih" first reads as the Arabic-origin Turkish plural "mesalih" (affairs, interests), not as a garment.

27. Kaytan: "El dikimi bişt ve meşalih (ikisi de geleneksel pelerin)". "bişt" is a word I cannot place. The gloss gives the same word "pelerin" to both, so I cannot tell what separates them. It also says hand-sewn, while the FAQ says an authentic bişt is "elde dokunur" (hand-woven): sewn vs woven reads as a mismatch.

28. Kaytan: "kadın bişti de bulunur" — unclear whether this is a separate cut or just a women's version; the sentence stands alone. "Mağaza ürünlerini kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılan bir zanaat olarak tanıtır." The "tanıtır" hedge again makes it a claim by the store, which is fine, but the sentence sits oddly after the collection sentence.

29. El-Kattan Kraliyet Meşalih: "Farklı sırma iplik türleriyle hazırlanan el yapımı El-Ahsa bişti konusunda uzmandır." Long modifier chain before "konusunda uzmandır"; I lost the thread before the verb. "Kraliyet" ("royal") in a store name is brochure-loud. "Lüks hediye kutuları" is shouty for a cloak seller.

30. Ahmed Muhammed el-Kattan: "Çeşitli türlerde kraliyet meşalih diker". "kraliyet meşalih" has no Turkish grammar link (a plural Arabic noun with no izafet), so it reads as a foreign string dropped into a sentence.

31. Same card: "Hesabın son paylaşımı yaklaşık sekiz ay önce yapıldı." Eight months before what date? On a page that will not change, "sekiz ay önce" goes stale; it also leaves me asking whether this seller is still active.

32. Same card: "Siparişinizi hesap üzerinden doğrudan zanaatkâra verin." An imperative that raises a question the page never answers: how do I order on an Instagram account (message? link?)? Which "zanaatkâr" — the card has no craftsperson named. The same sentence appears on the next card, so it reads like a template line.

33. Muhammed el-Emir Kraliyet Meşalih: "Doğal deve tüyü ve yünden, gümüş ve altın sırmalı el yapımı bişt." A fragment with no verb, unlike its neighbours. "Bu hesap her gün aktiftir." contrasts with the previous card and reads like a monitoring note. Also "el-Emir" / "El-Emin" (card in the dates group) look almost the same.

34. İkal es-Sultan: "Ayrıca ud yağı satar." "ud" is the instrument in Turkish; I read "ud yağı" as oil of the instrument before I understood it is oud oil. Also "kadife" (velvet) as an ikal material surprised me, though it may be right.

35. El-Meyamin Tesbih: "Kendi yerel üretimi ürünlere ayrılmış bir bölümü de vardır." Stacked genitive-less chain ("own local production products") — I could not parse it on the first pass. I think it means a section for its own locally made items.

36. LANT: "Ahşap işleri ve modern dokunuşlu, elle tasarlanmış ürünler sunan küçük bir marka." The coordination is ambiguous: "wood work" and "modern-touch products", or wood products with a modern touch? "Ürün ayrıntılarının tamamı okunmadı." This sounds like a reviewer's note left in the text, and it is left hanging (read by whom? so what for me?). The all-caps name gave me no pronunciation.

37. El-Havan Mağazası: "Havan" is an ordinary Turkish word (mortar), so I first read the store name as "the mortar".

38. El-Havan: "Sosyal platformların çoğunda hesabı bulunur." Vague and unverifiable ("most social platforms"); it does not tell me anything useful.

39. Zeki es-Salim: "macbus (baharatlı pirinç yemeği) baharatları" is a stumbling noun chain ("spices of the machbous dish"). Also the spelling "macbus" here against "maabuc" on the Lomi Hasavi card: two spellings for what looks like the same word. "Zeki" is a common Turkish given name and word ("clever"), so the first read was odd for an Arabic-named shop.

40. El-Cubara Marketleri: "El-Ahsa içinde özel bir temsilcisi vardır." Left hanging: a "special representative" for what, and what does it mean for me as a buyer?

41. Vahat El-Ahsa Kahve Kavurmahaneleri: "kavurmahane" is a heavy compound; a Turkish writer would more likely say "kahve kavurma atölyesi" or just "kavurmahane" only in trade writing. It also appears again on the Bun card, so it is consistent, but still stiff.

42. Lomi Hasavi: "maabuc" appears with no gloss; unrelated to "macbus" on the Zeki es-Salim card. I could not tell whether it is the same product. "yaratıcı ürünlere dönüştüren" is brochure language.

43. Alma Nitelikli Kahve Mağazası: "Alma" is a Turkish word (apple; also the negative imperative "don't take / don't buy"), so a shop name that told me "don't buy" made me stop. "nitelikli kahve" sounds like a calque; I would expect a different phrase for specialty coffee.

44. Bun Kavurmahanesi: "yerinde kavurur" is ambiguous (on the spot? at the shop?). "Hüfuf'ta bir kafesi" — "Hüfuf" is not introduced elsewhere on the page and I would not know it is a town. "27.000'den fazla takipçisi olan aktif bir Instagram hesabı" — a follower count is an odd thing to put in a store description. The comma-heavy sentence before it ("bütün yulaftan yapılmış, ilave şeker içermeyen granola") is a long chain to hold before the verb "satar".

45. Gusn ez-Zuhur: "Gusn" is unpronounceable to me; "Zuhur" is a Turkish word (appearing). Flower shops in a guide to local products: unclear why they are here. Same for El-Ahsa Hediyeleri and El-Ahsa Gülleri.

46. El-Ahsa Hediyeleri: "Yenidoğan karşılama ve ısı transfer baskı malzemeleri de bulunur." "Yenidoğan karşılama" is left hanging: welcome what? And "ısı transfer baskı malzemeleri" appears with no connection to the sentence before. Unclear what is actually sold.

47. El-Ahsa Gülleri (Flower Hasa): "Hasa" here against "Ahsa" everywhere else; I could not tell if the Latin brand name is a different place or just a variant. The card name pair "El-Ahsa Hediyeleri" / "El-Ahsa Gülleri" is also hard to tell apart.

48. Muhammed el-Kattan Dükkânları: "Alıcılardan yüksek puan alır." Left hanging: what rating, where? An unsupported claim.

49. Et-Tacira Gufran: "Et-Tacira" read as "tacir" (merchant) plus a letter; "Gufran" I could not pronounce. "davet hediyelikleri" is unclear to me (party favours?).

50. Saaf: "Saaf" with a double a reads as a misspelling of "saf". "düzenleme ürünleri" is vague. "takı standları, kartlar, dekoratif standlar ve masa yolluğu": "standlar" twice, and the last item is singular while the others are plural.

51. Es-Selatin: "Mağazanın her bölümünü, alanında uzman bir tesbih meraklısı yönetir." Odd claim, and "uzman" next to "meraklısı" (enthusiast) contradicts itself; also "her bölümünü ... bir" implies one person manages every section. Sounds like a translated line.

52. Hurz ve Farz Tesbih: "Farz" is a religious term in Turkish (obligatory act), so the name read as devotional, not as a shop name. In the description, "gümüş kakmalı" can attach to the coral only or to every item; unclear. "faturan (reçine)" and "yusr" are glossed or not, but the coral gloss "(yusr)" tells me nothing.

53. Kiral: "Kiral" reads as a typo of "kral" or a half-word of "kiralamak" (rent). The card is menswear, so it is not clearly "local products" either.

54. El-Faravla: "Birden çok markadan kozmetik ve cilt bakımı ürünleri." A fragment with no verb; "Faravla" gives me no cue for pronunciation. It is also unclear what is local about a multi-brand cosmetics shop.

55. Cards generally: "kanalları" (El-Abdüllatif, Er-Rafayi, Kaytan) against "hesapları" elsewhere for the same kind of thing. "Kanal" suggests a broadcast channel. Also "gönderim", "teslimat" and "kargo" all appear for delivery, with no pattern.

56. Note: "Bu rehber yalnızca bilgi amaçlıdır. Satış, ödeme, kargo ve iade her mağazanın sorumluluğundadır." Bureaucratic but clear. It contradicts the FAQ: here nothing is guaranteed, but the FAQ says "özgünlükleri garantilidir" and keeps using "doğrulanmış" and "onaylı". "mağazanın politikasını" — "politika" is a loan word where "koşulları" would sound more natural. It also comes after all 39 cards, where I would not read it.

57. FAQ 1: "Ürünler yüksek kalitelidir ve özgünlükleri garantilidir." Brochure promise, out of register with the note above; no one is named as the guarantor. "minerallerce zengin" is an unsupported brochure phrase. "işlenmiş kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi) ürünleri" is a stumbling chain of two participles. "kırmızı pirinç" is new here: the cards just say "El-Ahsa pirinci".

58. FAQ 1 and 2: "Doğrulanmış çevrim içi mağazalar", "onaylı platformlar". Verified or approved by whom? Left hanging, and other cards say a seller's details were not fully read, which sits badly with "doğrulanmış".

59. FAQ 2: "Bu çeşitler için kalite ve paketleme standartları geçerlidir." Which standards? Hanging. "Böylece mahsul ... taze ulaşır." The "Böylece" implies the standard causes freshness on arrival, which I cannot follow. The question asks "nereden", but the answer never names a place; it only repeats that the guide links to stores.

60. FAQ 3: "Genel anlamıyla bişt de 2025'ten beri UNESCO Somut Olmayan Kültürel Miras Listesi'nde yer alır." "Genel anlamıyla" and the "de" have no earlier thing to refer to; I wondered "also with what?". How this helps me check authenticity is left hanging.

61. FAQ 3: "mağazanın onaylı olduğundan ve El-Ahsa vahasında yer aldığından emin olun" — the check is circular and gives me no concrete way to verify. "nakışın hassasiyetini ve kullanılan kumaşın kalitesini teyit eden garantiler" is bureaucratic ("teyit eden garantiler"); "nakış" (embroidery) is new, since cards talk of sırma, not nakış.

62. FAQ 4: "Tamamen pişmesi için bol su ve sıradan pirinçten daha uzun süre gerekir." The question promises "nasıl pişirilir"; the answer gives no amount or time. "bu sıcaklıklar pirince ayırt edici özelliklerini kazandırır" is an empty cause claim; "yüksek besin değeriyle" is brochure language.

63. FAQ 5: "Evet, ... tüm Arap Körfezi ülkelerine hızlı ve güvenilir gönderim sunar." "birçok mağaza ... tüm ülkelere" mixes "many" and "all" in one sentence; "hızlı ve güvenilir" is shouty. "Arap Körfezi ülkelerinin ekonomik anlaşması" names no agreement; "ulusal ürünlerin ticaretini" reads foreign. "Bu mağazalar bu anlaşmadan yararlanır." repeats "bu" twice and does not tell me what I gain. As a reader from Turkey I also find nothing on shipping to Turkey; it is missing and not answered.

64. FAQ 6: "Yeni mahsulü almak için El-Ahsa'da hurma hasadı ne zaman yapılır?" The purpose clause "almak için" is oddly bolted onto the question. "Gar ve Şişi çeşitleriyle" — "Gar" is a Turkish word (railway station), and "Gar" appears nowhere else on the page. "yeni ve taze mahsulü" is redundant; "onaylı çiftliklerinden" again.

65. FAQ 7: The question "ve çevrim içi nasıl satılır?" is odd from a buyer's side. "Suudi ve Arap Körfezi mutfaklarına" lacks a noun for "Suudi". "Misket limonları büyük bir özenle güneşte kurutulur." is filler. "bütün taneler hâlinde" reads like "whole grains", not limes. "benzersiz bir lezzet derinliği ve özgün bir tat" is brochure language and says nothing.

66. FAQ 8: Switches from imperative ("saklayın") to impersonal ("saklamanız önerilir") inside one answer. "onları" is stiff. "Bu yöntem" does not say which method (the closed container or the freezer).

67. FAQ 9: "Yün ya da keçi kılından yapılan El-Ahsa ikalı gibi geleneksel el sanatları, yalnızca El-Ahsa'dan faaliyet gösteren uzman çevrim içi mağazalarda satılır." A long chain before the verb; and "yalnızca" is an absolute claim that I cannot square with the cards. "önde gelen güvenilir platformları" is shouty. "özgün sanat parçalarına" inflates a cord into "art pieces".

68. FAQ 10: "uygun toptan miktarlarda": "uygun" could mean suitable or cheap. "toplu" in the question but "toptan" in the answer. "Millî Hurma Ağacı ve Hurma Merkezi adlı kurumun" parses as two things before I saw it is one name. The claim that all products are packed to its standards is not supported elsewhere on the page. "güvenle ulaşması" and "özel gönderim seçenekleri" are vague.

69. Search box and labels: "Eşleşen mağaza yok." is blunt but acceptable. "Platform: Bağımsız internet sitesi" would read oddly aloud, since an own website is not a platform. The aria-label "Mağazalarda arayın" is an imperative where a noun phrase would be more usual.

70. Picture descriptions: "Eş-Şira Hurma çevrim içi mağaza sayfası" gives no sign that it is a picture; "Hecer Bahçeleri Hurma Fabrikası logosu" is fine. Mild.

71. Buttons: "Mağazayı ziyaret edin" repeated on all 39 cards is stiff but understandable; "Hesabı ziyaret edin" is right for Instagram cards.

72. Global: "çevrim içi" is used everywhere (title, headings, FAQ). It is correct but heavy; next to "internet sitesi" it reads as two different registers.
