# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r4

## Page 1 - Al-Ahsa online stores (/tr/online-stores/)

1. Meta description: "Hurmadan el sanatlarına, kahveden El-Ahsa pirincine kadar özgün El-Ahsa ürünleri satan çevrim içi mağazaları keşfedin." "El-Ahsa" appears twice in one breath ("El-Ahsa pirincine", "özgün El-Ahsa ürünleri"), which sounds clumsy. "Kahve" is also promised here, but there is no coffee category on the page. A single "El-Ahsa" would read better, for example "özgün yerel ürünler".

2. Meta description: "Tek bir rehberden her mağazaya doğrudan ulaşabilirsiniz." Fine on its own. But several cards are Instagram accounts, not stores, so "her mağaza" over-promises.

3. Heading: "Yerel ürün satan El-Ahsa çevrim içi mağazaları rehberi." Four nouns stacked before "rehberi". I read it twice. Also, "yerel ürün" does not fit many cards (cosmetics, menswear, home decor, flowers, insulated cups). Nothing says these are local products, so the heading and intro promise something the list does not deliver.

4. Intro: "Her bağlantı sizi doğrudan mağazaya götürür." Four cards are Instagram accounts whose button says "Hesabı ziyaret edin", not a store. The promise does not quite match.

5. Filters: "Diğer ürünler 17". "Diğer ürünler" ("other products") is a vague catch-all. It holds flowers, cosmetics, coffee, menswear and rosaries. Two rosary shops (Es-Selatin, Hurz ve Farz Tesbih) sit here, while El-Meyamin Tesbih is under "El sanatları". The grouping looks inconsistent.

6. Platform line "Salla": I read it as the Turkish imperative "salla" ("wave/shake it"), not a platform name, until "Platform: Salla" appeared in the screen-reader text. Nothing on the page says what Salla is. The reader is left to guess.

7. Platform line "Zid": reads like "zıt" ("opposite"). It is not explained as a platform either.

8. Platform line "Salla (Mahally rehberi üzerinden)": "Mahally" is unexplained, and "rehberi" is left hanging. Which guide? A reader cannot tell if it is a directory, an app or a person.

9. Platform line "Bağımsız internet sitesi": it reads as a description, not a platform name. It sits in the same slot as "Salla". Acceptable, but the register changes.

10. Eş-Şira Hurma: "Halas ve Raziz hurması, hurma poleni suyu ve Şaabiyat satar." "Hurma poleni suyu" is an odd chain; I did not know what it is. "Şaabiyat" is not a word I can place and nothing glosses it. The sentence also has no subject, so I do not see who "satar".

11. Hecer Bahçeleri: "Fabrika farklı kalitelerde Halas hurması ile Şişi hurması satar; kaju, badem ve Antep fıstığıyla içi doldurulmuş hurmalar da sunar. Ayrıca hurma pekmezi ve hurma tahini vardır." The subject changes from "Fabrika ... satar" to the impersonal "vardır". The first sentence is long and the "ile" pairing is stiff. "Şişi" read first as a plain word (close to "şiş", skewer) before I understood it is a variety.

12. Hecer Bahçeleri: "Fabrikanın Instagram ve Snapchat hesapları bulunur." "Bulunur" is a bureaucratic way to say "var". Nothing is wrong grammatically, but "bulunur" repeats on card after card in the same dry register.

13. Eş-Şark Hurma: "Fabrikanın en çok öne çıkan ürünü hurma şekeridir." "En çok öne çıkan" is clumsy. "Hurma şekeri" is ambiguous: date sugar or date candy? The preceding "Hurma ve hurma ürünleri satar" is also very thin and bare. Also, "Şark" is a real (bookish) Turkish word for "East", so the name reads like "Eastern Date".

14. Sifsif Mağazası: "Aile markasının ana ürünleri El-Ahsa Raziz hurması, içi doldurulmuş Halas hurması, hurma dolgulu hamur işi (maamul) ve hurma tatlılarıdır." Long, but it works. The gloss "(maamul)" helps. "X platformunda, Instagram'da ve Snapchat'te" is fine. "Snapchat'te" looked off at first because of the suffix on an English name.

15. El-Emin Hurma Fabrikası: "aside (bulamaç) karışımları". "Aside" reads like the English word "aside" or a foreign word. The gloss "bulamaç" helps a little but does not tell me what it is for. "Preslenmiş hurma" (pressed dates) is also unclear to me.

16. El-Emin Hurma Fabrikası: "Kontrol edildiği sırada bazı ürünler stokta yoktu." Passive with no agent and no date. Checked by whom, and when? A reader cannot act on it. It also sounds like an editorial note that slipped into the text. It raises a question the page does not answer (which products?).

17. El-Abdüllatif: "Üst kalite hurma satan bir fabrika." "Üst kalite" feels like a calque of "premium". "Yüksek kaliteli" or "birinci sınıf" would be more natural. It recurs on several cards (Cevheriye, Ariş, FAQ).

18. El-Abdüllatif: "Hurma paketleme hizmeti verir, ayrıca hurma alımı yapar." "Hurma alımı yapar" is ambiguous: does it buy dates from farmers, or arrange purchases for me? I did not understand the offer.

19. İbn Zeyd Mağazası: "Halibi hurması, İbn Zeyd'in “Kulla” ve “Şanna” adlı iki El-Ahsa serisi". I could not tell what "iki El-Ahsa serisi" means (two product lines? two date varieties?). The names "Kulla" and "Şanna" look like Turkish verbs/word fragments ("kulla", from "kullanmak"). No explanation, so this is unclear. "Halibi" is also unexplained.

20. Vakla: "Elle hazırlanan Halas ve Şişi hurması". I did not understand what "hand-prepared" means for a date. Then "Hurma servis kapları da sunar" is a little jump from food to tableware, but fine.

21. Akil el-Habib Çiftlikleri: "hurma lokumu". Is that Turkish delight made with dates, or something else? Unclear. "Bal ve limon ürünleri de vardır." Which "limon"? It is not "misket limonu (lomi)" as elsewhere. It left me unsure whether it is the same product.

22. El-Cevheriye Hurma: "1993'te kurulduğunu belirten bir fabrika." The hedge ("says it was founded") appears only here. Other cards state dates flatly ("1995'te kurulan", "2011'den beri çalışan"). The tone suddenly becomes suspicious about this one factory and I wondered why. Inconsistent register or policy.

23. Et-Tayyar: "geleneksel hediyeler ve maamul satar". "Geleneksel hediyeler" (traditional gifts) is vague. What are they?

24. Er-Rafayi: "Paketleme ve fümigasyon hizmetleri de sunar." "Fümigasyon" is a technical loanword a shopper would stumble on, and I do not see why a buyer needs it. Also it follows "Bir tarım şirketi" (an agricultural company) as if it were a store; the nature of what I can buy is murky.

25. El-Yasemin Organik Çiftliği: "Ağırlıklı olarak hurmaya odaklanır, ancak yalnızca hurmayla sınırlı değildir." This says nothing. It promises that there are other products but never says which. The sentence before it ("organik hurma bölümü öne çıkar") already says the same. Left hanging.

26. Kaytan (Ahmed Abdullah el-Kattan Meşalih): "Kaytan" is an ordinary Turkish word (braid, cord, trim), so I first read it as "braid" rather than a shop name. "Meşalih" looks plural and strange to a Turkish eye.

27. Kaytan: "El dikimi bişt ve meşalih (ikisi de geleneksel pelerin) satar." I do not know either word. The gloss says both are "traditional cape" — if they are the same thing, why two words? If they differ, the gloss hides it. Unclear what I would actually buy. Also "pelerin" for a men's ceremonial cloak is a slightly odd choice.

28. Kaytan: "Mağaza ürünlerini kuşaktan kuşağa aktarılan bir zanaat olarak tanıtır." Fine, but "tanıtır" ("presents") makes it a claim of the shop; ok.

29. El-Kattan Kraliyet Meşalih: "Kraliyet Meşalih" reads strangely: "kraliyet" (royal) next to an unknown word. I could not tell whether "Kraliyet" is a quality claim or part of the name. It appears in three names/descriptions (also Ahmed Muhammed, Muhammed el-Emir) and is not explained.

30. El-Kattan Kraliyet Meşalih: "Farklı sırma iplik türleriyle hazırlanan el yapımı El-Ahsa bişti konusunda uzmandır." Long, with a stacked adjective phrase before "bişti" ("sırma iplik türleriyle hazırlanan el yapımı El-Ahsa bişti") and the verb comes late. I lost the thread. "Lüks hediye kutuları da sunar" is a little brochure-like for an otherwise dry page.

31. Ahmed Muhammed el-Kattan Meşalih: "Hesabın son paylaşımı yaklaşık sekiz ay önce yapıldı." This goes stale and says "eight months ago" with no date. It also sounds like a warning, and it is the only place where the page tells me an account is inactive. It is also an odd thing for a site to say about a shop it lists.

32. Ahmed Muhammed el-Kattan Meşalih: "Siparişinizi hesap üzerinden doğrudan zanaatkâra verin." Imperative instruction repeated word for word on the next card. It reads as boilerplate. "Zanaatkâr" appears out of nowhere: the card never mentioned a craftsman.

33. Muhammed el-Emir Kraliyet Meşalih: "Doğal deve tüyü ve yünden, gümüş ve altın sırmalı el yapımı bişt." This is a noun phrase with no verb. It reads as a caption, unlike the neighbouring cards. "Bu hesap her gün aktiftir." conflicts oddly with the previous card ("sekiz ay önce"): this tells me the opposite and reads as a sales claim.

34. İkal es-Sultan: "El yapımı El-Ahsa ikalı (başlık kordonu) yapar" — "yapımı ... yapar" repeats the same root in one sentence. "Kokulu seçenekleri de vardır" — scented ikal? I was not sure what that means. "Oud yağı" is fine, though "ud yağı" would be the Turkish spelling.

35. El-Meyamin Tesbih: "Yerelde kendi ürettiği ürünlere ayrılmış bir bölümü de vardır." "Yerelde" sounds foreign (a calque of "locally"). "Yerel olarak" or "kendi atölyesinde" would be more natural. "Bölüm" of a website, or of a physical shop? Unclear.

36. LANT: "Ürün ayrıntılarının tamamı okunmadı." This is an internal editorial note, not text for a reader. It reads as "I did not read all of it". It tells me nothing about the shop and sounds careless. Also the first sentence ("Ahşap işleri ve modern dokunuşlu, elle tasarlanmış ürünler sunan küçük bir marka") does not tell me what the products are.

37. El-Havan Mağazası: "Sosyal platformların çoğunda hesabı bulunur." Vague ("most of the social platforms"). Nothing I could use.

38. Zeki es-Salim: "Zeki" read as a normal Turkish given name/adjective ("clever"). "macbus (baharatlı pirinç yemeği) baharatları" — "baharatlı pirinç yemeği baharatları" is a heavy stack even with the parentheses. And the spelling "macbus" differs from "maabuc" on the Lomi Hasavi card. Same dish, two spellings.

39. Zeki es-Salim: "Halas hurması ek ürün olarak bulunur." Fine but "bulunur" again; "ek ürün olarak" ("as an extra product") reads translated.

40. El-Cubara Marketleri: "El-Ahsa içinde özel bir temsilcisi vardır." I do not know what this representative is for (delivery? sales?). Left hanging. "Ev erzağı" is acceptable but slightly bookish.

41. Vahat El-Ahsa Kahve Kavurmahaneleri: "Kavurmahane" sounds invented to me. Turkish would say "kahve kavurma atölyesi" or "kahve kavurucu". It is used again on Bun and Alma, and is heavy on a page that is otherwise plain. "Bitki, yağ, kuruyemiş ve safran" — "bitki" alone is unclear (herbs? plants?).

42. Lomi Hasavi: "Kurutulmuş misket limonunu (lomi) yaratıcı ürünlere dönüştüren bir marka: acı sos, maabuc, öğütülmüş lomi, baharat karışımları ve hediye kutuları." "Maabuc" is unknown and is not glossed here, though the same dish is glossed as "macbus" on another card. "Yaratıcı ürünlere dönüştüren" sounds like marketing copy. "Hasavi" reads like "Hasavi" I could not place; it looks like a foreign spelling next to "El-Ahsa" elsewhere.

43. Alma Nitelikli Kahve Mağazası: "Alma" is the Turkish word for "apple" (and "taking"), so the name looked like "Apple specialty coffee store". "Nitelikli kahve" sounds bureaucratic and translated (specialty coffee is usually said "özel kahve" or kept as "specialty coffee"). I did not know whether "nitelikli" means "quality" or a certified category.

44. Bun Kavurmahanesi: "Bun" reads as the English word "bun" (bread roll); I only guessed it is a coffee term. "Yalıtımlı bardaklar ve bütün yulaftan yapılmış, ilave şeker içermeyen granola satar" jumps from coffee to cups to granola in one breath and the second item has a long participial chain ("bütün yulaftan yapılmış, ilave şeker içermeyen granola"). "Hüfuf'ta bir kafesi" — "Hüfuf" is not introduced; I assume it is a town in El-Ahsa but the page does not say.

45. Bun Kavurmahanesi: "27.000'den fazla takipçisi olan aktif bir Instagram hesabı vardır." A stale number with no date. Also strange to include follower counts for this shop only; no other card does.

46. Gusn ez-Zuhur: "Taze ve yapay çiçek, çiçek aranjmanı, çikolata, balon ve hediye satar." A list, which is fine, but nothing ties it to El-Ahsa products; see item 3.

47. El-Ahsa Hediyeleri: "Yenidoğan bebeklerin karşılanması için malzemeler de satar." "Bebeklerin karşılanması" ("receiving of newborns") is a literal calque; I had to guess it means welcome-baby supplies. "Ayrıca ısı transfer baskı malzemeleri bulunur." This comes from nowhere and I could not tell what it is for.

48. El-Ahsa Gülleri (Flower Hasa): "Mobil uygulaması ile Instagram ve Snapchat hesapları vardır." Fine. But "Hasa" in the Latin name versus "Ahsa" everywhere else looks like a typo until you see it is a brand name.

49. Muhammed el-Kattan Dükkânları: "Alıcılardan yüksek puan alır." Unattributed claim: what rating, on which platform? Left hanging, and the only card to cite ratings.

50. Et-Tacira Gufran: "Et-Tacira" ("the merchant", feminine) reads strangely as the opening of a name with the article. "Mağaza güzellik ve cilt bakımı malzemeleri ... satar." This is the only card that begins with "Mağaza" as a subject; others omit it. "Davet hediyelikleri" is fine.

51. Saaf: "Ev dekorasyonu ve düzenleme ürünleri: takı standları, kartlar, dekoratif standlar ve masa yolluğu." "Takı standları" and "dekoratif standlar" overlap. "Düzenleme ürünleri" is vague. "Masa yolluğu" is singular in a list of plurals. The name "Saaf" with the double a made me unsure of the pronunciation.

52. Es-Selatin: "Her bölümün başında, konusunda uzman bir tesbih tutkunu vardır." I could not tell what "bölüm" is here (store section? collection?) or why that matters. Left hanging. "Lüks tesbihler" with "kehribar, ceylan boynuzu" is a noun phrase with no verb.

53. Hurz ve Farz Tesbih: "Farz" is a well-known Turkish religious word (an obligation). "Hurz ve Farz" first read as a religious phrase; I only understood it was a shop name from the context. "Faturan (reçine)" is unknown; the gloss helps. "Gümüş kakmalı siyah mercan (yusr)" is fine.

54. Kiral: reads like "kıral / kiralık" for a Turkish eye ("king" or "for rent"). The description itself is short and clear.

55. El-Faravla: I could not place the name; no clue to its meaning. The description is a fragment with no verb ("Birden çok markadan kozmetik ve cilt bakımı ürünleri.").

56. Note: "Bu rehber yalnızca bilgi amaçlıdır. Satış, ödeme, kargo ve iade her mağazanın sorumluluğundadır." Legal and a bit stiff, but understandable. It contradicts the FAQ below, which says the stores are "doğrulanmış", "onaylı" and that quality is "garantilidir" (see 58, 61, 69).

57. FAQ 1 answer: "minerallerce zengin El-Ahsa kırmızı pirinci". The cards only ever say "El-Ahsa pirinci" (and "yıllanmış"); "kırmızı" and "minerallerce zengin" appear first here, with no source. "İşlenmiş kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi) ürünleri" stacks two participles and sounded heavy.

58. FAQ 1 answer: "Ürünler yüksek kalitelidir ve özgünlükleri garantilidir." Brochure-shouty. It is also a blanket guarantee that conflicts with the note above, which says sales, payment, shipping and returns are each shop's responsibility. Who guarantees?

59. FAQ 1 answer: "Doğrulanmış çevrim içi mağazalar bu geleneksel ürünleri Suudi Arabistan'daki ve Arap Körfezi ülkelerindeki müşterilere sunar." "Doğrulanmış" (verified) is never explained anywhere on the page. By whom and how? The cards instead say things like "Kontrol edildiği sırada" and "1993'te kurulduğunu belirten".

60. FAQ 2 answer: "Visit Al-Ahsa rehberi, Halas ve Raziz çeşitlerini sunan onaylı platformlara bağlantı verir." "Onaylı platformlar": approved by whom? Not stated. "Bu çeşitler için kalite ve paketleme standartları geçerlidir." Whose standards? Left hanging. "Böylece" then ties freshness to these unnamed standards. Also "mahsul" is bookish (it reads as agricultural-report language). "Visit Al-Ahsa rehberi" mixes the English brand name into a Turkish sentence.

61. FAQ 3 answer: "mağazanın onaylı olduğundan ve El-Ahsa vahasında yer aldığından emin olun." I was not sure how a shopper checks either point. "Onaylı" is again unexplained. The last sentence ("Uzmanlaşmış ve doğrulanmış mağazalar, nakışın hassasiyetini ve kullanılan kumaşın kalitesini teyit eden garantiler sunar.") is heavy and bureaucratic. It also claims "garantiler" that no card mentions, and it talks of "nakış" (embroidery) where earlier text talks about "sırma".

62. FAQ 3 answer: "Genel olarak bişt zanaatı 2025'ten beri UNESCO Somut Olmayan Kültürel Miras Listesi'nde yer alır." "Genel olarak" is misplaced: it reads "in general", and I did not understand it was meant as "the bisht craft as a whole, not only El-Ahsa's". Reading it, I could not tell whether El-Ahsa bisht itself is on the list. Also the date and list are asserted without any attribution.

63. FAQ 3 answer: "Özgün bir bişt, gümüş ya da altın sırma iplikle elde dokunur." The card for Muhammed el-Emir says deve tüyü ve yün with sırma; this one makes sırma the only criterion. A reader cannot tell if the Kaytan or other cards without sırma are inauthentic.

64. FAQ 4 answer: "El-Ahsa vahasında yüksek sıcaklıklarda yetişir; bu sıcaklıklar pirince ayırt edici özelliklerini kazandırır." Odd cause-and-effect claim; "yüksek sıcaklıklarda yetişir" sounded like a problem, not a feature. "Yüksek besin değeri" is also unsupported.

65. FAQ 4: the question asks "nasıl pişirilir" (how to cook). The answer ("Tamamen pişmesi için bol su ve sıradan pirinçten daha uzun süre gerekir.") gives no amount or time. The question is raised and left half-answered. "Özenle paketlenmiş olarak sunar" is a stiff "olarak" construction.

66. FAQ 5 answer: "tüm Arap Körfezi ülkelerine hızlı ve güvenilir gönderim sunar." Brochure-shouty, and it overclaims. Cards say only some stores ship to Gulf countries (and several say only Saudi cities). "Hızlı ve güvenilir" is promise language. "Arap Körfezi ülkelerinin ekonomik anlaşması ulusal ürünlerin ticaretini kolaylaştırır. Bu mağazalar bu anlaşmadan yararlanır." Which agreement? It is never named, and the sentence repeats "bu ... bu". It left a question open. "Ulusal ürünler" is bookish. Also, "Arap Körfezi" for a Turkish reader sounds slightly odd next to the common "Körfez ülkeleri", but consistent.

67. FAQ 6 question: "Yeni mahsulü alabilmek için El-Ahsa'da hurma hasadı ne zaman yapılır?" The purpose clause "Yeni mahsulü alabilmek için" is attached to the harvest, as if the harvest happens in order to let me buy. I had to reread. Better to move it to the shopper: "Yeni mahsul hurmayı almak için ... ne zaman?".

68. FAQ 6 answer: "ağustos ayının sonlarında Gar ve Şişi çeşitleriyle başlar." "Gar" is the ordinary Turkish word for a railway station; I read it as a mistake first. It does not appear on any card either. "Zirveye ulaşır" for the harvest is fine. "Yeni ve taze mahsulü" is redundant, and "bölgenin onaylı çiftliklerinden ve fabrikalarından doğrudan alabilir" repeats the unexplained "onaylı".

69. FAQ 7 question: "Kurutulmuş misket limonu (lomi) nasıl kullanılır ve çevrim içi nasıl satılır?" The second half ("nasıl satılır") is odd, since the reader is a buyer. "Nasıl satın alınır" is what I would expect. The answer never explains how it is sold, other than "bütün taneler hâlinde ya da öğütülmüş".

70. FAQ 7 answer: "geleneksel ve temel bir malzemedir; Suudi ve Arap Körfezi mutfaklarına kendine özgü ekşi bir lezzet katar." "Suudi ve Arap Körfezi mutfakları" lists Saudi beside the Gulf as if separate. "Misket limonları büyük bir özenle güneşte kurutulur." is filler. "Bu yemeklere benzersiz bir lezzet derinliği ve özgün bir tat verir." is brochure-shouty and repeats "kendine özgü" from earlier.

71. FAQ 8 answer: "saklamanız gerekir ... saklamanız önerilir ... Bu saklama yöntemi" — the root "sakla-" is repeated three times in three short sentences and sounds heavy. "Nemden ve yüksek sıcaklıktan uzakta" but then "buzdolabında" (humid) — no contradiction stated but I wondered. "(birkaç aya kadar)" after "daha uzun süreler" is vague about how long.

72. FAQ 9 answer: "Bu mağazalar yalnızca El-Ahsa'dan faaliyet gösterir." "Faaliyet gösterir" is bureaucratic, and "yalnızca El-Ahsa'dan" is ambiguous (only based in El-Ahsa, or only selling El-Ahsa goods?). Cards such as Bun mention a Hüfuf cafe and others ship across the Kingdom, so I was not sure what this says.

73. FAQ 9 answer: "Visit Al-Ahsa rehberi yerel zanaatkârları destekleyen önde gelen güvenilir platformları bir araya getirir." "Önde gelen güvenilir platformlar" is brochure language ("leading trusted platforms"). The page lists stores and Instagram accounts, not platforms, and it disclaims responsibility. Also the question asks "nerede satılır" and the answer names no place. Left hanging.

74. FAQ 10 answer: "uygun toptan miktarlarda hurma ve El-Ahsa pirinci satın almaya imkân verir." "Uygun toptan miktarlar" was unclear (suitable? affordable?), and "imkân verir" is bookish. No card mentions wholesale dates or rice; only the flower shop says "toptan". So this claim is not supported on the page.

75. FAQ 10 answer: "Ürünler Millî Hurma Ağacı ve Hurma Merkezi adlı kurumun sıkı standartlarına göre paketlenir." Long chain with "adlı kurumun" and the institution's name ("Millî Hurma Ağacı ve Hurma Merkezi") parsed oddly: "Hurma Ağacı ve Hurma" is a tongue-twisting pair. I did not know whether it is a real body. It also claims all packing follows "sıkı standartlar", which adds to the unexplained standards in FAQ 2. "Güvenle ulaşması" and "özel gönderim seçenekleri" are vague.

76. Buttons: "Mağazayı ziyaret edin" is repeated 35 times; "Hesabı ziyaret edin" four times. A shopper "visiting" an online shop is understandable, but the repetition is heavy. Fine otherwise.

77. Search: placeholder "Mağaza adına ya da ürüne göre arayın…" is clear. aria-labels "Mağaza filtreleri" and "Mağazalarda arayın" are fine. "Eşleşen mağaza yok." is clear.

78. Screen-reader text "Platform: Salla" is clear in form, but the platform is still unexplained; see item 6.

79. Image descriptions: "Eş-Şira Hurma çevrim içi mağaza sayfası" / "... logosu" are clear. The first one is a little long-winded for a photo description ("çevrim içi mağaza sayfası" could be "mağaza sayfası"), but not a problem.
