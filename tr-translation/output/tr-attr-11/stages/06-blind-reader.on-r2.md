# Blind reader notes (Turkish reader), on-r2

## Page 1 - El-Ahsa Misket Limonu Çiftliği

1. Breadcrumb "Ana sayfa / Çarşılar ve parklar / ... Çiftlikler". A farm sits under "Bazaars and parks". I expected a market or a park and had to guess the section was a catch-all. The category label (Çiftlikler) and the section (Çarşılar ve parklar) do not fit together. I have no fix, because I don't know what the section is meant to cover.
2. Browser tab: "El-Ahsa Misket Limonu Çiftliği — Visit Al-Ahsa". The same tab spells the place two ways, "El-Ahsa" and "Al-Ahsa". I paused on whether these are the same place. (The Latin-script site name may be a fixed brand; that is a guess.)
3. "ziyaretçiler ünlü El-Ahsa misket limonunu (lomi) mevsiminde burada toplar." I don't know "lomi". It looks like a typo for "limon" or "lime", and nothing explains it. In Turkish, "misket limonu" already means lime, so the bracket looks redundant or wrong until I assume it is a local term. If "lomi" is a different product from "misket limonu", the page never says so.
4. Same sentence: "ünlü ... toplar". "Famous" is a claim I cannot check. "Toplar" is a flat statement, and I cannot tell whether it is an offer. Can any visitor pick fruit? For free or for pay? Is there a basket, a guide, or a sale? The "Ziyaret bilgileri" block never says. The page promises picking but never says what I would actually do.
5. The lead sentence is printed twice, under the title and again directly below the buttons, word for word. The second copy reads like a template glitch.
6. "Haziran ortasından ağustos ayına kadar, misket limonu toplama mevsimi." This is a fragment with no verb, so I had to supply "is" myself. "Ağustos ayına kadar" is ambiguous: does it include August or stop at the start of it? "Kadar" usually reads as "until, including", but I was not sure.
7. "Her gün 16.00–21.30 (yetişme mevsimlerinde)". "Yetişme mevsimlerinde" is plural, "growing seasons", while the line above names one season, "toplama mevsimi" (harvest). Growing and harvesting are different things, and one season has become several. What are the hours outside the season? Is the farm closed? It is left hanging.
8. "Saatler ve ücretler değişebilir — ziyaretten önce işletmeci ile teyit edin." No fee appears anywhere on the page, so "ücretler" raises a question the page never answers (is there an entrance fee or a price per kilo?). "Teyit edin" is bureaucratic and bookish; a Turkish writer would more likely say "doğrulayın" or "işletmeciyle görüşün". "İşletmeci ile" is stiff and written apart from its suffix. There is no phone, contact or name, so I cannot do what I am told.
9. "[button] Benden ne kadar uzak?" This is grammatical, but "from me" in the place's own voice sounds foreign. It sounds like a literal button label; "Bana uzaklığı" or "Konumuma uzaklık" is how a Turkish interface would put it.

## Page 2 - Çilek Çiftliği

1. "Ukayr yolu üzerinde bir çilek çiftliği;". "Ukayr" is a name I cannot place or pronounce with confidence. It could be a town, a district or a road, and the page says nothing about it. I read "Ukayr yolu" as "the road to Ukayr", but no starting point or distance is given. Where the farm is stays unclear. The page has no "Bölge" field, unlike page 4.
2. "mevsimlik çilek toplama imkânı sunar." "İmkânı sunar" is brochure-style Turkish; "çilek toplayabilirsiniz" would be plainer. "Mevsimlik" says seasonal but not which season (the hours line later says winter and spring). As on page 1, nothing says whether picking is free or paid.
3. The lead sentence is repeated verbatim under the buttons.
4. "Kış ve ilkbahar mevsimlerinde 15.30–21.30". "Mevsimlerinde" after two season names is redundant. Unlike pages 1, 3 and 4, there is no "Her gün", so I cannot tell whether the hours apply on all days. Nothing says what happens in summer or autumn (closed? no strawberries?). I had to infer "closed", and it is not stated.
5. "Saatler ve ücretler değişebilir — ziyaretten önce işletmeci ile teyit edin." The same issues as page 1. No fee is given, no operator is named, and "teyit" and "işletmeci ile" are stiff.

## Page 3 - Es-Sevsen Kuş Bahçeleri

1. "Es-Sevsen". I first read the second element as the Turkish word "sevsen" ("if you loved"), so "Es-Sevsen" sounded like a strange Turkish phrase and not a name. Hyphen plus article does not stop that reading. I would stress and pronounce it wrongly.
2. "El-Ahsa vahasının atmosferinde kuşların sergilendiği bir bahçe." This stumbled. "Vahasının atmosferinde" ("in the atmosphere of the oasis") sounds foreign. "Atmosfer" is a calque, and "vahanın havasında/havasını yansıtan" is closer to how Turkish writers put it. "Kuşların sergilendiği" ("birds are exhibited") treats the birds as exhibits. I cannot tell whether this is an aviary, a garden with free-flying birds, or a collection in cages. What would I do there: look, walk, feed? The page doesn't say.
3. Title "Kuş Bahçeleri" (plural, "gardens") against "bir bahçe" (singular) in the lead. I could not tell whether it is one place or several.
4. There is no location of any kind: no "Bölge" field and no neighbourhood or road. I cannot place this garden, even loosely.
5. The lead sentence is repeated verbatim under the buttons.
6. "Her gün 15.30–22.30". The hours are identical to page 4's. This is probably coincidence, but it may be a copied value, and it makes me trust the figure a little less.

## Page 4 - Et-Taraf Hayvanat Bahçesi

1. "Et-Taraf". "Taraf" is an ordinary Turkish word ("side, party"), so "Et-Taraf" first read as "the side". After that, "Et-Taraf kasabası" read as "the side town" before I took it as a name. Hyphen and article help only a little.
2. "Et-Taraf kasabası, Hüfuf'un doğusunda, vahanın köyleri arasında yer alır. Kasabada Et-Taraf Hayvanat Bahçesi bulunur." The body repeats the lead and then adds a second short statement with the name repeated again. The chain of short "yer alır / bulunur" sentences is flat and dry, almost reference-book. "Vahanın köyleri arasında" also sounded odd: a kasaba (town) located among villages. The relation of "kasaba" and "köyler" is not explained.
3. "Hayvanat bahçesi, çocukların çeşitli hayvanları yakından görebildiği bir aile gezi noktasıdır." "Gezi noktası" is tourism jargon and sounds like a translation; "gezilecek yer" or "gezi yeri" would be natural. "Çeşitli hayvanlar" tells me nothing: which animals? Is it a small local zoo? That is missing.
4. "Bölgenin sade ve telaşsız aile gezilerinden biridir;" This stumbled. The zoo (a place) is called "one of the region's family trips (gezi)", a mismatch of noun types. "Sade" is an odd word for a zoo ("simple, plain"), and I could not tell if it is praise or a warning.
5. "yakındaki Et-Taraf kırsalını ve vahanın güney kesimlerini ziyaret etmekle kolayca birleştirilebilir." This is a long passive sentence and it lost me before the verb. "Et-Taraf kırsalı" and "vahanın güney kesimleri" are never named or explained: what is there to see? The page says Et-Taraf is east of Hüfuf, then "south parts of the oasis" appear with no connection. The sentence promises combinations and then does not give a single thing to visit.
6. "Geziyi planlamadan önce yerinde teyit edin." This is contradictory. "Yerinde" means "on the spot, on site", but I am told to do it before planning the trip, and I can't be on site before planning. It also does not say what to confirm (hours? whether it is open?). It then clashes with the box below, which says to confirm "with the operator". Two different instructions for the same thing.
7. "Bölge: Et-Taraf kasabası — Hüfuf'un doğusu" only repeats what the lead and body already said. It is the only page of the first four that has a "Bölge" field.
8. "Her gün 15.30–22.30" is identical to page 3's. The disclaimer still mentions "ücretler" with no fee anywhere.

## Page 5 - El-Hamidiye Çarşısı

1. Title "El-Hamidiye Çarşısı" with the lead "Artık çarşı olarak çalışmıyor". The name promises a bazaar, and I only learn in the second sentence of the lead that there is none. As a reader planning a trip I misread it at first and expected shopping. The page then spends its length contradicting its own title.
2. "Hüfuf'un merkezindeki geleneksel çarşı binası, tarihî şehir merkezini geliştirme çalışmaları kapsamında restore edildi." This is a long chain, and the verb arrives only at the end. "Tarihî şehir merkezini geliştirme çalışmaları kapsamında" is project-office, bureaucratic language ("kapsamında" is pure official-document Turkish). "Merkez" appears twice in one sentence (Hüfuf'un merkezindeki, şehir merkezini). The word "çalışma" here means "project work", but I first read it as "working", given the next sentence about not working.
3. "bugün binada Şark Restoranı hizmet veriyor." "Şark" is an ordinary Turkish word ("the East, Orient"), so "Şark Restoranı" reads as a generic "Oriental restaurant" and not a name. "Hizmet veriyor" is officialese; "restoran var / restoran bulunuyor" is closer to speech. I cannot tell if the restaurant is the point of a visit: no hours, no kind of food, nothing.
4. "El-Hamidiye Çarşısı, Hüfuf'un merkezinde bir binadır." This is circular: a "Çarşı" that "is a building". "Hamidiye" also reads to a Turk as an Ottoman-era name (Abdülhamid), and the page says nothing of origin. The word "geleneksel" appears but no date or period anywhere.
5. Repetition. The restore sentence appears in the lead, the body and the FAQ (twice). "Artık çarşı olarak çalışmıyor; içinde/binasında Şark Restoranı hizmet veriyor" appears in the lead, body, "Güncel durum" and the first FAQ answer, and the location (Hüfuf merkezi, Kuzey er-Rifa, Kral Abdülaziz Yolu) is repeated in body, "Bölge" and FAQ. Reading it top to bottom I felt I was reading the same two facts five times, and the page has almost nothing else.
6. "Bu çalışmalarla birlikte El-Kaysariye Çarşısı yeniden inşa edildi, Zanaatkârlar Çarşısı da kuruldu." Two new names appear with no location relative to this building and no explanation. "Yeniden inşa edildi" (rebuilt) against "restore edildi" (restored) for this building is a different verb for a similar action, and I could not tell if they were rebuilt from scratch. Are those two bazaars working? Can I shop there? This raises more questions than it answers. "Zanaatkârlar" is also a bookish word; I wasn't sure it is a name or a description.
7. "Böylece bina, şehrin kalbindeki geleneksel çarşı binaları kümesine katıldı." "Kümesine katıldı" ("joined the cluster") sounds foreign. "Küme" is a statistics word, and with the possessive "kümesine" I briefly saw "kümes" (coop). "Şehrin kalbi" is a cliché. It also contradicts the next sentence: the building joins "traditional market buildings" yet "is not a working market".
8. "Günümüzde bina çalışan bir çarşı değil, tarihî bir yapıdır: artık halk çarşısı olarak çalışmıyor; içinde Şark Restoranı hizmet veriyor." The register jumps from the bookish "günümüzde / yapıdır" to the plain "çalışmıyor" inside one sentence. "Halk çarşısı" ("people's/public bazaar") is a strange phrase; I do not know what a non-"halk" çarşı would be. The colon is followed by what is the same statement again.
9. "Binaya ve tarihine dair daha fazla ayrıntı, burada yayımlanmadan önce güvenilir kaynaklardan teyit edilmeyi bekliyor." This is the site talking about its own editing process. As a reader I don't care that something is awaiting confirmation. It sounds bureaucratic ("teyit edilmeyi bekliyor"), and it leaves me with an empty promise: "its history" is mentioned, but no history is given anywhere on the page, not even one date.
10. "Güncel durum: Tarihî bir yapı — artık çarşı olarak çalışmıyor; içinde Şark Restoranı hizmet veriyor". This is the fourth copy of the same statement. "Ziyaret bilgileri" has no hours here, unlike pages 1 to 4, so I can't tell whether the building can be entered or whether only the restaurant is open.
11. FAQ: "El-Hamidiye Çarşısı'nın çevresindeki geleneksel çarşılarla bağlantısı nedir?" The question stumbled (bağlantısı = "connection"?). The answer states only that this building was restored and that two other markets were built or rebuilt in the same project, so it never answers the stated "connection". The question also assumes the other markets are "çevresindeki" (surrounding), which no earlier line says.
12. "Kuzey er-Rifa mahallesi". "Er" is an ordinary Turkish word (private soldier), so "Kuzey er-Rifa" briefly read as "Northern soldier ..." until the hyphen registered. The article is also written with a small letter here, "er-Rifa", against "El-Hamidiye" and "El-Kaysariye" with capitals. I could not tell whether that is a rule or an inconsistency.
