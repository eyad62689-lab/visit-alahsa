## Answers

(a) The distinction comes from hand embroidery ("哈萨刺绣") using zari thread plated with gold or silver; it is fine, dense hand work, passed down through families. The text gives no stitch or technique detail beyond that.

(b) To escape the summer heat: the caves and passages are cool in midsummer (and keep some warmth in midwinter).

(c) No. The bisht is always "绣成" / "刺绣" (embroidered). The only weaving mentioned is "棕榈叶编织" (palm-leaf weaving) in [4], which is explicitly listed as a separate craft "besides the bisht".

(d) No. All passages ([3], [4], [5], [7], [9]) name the craft consistently as hand embroidery with zari thread ("手工绣成", "哈萨刺绣", "刺绣手艺"). The only small variations are the thread description ("镀有金银" / "镀金或镀银" in [4] and [7] versus "银色或金色" in [9]) and the unclear relation between "凯撒利亚集市" and "手工艺人集市"; neither changes the craft's name.
