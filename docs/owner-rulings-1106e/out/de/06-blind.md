## Answers

(a) Jameed, from the text alone, is the juice of the lomi (lime), so it is a liquid. It is made by pressing the fruit (families do this in summer), then leaving the juice in the sun to mature in glass jars. This gives a store that lasts all year. The text never says anything about a paste or a solid, and it never says how the sun-maturing works (fermentation, concentration, ...). The word "Jameed" itself is not explained either, so the form is inferred, not stated.

(b) Yes, the lomi is "getrocknet" (dried) in passage [5] and [6]: it can be used fresh, as juice, or dried. Passage [2] also mentions "das Trocknen an der Sonne" as inherited know-how, with no object named. The thing dried is thus the lomi/lime fruit itself, though this is not clearly stated. The juice is "gereift" (matured) in the sun, not dried.
