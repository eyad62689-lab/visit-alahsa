# Blind reader notes (Turkish) - navigation

## Section headings
- "Vahayı keşfedin" (heading / laptop button): Reads naturally; "keşfedin" is a standard tourism verb. Under it I expect sights and things to do, and the items (Gezilecek yerler, Rotalar, Etkinlikler, Çarşı...) match. Fine. Slightly vague ("the oasis" before I know what El-Ahsa is), but acceptable.
- "Tadın ve satın alın": PROBLEM. "Tadın" is ambiguous in Turkish: it is the imperative "taste!", but it is identical to the noun "tadın" ("your taste/flavour"), so on first glance it reads like a possessive noun, not a verb. "Tadın ve satın alın" also sounds like a shop slogan rather than a menu heading, and "satın alın" (buy) only fits the online-stores item, not "El-Ahsa mutfağı" or "Yeme içme". Better: "Lezzetler ve alışveriş" (noun-style, covers cuisine, dining, fruit, shops) or "Yeme içme ve alışveriş".
- "Ziyarete hazırlanın": Natural and clear, "prepare for the visit". Under it Konaklama and Kayıtlı yerlerim are plausible (practical planning). However it is CONFUSABLE with the gold button "Gezinizi planlayın": both are imperatives about preparing/planning the trip, so I cannot tell how the heading differs from the button. Better to make the heading a noun phrase for practicalities, e.g. "Pratik bilgiler" or "Gezi öncesi" (a label, not a call to action), so it no longer competes with the gold CTA.
- Register inconsistency: all three headings are imperatives, while the items under them are noun phrases. Imperative headings feel like marketing copy; for a menu, noun-style headings ("Keşif", "Lezzetler ve alışveriş", "Gezi hazırlığı") are more idiomatic in Turkish sites. Not wrong, just a bit promotional.

## Gold button
- "Gezinizi planlayın": Understandable. "Gezinizi" (formal "-niz" possessive) is a little stiff and bureaucratic; Turkish sites usually say "Gezinizi planlayın" in formal brands but "Seyahatinizi planlayın" or "Gezi planı oluşturun" is more common/clearer. Also "gezi" + "-niz" could briefly parse as the verb "gezinmek" (to wander). Low severity. Keep unless the heading problem above is fixed; main issue is the confusability with "Ziyarete hazırlanın".

## Menu items
- "Gezilecek yerler": Excellent, the standard Turkish phrase (and matches the page title).
- "Rotalar": OK and understandable; "Güzergâhlar" is also used, but "Rotalar" is common in travel contexts. Fine.
- "Etkinlikler": Natural.
- "Çarşı, park ve çiftlikler": Clear in meaning, but singular "Çarşı, park" next to plural "çiftlikler" reads uneven; "Çarşılar, parklar ve çiftlikler" is smoother. Minor.
- "Harita": Natural. Inconsistent placement: on laptop it is a separate top-level link, on phone it sits under "Vahayı keşfedin". Not a wording issue but a mental-model mismatch.
- "El-Ahsa mutfağı": Natural and clear (local cuisine).
- "Yeme içme": Standard Turkish category label, natural. But it overlaps with "El-Ahsa mutfağı": I cannot tell which has dishes/culture and which has restaurants. Clearer: "Restoranlar ve kafeler".
- "Vaha meyveleri": Natural enough; tells me it is about fruits (dates etc.). Fine.
- "Çevrim içi mağazalar": Correct (TDK spelling, two words) but "Online mağazalar" is what most users say; it is also unclear whose shops (local producers?). Acceptable; "Çevrim içi mağazalar" is slightly formal. Minor.
- "Konaklama": Natural, standard.
- "Kayıtlı yerlerim": Slightly off. "Kayıtlı" suggests "registered", while "saved places" in Turkish UI is usually "Kaydettiğim yerler" or "Kaydedilenler". On laptop it is an icon only, so the accessible/tooltip text matters. Suggest "Kaydettiğim yerler".

## Bottom row
- "Dil: Türkçe Değiştirin ⌄": PROBLEM. Reads as one broken sentence ("Language: Turkish Change"): the capital "D" in "Değiştirin" mid-line looks like a typo, and the formal imperative "Değiştirin" is unusual for a language switcher. Turkish sites normally show just "Türkçe" with a globe, or "Dil: Türkçe". Better: "Dil: Türkçe" with a chevron, or "Dili değiştir" if an action label is needed.

## Other
- Page title "El-Ahsa'da gezilecek yerler": natural. Some Turks know the region as "Hasa"/"Lahsa" (Ottoman period), but "El-Ahsa" is acceptable and consistent.
- No other issues with spelling or apostrophes.
