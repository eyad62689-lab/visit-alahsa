# Urdu reader notes on the navigation (06-input-ur.txt)

## Problems (with suggested wording)

- **Section heading "اپنا دورہ ترتیب دیں" (third heading, phone): the main problem.**
  - It is confusable with the gold button "سفر کا منصوبہ بنائیں". "Arrange your visit" and "Make a travel plan" are the same kind of imperative, planning-themed phrase, so I read the heading as a second plan button.
  - It does not say what is under it. Only "رہائش" and "میرے محفوظ کردہ مقامات" are below it, and neither is "arranging".
  - "دورہ" is the wrong word for a tourist trip. In Urdu it means an official or inspection visit (وزیر کا دورہ), and also an attack or fit (دل کا دورہ). "سفر" is the natural word, and the gold button already uses it.
  - Suggestion: use a noun heading that names the contents, not an action. For example "قیام اور محفوظات" (Stay and saved), or "رہائش اور میری فہرست". Avoid planning verbs here so the gold button stays unique.
- **Bottom row "زبان: اردو تبدیل کریں ⌄": awkward.**
  - It reads like "Language: Urdu — change", so it is unclear whether "change" applies to the language or to "Urdu" itself. It looks like a pasted English pattern, "Language: English (change)".
  - Native sites use either the current language alone ("اردو ⌄" or "زبان: اردو ⌄") or the action alone ("زبان تبدیل کریں").
  - Suggestion: "زبان: اردو ⌄", or "زبان تبدیل کریں" if the action must be stated. If both are needed, put the current language in brackets: "زبان تبدیل کریں (اردو)".
- **"میرے محفوظ کردہ مقامات" (saved-places link): stiff, and partly ambiguous.**
  - "محفوظ کردہ" is official or bureaucratic register, like a file system.
  - "محفوظ مقامات" on its own can also read as "safe places".
  - Suggestion: "میرے پسندیدہ مقامات" (my favourite places) or "میری محفوظ فہرست" (my saved list). Both are shorter and clearer, and they also suit an icon link's accessible name.

## Fine, with small notes

- **"نخلستان دریافت کریں"** (heading and laptop button): natural and inviting, and a normal imperative for a menu. It is vague about contents. Nothing under it says "oasis" literally, but "Discover" fits attractions, routes, events and markets. Keep.
- **"سیاحتی مقامات"**: natural and clear.
- **"سیر کے راستے"**: natural, and it reads as routes or trails. Keep.
- **"تقریبات"**: clear. It reads as events or ceremonies and is slightly formal, but fine.
- **"بازار، پارک اور زرعی فارم"**: clear and idiomatic, but long for a menu button. "بازار، پارک اور فارم" would be shorter. This is optional.
- **"چکھیں اور خریداری کریں"**: natural and friendly. "چکھیں" suggests only tasting, but the sub-items (dishes, eateries, fruits, shops) make it clear. Keep.
- **"الاحساء کے پکوان"**: natural, and "پکوان" is the right word for dishes.
- **"کھانے پینے کی جگہیں"**: very idiomatic, the way a native would say it.
- **"نخلستان کے پھل"**: natural. It is slightly ambiguous whether it is fruit information or fruit for sale, but it sits under "taste and shop", so it is acceptable.
- **"آن لائن دکانیں"**: natural.
- **"رہائش"**: natural, a standard one-word Urdu term for accommodation.
- **"نقشہ"**: natural.
- **Gold button "سفر کا منصوبہ بنائیں"**: natural, idiomatic and clear as a call to action. It is confusable with the third heading (see above), not the other way round.

## Structure observations (not wording)

- On the laptop, "نقشہ" is a top-level link. On the phone, it sits inside "نخلستان دریافت کریں" between "سیر کے راستے" and "تقریبات". A reader switching devices will wonder why it moved.
- The first two headings are verb phrases ("دریافت کریں", "چکھیں اور خریداری کریں"). The third is also a verb phrase, so the pattern is consistent. That is exactly why it is mistaken for a button.
