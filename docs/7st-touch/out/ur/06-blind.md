# Reader observations — map chapter (/ur/)

## What the chapter asks me to do
The chapter shows the region's tourist sites on a map, each at its real location. I am asked to pick a site, by clicking its pin on the map or one of the six name buttons (Jabal al-Qarah, Qasr Ibrahim, Jawatha Mosque, Al-Asfar Lake, Ain Najm Park, Al-Uqair). A card then shows its photo and a short description. From the card I can save the site for my trip ("+یہ مقام محفوظ کریں") or go to its full page.

Laptop with a mouse: this works. The name buttons are clickable, and "چنیں" (choose) in the hint line suits a click.
Phone: this also works. The buttons are tappable and nothing in the text depends on hovering. The text never says "click" or "tap", so it is neutral about the device. It does not explain how to close a card or move between cards, but I can guess that.

## Language issues
1. minor — «کسی مقام کو کھول کر اسے دیکھیں»: in a UI this is understandable as opening a site's card. But "opening a place" is slightly odd in Urdu, and «اسے دیکھیں» is vague: see what, the place or its details? «کسی مقام کو چن کر اس کی تفصیل دیکھیں» would be clearer, and it would match the hint line.
2. minor — Inconsistent verbs: the lead says «کھول کر» while the hint line says «کوئی مقام چنیں». The same action has two names. One verb (چنیں / منتخب کریں) for both would be better.
3. minor — «وقت کے تراشے ہوئے غار»: the meaning is clear (caves carved by time), but the more idiomatic phrasing is «وقت کے ہاتھوں تراشے ہوئے غار».
4. minor — The line «جبل القارہ کا صفحہ ←» appears above the chapter label with no context. If it really sits there on the page, it reads as a stray link. It is probably the tail of the previous chapter or of a card.
5. minor — «جھیل الاصفر» mixes an Urdu noun with an Arabic definite adjective. It is acceptable as a proper name, but «الاصفر جھیل» or «بحیرۂ اصفر» would sound more Urdu. This is a matter of taste.

Everything else reads naturally. That includes the title «جو دل کو بھائے، وہ چنیں», which is warm and idiomatic, the first lead sentence, the hint line, the Qasr Ibrahim description, and the save and page buttons. Numerals are Latin, and the arrows point the correct way for RTL.

## Directed questions
(a) In context (a map chapter, followed by a list of site buttons), «کسی مقام کو کھول کر اسے دیکھیں» is most likely understood as opening the site's card on the map. Read on its own, though, «مقام کو کھولنا» can suggest a place being opened, as in opened to visitors or unlocked. It is a weak but real ambiguity, and «اسے دیکھیں» adds vagueness. Rewording to «چن کر» / «پر کلک یا ٹیپ کر کے» removes it.
(b) «کھولنا» is device-neutral: you can open something with a click or with a tap, so it fits both. «چنیں» is equally neutral and more natural for selecting an item on a map.
