# Blind read: /tr/trails/historic-hofuf/ intro

## 1. Paraphrase
The route winds through Hofuf's old town between the El-Kut quarter and the Qaysariyah Bazaar. It passes an Ottoman garrison fort, the house where Al-Ahsa's people pledged allegiance to King Abdulaziz, Al-Ahsa's first state school, and a living covered market. Stops are close together; best walked in the morning or late afternoon. Last sentence: "On Fridays the Qaysariyah Bazaar opens only in the evening." So the place is the Qaysariyah Bazaar, the day is Friday, and it opens in the evening only. A Friday visitor should not go in the morning or afternoon; come for the bazaar in the evening (and presumably do the other stops earlier, or reorder the walk).

## 2. Stumbles
- P1: "Cuma günleri El-Kaysariye Çarşısı yalnızca akşam açılır." Clear, but it is a bare fact. It never says what to do (shift the route, visit the bazaar last). I first read it as just a warning, and had to infer the advice myself. Mild.
- P2: "Duraklar birbirine yakındır; en iyisi sabah ya da öğleden sonranın geç saatlerinde yürüyerek gezmektir." Natural, but it clashes with the Friday sentence. Morning is recommended, yet the bazaar is closed on Friday mornings. Not a language flaw, a logic tension.
- P3: "biat ettiği ev" (the house where they pledged allegiance). "Biat" is understood but formal and religious-political. Fine for a Turkish reader, slightly heavy.
- P4: "hâlâ bütün canlılığıyla yaşayan üstü kapalı bir çarşı" is a bit ornate and translated-sounding ("yaşayan ... canlılığıyla" is redundant). Minor.
- P5: "El-Ahsa'nın ilk devlet okulu" and "El-Kut", "El-Kaysariye" use the Arabic "El-" form. Consistent and readable, though "Kaysariye" may suggest Kayseri to some readers. Minor.
- P6: "Rotanın durakları" ends the intro with nothing after it in this excerpt; fine.
- P7: "UNESCO listesindeki durak sayısı" is slightly vague about which list (World Heritage), though later text says it. Minor.

## 3. Score
Readability of the intro: 88/100. The Friday sentence is understandable and correct, but terse, with no explicit advice and a slight tension with the "morning" recommendation.
