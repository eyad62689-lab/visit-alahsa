# Blind read: Urdu trail intro (historic-hofuf)

## 1. Paraphrase

The trail runs through old Hofuf between the Souq al-Qaisariya market and the Al-Kut neighbourhood. Along the way: a fort that once housed an Ottoman garrison, the house where Al-Ahsa's people pledged allegiance to King Abdulaziz, Al-Ahsa's first government school, and a covered market that is still lively. All stops are close together, so the best time to walk them is morning, or after the afternoon heat has eased (late afternoon). Last sentence: on Friday, Souq al-Qaisariya opens only in the evening. So a Friday visitor should not plan the souq for the morning or afternoon; visit it in the evening (and presumably put the other stops in the morning/afternoon).

Which place: Souq al-Qaisariya. Which day: Friday. Which time: evening only. What to do: come to the souq in the evening on Fridays.

The last-stop card adds: built 1238 AH (1822 CE), over 15,000 sq m, 14 gates, about 422 shops, fire damage in 2001, rebuilt, fully reopened late 2013, part of the UNESCO-listed Al-Ahsa oasis, natural end of the trail.

## 2. Stumbles

- P1. "جمعہ کو سوق القیصریہ صرف شام کو کھلتا ہے" is clear, but it sits after the advice "morning or late afternoon", so I at first wondered whether Friday means the whole trail is unsuitable in the morning. It never says directly "so on Friday, visit the souq last / in the evening". The action for a Friday visitor is only implied. Mild.
- P2. "سہ پہر ڈھلنے کے بعد" is natural Urdu, but I read it as roughly 4-5 pm; the exact timing is vague. Minor.
- P3. "پڑاؤ" (stops/halts) is slightly camp-like, but understandable in context. The header "پڑاؤ 4" and "2" are bare numbers with no labels (what is 2? UNESCO stops, presumably). Might be read as an unexplained figure.
- P4. "شاہ عبد العزیز کے ہاتھ پر بیعت کی" is fine for a Pakistani reader. "الاحساء کا پہلا حکومتی اسکول" reads slightly translated (a "government school" would normally be "سرکاری اسکول"). "حکومتی" feels off.
- P5. "چھت دار بازار جس کی رونق آج بھی قائم ہے": fine, but "ایک چھت دار بازار" (a covered market) appears to be a different stop from Souq al-Qaisariya, though the opening sentence names the souq as the trail's start/end. First read: it might be the same souq or a second one. Ambiguous.
- P6. "سوق القیصریہ نامی بازار" is a little redundant ("souq" + "bazaar"), but acceptable.
- P7. "اس کی ازسرنو تعمیر ہوئی... پوری طرح دوبارہ کھل گیا": fine. "1238 ہجری (1822 عیسوی)": fine.
- P8. "ہفوف کے وسط میں یہ تاریخی چھت دار بازار ... بنا" and "یہی اس راستے کا فطری اختتام بھی ہے": "فطری اختتام" (natural end) is slightly literary. Also the intro says the trail runs "between the souq and Al-Kut", with the souq possibly the start, while the last card calls the souq the end. Order is unclear; I could not tell where to begin.
- P9. "ہفوف", "الکوت" and "الاحساء" are written with Arabic definite article forms. A Pakistani reader would say "احساء", but this is a stylistic choice, not an error.
- P10. "سیاحتی مقام کا صفحہ دیکھیں ←": the arrow direction. In an RTL layout "←" points forward; fine.

## 3. Readability score

84/100. Clear and natural overall. The main loss is that the Friday advice is stated as a fact rather than an instruction, plus mild ambiguity over the souq versus "the covered market" and the start/end direction.
