# Observations (native Urdu reader, Pakistan)

## Map page intro
What it tells me to do: search, or pick a category; then pick any place, either in the list or on the map, and a short introduction to that place opens. The page says there are 59 places on the map of the Al-Ahsa oasis.

Laptop and phone: «چنیں» ("choose") does not name a device. It works for a mouse click and for a finger tap. Nothing in it assumes hovering or a mouse.

## Restaurants page intro
What it tells me: it is a guide to restaurants, cafes and red-bread (date-bread) bakeries in Hofuf, Al-Mubarraz and nearby. Each place comes with a photo, and its location opens in Google Maps "in one go". It does not tell me to do anything directly. The action is only implied.

Laptop and phone: there is no action verb, only a result («کھل جاتا ہے»), so nothing is device-specific. It is fine for both. It just does not say what I should press.

## Language issues
1. minor — Map: «کوئی زمرہ چنیں۔ ... کسی بھی مقام کو چنیں تو» uses «چنیں» twice in a row. The second, conditional use («چنیں تو ... کھل جائے گا») is understandable but a bit conversational. «منتخب کریں تو» or «پر جائیں تو» would vary it. Not wrong.
2. minor — Restaurants: «کھانے کی جگہیں» / «کھانے پینے کی جگہیں» comes up about four times in a short paragraph (title, intro, «جدید کھانے کی جگہیں»). It reads repetitive. In Pakistan «ریستوران» would be natural for at least one of them.
3. minor — Restaurants: «قہوہ خانے» for cafes. To a Pakistani reader this sounds like a traditional qahwa or tea house, so «خاص کافی پیش کرنے والے قہوہ خانے» is slightly odd. «کیفے» is the everyday word for a specialty-coffee cafe. (For traditional Arabic-coffee places «قہوہ خانے» fits well.)
4. minor — Restaurants: «گوگل میپس پر اس کا مقام ایک ہی بار میں کھل جاتا ہے» is understandable, but it does not say what to press. A reader has to guess that a button or link on each card does this. «ایک ہی بار میں» ("in one go") is natural Urdu but slightly vague.
5. No serious problems. «لال روٹی (کھجور کی روٹی)»: the gloss in brackets saves an otherwise puzzling literal name. Good.

## Directed questions
(a) Map intro: «چنیں» fits both a mouse click and a finger tap. It is device-neutral. Restaurants intro: it has no action verb at all («کھل جاتا ہے» describes the result), so it fits both a click and a tap. It is neutral, but less explicit about what to press.

(b) «گوگل میپس پر اس کا مقام ایک ہی بار میں کھل جاتا ہے»: natural enough, with no grammar problem. «ایک ہی بار میں» reads as "in a single go / straight away". Most readers will understand that the place's location opens in Google Maps with one action. But it does not name the action or where it happens (for example «ایک بار دبانے سے»), so "one action" is inferred rather than stated. It is clear in meaning but a little vague.
